# m0165 — self-diagnosis

⚠️ A HYPOTHESIS from the model that produced the failure. Not a diagnosis. Test before acting.

The failure was mine, and the instructions were clear. Here is the post-mortem.

**What I was trying to express.** I wanted to make the predicates `user/1` and `evidence_to_contrary/1` available for the rule body, so that the obligation `assume_preference(U)` could fire when a user is present and no contrary evidence exists. I mistakenly placed them in the `ontology` block as ground atoms with variables, thinking that would declare their existence and allow instantiation. But that is not what `ontology` is for — it is for classification facts, and an atom with an unbound variable and no body is unsafe in ASP.

**What in the instructions led me there.** The instructions actually contained a direct warning that I failed to heed:

> ⚠️ An ontology entry with an unbound variable and NO body is neither: `restricted(M).` with nothing to bind `M` makes the solver reject the whole file. If you mean "the concept exists", declare it in `concepts` instead.

I read that warning but did not apply it to my own output. I also had the correct alternative available: the `inputs` field, which the instructions define as "predicates describing **the case being judged**, not the document. Head-less by design: facts supplied at query time." That is exactly what `user/1` and `evidence_to_contrary/1` are — facts about the case, not classifications the clause establishes. I should have put them there.

**What change would have prevented the error.** Remove the two ontology entries:

```json
{ "atom": "user(U)", "body": null, ... },
{ "atom": "evidence_to_contrary(U)", "body": null, ... }
```

and instead add `"user/1"` and `"evidence_to_contrary/1"` to the `inputs` list. That is the correct home for them, and it is what I did in the corrected version. The instructions were clear; I misapplied them.
