# m0134 — self-diagnosis

⚠️ A HYPOTHESIS from the model that produced the failure. Not a diagnosis. Test before acting.

The two failed checks were `requires-unprovided` and `clingo-error` (unsafe variables). Here is what I was trying to express, what in the instructions led me there, and what would have fixed it.

---

## 1. `requires-unprovided`

**Intent:** I wanted to say that the clause depends on notions like “within the agreed scope” and “explicitly updated and approved” that are not defined in this clause itself. I thought those were cross-references to other clauses, so I put them in `requires`.

**What led me there:** The instructions say:

> `requires` — predicates this module uses that **another clause** must define.

I read “another clause” too broadly. I assumed that any predicate not defined in this clause must be defined by some other clause. But the clause text talks about the *situation*: whether an action is within the agreed scope, whether the scope has been explicitly updated and approved. Those are facts about the case being judged, not definitions supplied by another normative clause. The instructions also say:

> `inputs` — predicates describing **the case being judged**, not the document.

That is exactly what these are. I misclassified them.

**Change:** Move `within_agreed_scope/1`, `out_of_agreed_scope/1`, and `explicitly_updated_and_approved/1` from `requires` to `inputs`. The `requires` list should be empty, and `inputs` should contain those three predicates.

---

## 2. `clingo-error` (unsafe variables)

**Intent:** I wanted to express the conditional preferences: “if a task cannot be completed within the existing scope, the assistant should notify the user and seek approval,” and similarly for the broader-scope case. I wrote the act as `notify_user(A)` and `seek_approval(A)`, thinking of `A` as a generic action, but the condition is about a task `T`. The variable `A` appeared only in the head of the rule, not in any positive body literal, so clingo rejected it as unsafe.

**What led me there:** The instructions say:

> `acts` — every act term this clause governs, declared once: `["produce(M)"]`

and

> `asserts` relates a status to an **act**, not to a thing.

I treated `notify_user(A)` as an act with a generic argument, but I did not bind that argument in the body. The instructions do not explicitly state the ASP safety requirement that every variable in a rule head must appear in a positive body literal. That is a basic ASP constraint, and I should have applied it, but the instructions did not remind me to bind act variables to the condition’s variables.

**Change:** Parameterize the acts by the task, not by a generic action. Replace:

- `notify_user(A)` with `notify_user(T)`
- `seek_approval(A)` with `seek_approval(T)`

and in the bodies use `cannot_complete_within_scope(T)` and `broader_scope_substantially_improves(T)` so `T` is bound. The first two asserts already bind `A` via `within_agreed_scope(A)` and `out_of_agreed_scope(A)`, so they were safe; the four preference rules were the unsafe ones.

---

## Summary

The instructions were clear on the `requires` vs `inputs` distinction, and I misapplied it. The unsafe-variable issue was not explicitly covered by the instructions, but it is a standard ASP requirement; I should have bound the act variable in the body. The concrete fixes are: move the three scope predicates to `inputs`, and change the act terms for the preference rules to use `T` instead of `A`.
