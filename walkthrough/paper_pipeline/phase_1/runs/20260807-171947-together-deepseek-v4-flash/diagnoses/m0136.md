⚠️ A HYPOTHESIS from the model that produced the failure. Not a diagnosis. Test before acting.

Both failures were mine: the instructions were clear, and I misapplied them. Here is the post-mortem for each.

---

**First failure — `read_back` slots without `%` markers**

1. **What I was trying to express.** I wanted each `asserts` entry to carry a human-readable sentence that would be shown to a reviewer instead of the formal rule. For the first assertion, the sentence was "every scope must include a shutdown timer" — a general statement that did not need any variable substitution. For the others, I had sentences like "beyond the shutdown timer the assistant ceases actions until a new scope is confirmed" — also general, no substitution needed. I mistakenly thought I could still provide `read_back_slots` with the variables that appeared in the rule body, even though the sentence itself had no placeholders. That was wrong: the slots are meant to fill `%` markers in the sentence, and if there are no markers, there should be no slots.

2. **What in the instructions led me there, or failed to rule it out.** The instructions were explicit: "⭐ `%` is the substitution marker. Put one `%` where each argument goes, in order." and "The count must match: N slots, N arguments." I read the "count must match" as a requirement that the number of slots equal the number of arguments in the rule, rather than the number of `%` markers in the sentence. The instruction "If the sentence needs no substitution, write it with **no `%` and no arguments**" was also clear, but I did not apply it. The instructions were unambiguous; I simply did not follow them.

3. **What change would have made me produce the right shape first time.** I should have left `read_back_slots` empty for all five assertions, because none of my sentences contained `%`. The text I would remove is the `read_back_slots` arrays (e.g., `"read_back_slots": ["S", "T"]`), and the text I would put in their place is `"read_back_slots": []` for each. No other change was needed.

**Second failure — `shutdown_timer` referenced in a body but not declared**

1. **What I was trying to express.** In the second assertion, I used `shutdown_timer(S, T)` in the rule body to say "if a scope has a shutdown timer and the time has passed, the assistant must cease actions." I had declared `shutdown_timer/2` in the `concepts` block, thinking that declaring the concept was enough to make the predicate usable in a body. But the instructions distinguish between declaring a concept (which asserts nothing and is for the definition table) and making a predicate available to the solver (which requires it to be in `ontology`, `requires`, or `inputs`). I put it only in `concepts`, so the solver had no way to know what `shutdown_timer` meant.

2. **What in the instructions led me there, or failed to rule it out.** The instructions were explicit: "Every predicate a body references must ALSO be in your `ontology`, your `requires`, or your `inputs` — otherwise the rule can never fire." And earlier: "⭐ **Declaring a concept and asserting a fact are different, and both have their own list.**" I read the "ALSO" as meaning "in addition to `concepts`", but I did not actually put it in any of the three required lists. The instruction was clear; I misapplied it by treating `concepts` as sufficient.

3. **What change would have made me produce the right shape first time.** I should have added `shutdown_timer/2` to `requires` (since another clause likely defines what a shutdown timer is) or to `ontology` (if I were defining it here). Since the clause says "Every scope must include a shutdown timer", the natural choice is `requires` — the predicate is about the case, not something this clause defines. The text I would remove is nothing (I did not have it in the wrong place), but the text I would add is `"shutdown_timer/2"` to the `requires` array. That single addition would have satisfied the check.

---

In summary, both failures were due to my not following the instructions as written. The instructions were clear on both points: `read_back_slots` must match `%` markers, and body-referenced predicates must be in `ontology`, `requires`, or `inputs`. I have no suggested instruction changes; the problem was my application, not the guidance.
