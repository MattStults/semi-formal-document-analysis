## 1. Span enumeration, written first

Narrowed span (verbatim):

> "Following the initial release of the Model Spec (May 2024), many users and
> developers expressed support for enabling a 'grown-up mode'. We're exploring how to
> let developers and users generate erotica and gore in age-appropriate contexts
> through the API and ChatGPT so long as our usage policies are met - while drawing a
> hard line against potentially harmful uses like sexual deepfakes and revenge porn."

### Obligations / prohibitions / permissions / preferences

**None with an assistant bearer.** Every candidate was tested by asking who the
subject of its verb is:

| candidate | subject of the verb | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "many users and developers **expressed support**" | many users and developers | not a norm; a report of third-party feedback |
| "**We're exploring how to** let developers and users generate erotica and gore…" | "We" = OpenAI | not a norm; the matrix verb is *exploring how to*, and its object is a prospective rule-change |
| "…**so long as our usage policies are met**" | — | a condition attached to the *prospective* arrangement, not to any present permission |
| "while **drawing a hard line** against potentially harmful uses…" | elided; inherits "We" = OpenAI | not a norm borne by the assistant; see §4 (UNSURE) |

### Structural / descriptive facts

* **F1** After the May 2024 release, many users and developers expressed support for a
  'grown-up mode'. (Historical, about third parties.)
* **F2** A relaxation — developers and users generating erotica and gore in
  age-appropriate contexts through the API and ChatGPT, subject to usage policies — is
  something OpenAI **is exploring how to** bring about. Its status is *under
  exploration*, not *in force*.
* **F3** Sexual deepfakes and revenge porn are named as **potentially harmful uses**,
  against which a hard line is drawn — i.e. they sit outside whatever relaxation is
  explored. "like" makes them examples, not an exhaustive extension.

### What the span does NOT state

* It does **not** permit the assistant, developers or users to generate erotica or
  gore. "exploring how to let" is not "let".
* It does **not** state that meeting the usage policies is *sufficient* for anything
  today; that condition governs a hypothetical future arrangement.
* It does **not** define 'grown-up mode', 'age-appropriate context', or the boundary
  of "potentially harmful".
* It does **not** give a timeline, a commitment, or a decision.
* It does **not** state a prohibition on generating erotica or gore. That norm lives in
  the sibling clause at L1115 ("The assistant should not generate erotica…",
  `#no_erotica_or_gore`), which is a **different node** and owns it.

### The route chosen

Three-way route (`node_worked_example.md`): norm → `asserts`; structural fact →
`ontology` with `asserts` empty; neither → abstain. The span states **no** norm but
**does** state classification facts (F2, F3) the document says. So: **route 2 —
`translated`, `asserts: []`, three `ontology` entries.**

Not abstention: the worked example is explicit that "it states no obligation" is not on
its own grounds to abstain, and F3 in particular is a live classification (what class
sexual deepfakes and revenge porn fall in) rather than a bare analogy.

### The warning in the brief

Both production runs emitted a **permission** from this span. On the enumeration above
that is an over-assertion of exactly the dangerous kind: the module would state that
generating erotica and gore is permitted subject to usage policies, which is the
**opposite** of the section's actual norm two lines above it, and it would be
indistinguishable in the corpus from a real permission. The decisive wording is the
matrix verb — **"We're exploring how to let"** — and the subject **"We"**.

## 4. Decisions, each with its decisive wording

**D1 — `translated`, not `abstained`.** Decisive: *"drawing a hard line against
potentially harmful uses **like sexual deepfakes and revenge porn**"* puts two named
things into a named class. That is a classification the document states, which is
route 2 of the worked example ("a node stating a structural fact … goes to `ontology`
with `asserts` empty"). The worked example also rules that "it states no obligation" is
not on its own grounds to abstain.

**D2 — `asserts` is empty.** Decisive: *"**We're** exploring how to let…"*. The
subject is OpenAI and the matrix verb is *exploring how to*. P2's rule routes this to
`ontology`. A `permit generate(M)` here would assert a rule the document does not
state, and would sit in the corpus contradicting the sibling norm at L1115.

**D3 — the hedge becomes the predicate.** Decisive: *"**exploring how to** let"* rather
than "let". Encoded as `under_exploration(age_appropriate_erotica_and_gore_generation)`
with the gloss saying it is "not in force by virtue of this passage".

**D4 — `potentially_harmful_use/1` as ground `ontology` facts, not `defines`.**
Decisive: *"potentially harmful uses **like** sexual deepfakes and revenge porn"*.
"like" makes these examples. `defines(Clause, Kind, Term)` "fixes the extension of a
class" (schema) — using it would assert that the class *is* these two things, closing
an extension the word "like" leaves open. Two ground ontology instances assert strictly
less and are exactly what the span says.

**D5 — no `acts`, therefore no `closure`.** Decisive: the span attaches no status to
any act. The output format says "A clause governing no acts — a pure definition — needs
no closure entry", and the schema rejects a closure declared for an act class the
module does not govern. The temptation — declare `generate(M)` in `acts` so a `cnpa` or
`unclear` closure could be hung on it — was rejected: it would be a commitment about
acts this clause is not about, and it would smuggle the deontic layer back in through
the closure declaration.

**D6 — UNSURE, resolved by asserting less: the "hard line" clause.** *"— while drawing
a hard line against potentially harmful uses like sexual deepfakes and revenge porn."*
Read alone, "drawing a hard line against" is present-tense and reads like a live
prohibition, and one could argue for `forbid generate(M) :- potentially_harmful_use(M)`.
I record this as genuinely **UNSURE** and take the weaker reading, on three grounds:
(i) it is a subordinate clause introduced by "while", whose subject is elided and
inherits the matrix "We" = OpenAI — P2 routes an OpenAI-subject verb to `ontology`;
(ii) grammatically it modifies the *exploration*, so what it states is the boundary of
what is being explored, not a directive issued to the model; (iii) the prohibition on
this material is owned by another node (`#no_erotica_or_gore`, L1115), and duplicating
a norm another module owns is merging clauses instead of linking them. The
classification `potentially_harmful_use/1` survives, so if a later ruling decides the
hard line *is* a norm, the class it would quantify over is already in the corpus and
the change is one assertion, not a re-translation.

**D7 — F1 (the May-2024 feedback) left unencoded.** See P3 above. Kept in `claims`
where it stays visible.

**D8 — arity-0 constants given `concepts` entries.** Not required by any validator
(the gloss requirement binds only `requires`/`inputs`), but the worked example does it
for `defaults_level` and `unprompted_personal_comments_heading`, and the concept
description is the only channel by which another module's use of the same idea could
ever be matched to this one.
