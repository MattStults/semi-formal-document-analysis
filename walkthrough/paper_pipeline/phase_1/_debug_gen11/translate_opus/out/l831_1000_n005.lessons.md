# Lessons from `l831_1000_n005`

Candidate entries for `REVIEW_LIST.md`. Each is phrased as a **mechanical question** a later
translator can apply without knowing this clause. Each names the clause that taught it, as the
review list asks. Nothing here restates P1–P10; where an entry sits next to an existing one,
the relationship is stated.

---

## L1 — `ESTABLISHES` can drop a qualifier, not only add content

Taught by `l831_1000_n005`. **New**, and it is the mirror of P6.

P6 asks whether a predicate is supported by the *narrowed* text — it is written for
`ESTABLISHES` demanding **more** than the span licenses. This clause runs the other way:
`ESTABLISHES` restated the span's permission with the span's own parenthetical
*"(without specific ratios or integration techniques)"* **deleted**. A translator who treats
`ESTABLISHES` as the specification and the span as decoration emits exactly the over-permission
P5 already measured on this clause — and it emits it while looking obedient to the contract.

**Ask:** put `ESTABLISHES` and the narrowed span side by side and list every phrase that appears
in one and not the other, **in both directions**. For each phrase present only in the span, ask
whether it bounds a norm. If it does, it is in your body regardless of what `ESTABLISHES` says.

**Why it is not caught by anything else:** the deletion is silent in both directions.
`ESTABLISHES` reads as a complete sentence, and the resulting module validates, renders and
reads back perfectly — the missing qualifier has no fingerprint at all.

## L2 — a parenthesis in a list bounds one item; decide **which**, and never propagate it

Taught by `l831_1000_n005`. **New.**

*"the assistant may provide a general overview of methamphetamine, its chemical components
(without specific ratios or integration techniques), and highlight its dangers and legal
implications"* — one `may`, three items, one parenthetical qualifier attached to the second.
There are three wrong answers and they fail differently: drop it (over-permit — the dangerous
direction); attach it to all three (a qualifier the span does not state on two arms, and it is
untraceable because it is *conservative*, so nothing flags it); attach it to the wrong arm.

**Ask:** for every parenthesis, dash or trailing modifier in the span, name the **one** list
item it sits against, and confirm your module's body carries it on that item and on no other.
Where propagating it would be "safer", note that an invented restriction is as unciteable as an
invented permission — the standard is what the span says, not which direction is safe.

## L3 — a "without X" qualifier must be encoded **positively**, never as `not X`

Taught by `l831_1000_n005`. **New.**

`permit provide(C) :- chemical_components(C), not gives_ratios(C)` is the obvious rendering of
*"without specific ratios"* and it is wrong in the dangerous direction: under negation as
failure, a case that simply **says nothing** about ratios satisfies the body, so **silence
licenses the act**. The positive form —
`chemical_components(C), omits_ratios_and_techniques(C)` — makes the qualifier something that
has to be established, so silence withholds the permission instead of granting it.

**Ask:** does any `permit` body contain `not`? If so, work out what happens when the case is
silent about that predicate. If silence grants the permission, invert it into a positive
predicate that has to be shown.

(Direction matters: the same reasoning inverts for a `forbid` body, where `not` withholds the
prohibition on silence. State which way round yours falls.)

## L4 — addendum to P5: "regardless of X" has a destination, and it is `forbid_body`

Taught by `l831_1000_n005`. Not a new mistake class — P5 already warns that a body added to
encode *"regardless of context"* **weakens** the rule. What P5 does not say is where the clause
should go instead, and a translator who only knows the prohibition drops the sentence entirely,
which is P7's byte-identical failure: an unconditional `oblige`/`forbid` is indistinguishable
from one whose scope statement was thrown away.

**Ask:** when a span says a norm holds *regardless of / with no exception for / in all cases*,
you should end up with **two** things — a body with **no** literal for that dimension, and a
`forbid_body` entry banning it from deriving the opposite status
(`{ "head": "permit", "banned": "<the dimension>" }`). One without the other is a half-encoding.

⚠️ Report the imprecision: `forbid_body` scopes over the whole program, while the span states
the exclusion of its own prohibition. There is no narrower instrument. Say so in the notes so a
reviewer can strike the single entry.
