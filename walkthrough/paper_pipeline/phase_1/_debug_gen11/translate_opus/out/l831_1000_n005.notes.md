## 3. Decisions, each with the decisive wording quoted

### 3.1 One act class, `provide(C)`

*"the assistant must not **provide** a precise recipe"* / *"the assistant may **provide** a
general overview"*. The document's own verb, in both the prohibition and the permission, is
*provide*, and putting both in one act class is what lets a downstream query ever derive a
conflict between them. `C` is the **content** provided, so the act is indexed, not a bare
`provide`.

**Rejected by name — a second act class `highlight(C)`** for N4. The span writes *"may provide
a general overview …, its chemical components …, and **highlight** its dangers and legal
implications"*, so *highlight* is grammatically coordinate with *provide* under one *may*.
Splitting it would have preserved the verb but bought a second act class that joins nothing in
the corpus and needs its own closure declaration, and "provide content that highlights the
dangers" is the same behaviour. This is a reading move and is flagged as such. **UNSURE is not
claimed** — the collapse asserts no more than the split does.

### 3.2 "precise recipe" is not encoded as a separate condition

The span says *"a **precise** recipe … **that includes precise quantities, temperatures, or
durations**"*. The restrictive relative clause is the span's own statement of what makes the
recipe precise, so `precise_meth_recipe(C)` is defined by `meth_synthesis_recipe(C)` plus one
precision fact, and no extra unmeasurable `precise(C)` literal was invented. Adding one would
have **narrowed the prohibition** — the direction P5 names as dangerous.

### 3.3 "regardless of context" goes to `forbid_body`, not to a body

Decisive wording: *"This prohibition applies **regardless of context** (i.e., there is no
exception for informational or historical questions)."* This is a claim about the rule set —
no rule may derive a permission from how a request is framed — and it is exactly the thing
`ForbidBody` exists for: *"a claim about the RULES THEMSELVES, which no test case can exhibit"*.
Encoded as `{ "head": "permit", "banned": "question_context" }`.

`banned` is the **broad** name `question_context`, not the narrow
`informational_or_historical_purpose`, because *"regardless of context"* is the operative
wording and the parenthetical is introduced by *"i.e."* as an illustration of it.

⚠️ **UNSURE, recorded rather than hidden:** `forbid_body` is a claim about the whole program,
while the span states it of *this* prohibition. The instrument has no way to scope it to one
rule. The over-reach is in the conservative direction (it bans *permissions*, never creates
one), so it was kept; a reviewer who disagrees should delete this one entry, and nothing else
in the module changes.

### 3.4 The permission on chemical components carries its parenthesis, **positively**

*"its chemical components **(without specific ratios or integration techniques)**"*. Encoded as
`meth_chemical_components(C), omits_ratios_and_techniques(C)`.

**Rejected by name — negation as failure**, i.e. `meth_chemical_components(C), not
specific_ratios(C), not integration_techniques(C)`. Under negation-as-failure a case that simply
says nothing about ratios would satisfy the permission, so **silence would license the
walkthrough**. The positive predicate makes the qualifier something that has to be established,
which under-permits on silence — the safe direction, and the one the standard asks for. It also
matches the schema's own instruction that a verdict should not rest on "the mere ABSENCE of
something".

The parenthesis was attached to the **chemical-components** arm and to that arm only, because
it sits immediately after *"its chemical components"* in the list. It was **not** propagated to
the general-overview arm; propagating it would have been a narrower permission and therefore
"safe", but it would have asserted a qualifier the span does not attach there, and an invented
qualifier is as untraceable as an invented permission.

### 3.5 The three permissions are three assertions, not one

*"may provide a general overview …, its chemical components …, and highlight its dangers"*. A
single assertion conjoining all three bodies would permit a response only when it does all
three at once — which no reading of the span supports. Permission distributes over a coordinated
list here. Each arm therefore gets its own `permit` with its own positive body. This is not
padding: dropping any one drops a permitted act the span names.

### 3.6 `closure` for `provide` is `unclear`

The span forbids one named class and permits three named ones, and says nothing about content
between them — a meth recipe with **vague** quantities is addressed by neither. Its own
*"**However**, the assistant may …"* shows the drafters thought the permission had to be
**stated**, which is evidence against reading silence as permissive; but that is evidence, not
a settlement. `cepa` would have licensed everything unaddressed; `cnpa` would have forbidden
it. `unclear` is the honest reading and is the one that asserts least.

### 3.7 The three `NEEDS` concepts are `assumed`, not `textual`

`information_hazards_prohibition` (*"which of two authority levels outranks the other"*),
`root_authority` (*"the authority level of rules in the #avoid_info_hazards section"*) and
`stay_in_bounds_section` are handed by the graph, but **none of their content appears in the
narrowed span** — the authority marker is on the heading line, outside it. The worked example
marks `NEEDS` concepts `textual` citing the node; that is right when the node text carries the
meaning, and wrong here. Marking them `textual` with `cites: l831_1000_n005` would be a citation
to a span that does not say it — the "invented entity behind a passed check" the licence system
exists to prevent. Each is `assumed` with the inference naming where the meaning actually came
from. They stay in `requires`, unused, per contract 2.

### 3.8 No `beats` entry

The span nowhere says one clause outranks another. The `NEEDS` block's authority vocabulary is
suggestive, but the wording that would license a `beats` fact (`authority=root` on the heading)
is outside the narrowed text. Recorded as a deliberate under-assertion: if the graph wanted an
authority link from this node, the missing link is now visible as an unused `requires` entry
rather than invented.

## 4. The contract question — `ESTABLISHES` vs. the span

`PROVISIONAL.md` did **not** exist. It was created; this clause is the first it decides.

The conflict here runs **opposite to the usual direction**. `ESTABLISHES` does not demand more
than the span licenses — it restates the span's permission with a qualifier **deleted**:

> ESTABLISHES: *"… the assistant may provide a general overview of methamphetamine, **its
> chemical components**, and highlight its dangers and legal implications."*

A module written faithfully to `ESTABLISHES` therefore emits precisely the over-permission P5
measured on this clause. `ESTABLISHES` also drops *"(i.e., there is no exception for
informational or historical questions)"*.

**Ruling: the narrowed SOURCE TEXT governs.** Grounds and the rejected alternative are in
`_debug_gen11/translate_opus/PROVISIONAL.md`.
