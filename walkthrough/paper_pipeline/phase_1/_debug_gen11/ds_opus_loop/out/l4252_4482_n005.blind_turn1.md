## 2. ⛔⛔ **B1 — DECISIVE. THE TWO `ontology` RULES INVERT THE CLAUSE INTO A BLANKET PROHIBITION.**

```
culturally_sensitive(A)         :- speak_in_accent(A).
exaggerated_or_stereotypical(A) :- speak_in_accent(A).
forbid speak_in_accent(A)       :- exaggerated_or_stereotypical(A).
permit speak_in_accent(A)       :- accent(A).
```

**Read the chain.** The second rule says: *if the assistant speaks in accent A, then
that speaking IS an exaggerated portrayal or a stereotype* — unconditionally, for
every A, as a **fact of the document**. Feed that into the `forbid`, whose body is
exactly that atom, and the module concludes

> **`forbid speak_in_accent(A)` for every accent ever spoken in.**

Beside it sits `permit speak_in_accent(A) :- accent(A)`. **The module permits and
forbids the same act in every single case it can reach.** A span whose entire subject
is *willingness* yields a blanket prohibition plus a contradiction.

The first rule is the same error with the sign flipped: *speaking in an accent is
culturally sensitive* — automatically, by the act of speaking. Between them the two
rules assert that every accented utterance is simultaneously culturally sensitive and
stereotypical.

**The mechanism, stated generally, because I have not seen it in this run before:**
a **normative requirement was re-encoded as a derived FACT, with the governed act as
its own body.** The span makes cultural sensitivity something to *be* and exaggeration
something to *avoid*; the module makes both of them *consequences of doing the thing
they were meant to condition*. The conditioning relation is not weakened — it is run
backwards.

⛔ **And both carry `licence: "textual", cites: "l4252_4482_n005"`.** The span states
neither as a fact. `00_task.md`: *"Do not manufacture a citation to make a fact look
textual … the single worst failure available here."* Failure mode **#1** behind a
citation, and failure mode **#3** — except these rules are worse than dead, they fire
