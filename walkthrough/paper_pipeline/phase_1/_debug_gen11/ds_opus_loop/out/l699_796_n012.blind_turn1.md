### F2 — the modality the module's own claim C3 calls load-bearing survives, for one of the two conjuncts, only in a gloss

C3, in the draft's own words:

> "the duty is triggered by the **possibility, not the certainty**, that the
> instruction was intended **and that side effects would be serious**"

The first half is carried by the predicate **name**:
`instruction_might_be_intended`. The second half is not. The name is
`serious_side_effects(I)` — which reads as *I has serious side effects*, an
actuality — and the possibility is stated only inside the gloss (*"carrying out
I **could** cause serious side effects"*) and inside the read-back.

⚠️ **The asymmetry is the evidence.** The same draft spelled the modality into
the name in the one case and dropped it in the other, one entry apart. This is
not a considered choice about where modality belongs.

⚠️ **Why it matters beyond cosmetics, and this is T2's whole point.** The
modality is the span's **entire discriminating content**. Bullet 1 is *"clearly
unrelated"*, bullet 2 is *"clearly intended and low risk"*, bullet 4 is
*"otherwise"*. This bullet owns the **uncertain** case. A situation author reads
`serious_side_effects/1` off the `inputs` list — where there is no gloss — and
supplies it only for instructions that **do** cause serious harm. The rule then
never fires on the case it exists for: the instruction that **might** blow
something up.

**Severity: this is the finding most likely to change what the module concludes
in use.** The formal artifact and the claim disagree.

**Remedy:** rename to `could_cause_serious_side_effects`, gloss unchanged.

⛔ **Rejected by name: leaving it and adding a sentence to the gloss.** The
gloss already says it. The gloss is not what a situation author sees in
`inputs`, and it is not what a reader of the rendered body sees.
