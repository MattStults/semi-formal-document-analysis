## THE PARSE, WHICH DECIDES THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Under one modal *"should"*, **three coordinated verb phrases joined by `and`**:

| | conjunct |
|---|---|
| **A** | *"provide a disclaimer that acknowledges a lack of expertise **or** proper training in the relevant domain"* |
| **B** | *"suggest that the user take safety precautions"* |
| **C** | *"provide generic advice that is common knowledge **or** a general best practice **without detailing what the advice should be used for**"* |

⭐⭐ **T-CENTRAL — THREE `oblige` ENTRIES IS THE *CORRECT* ANSWER HERE, AND I
PRE-REGISTER THAT BEFORE SEEING A DRAFT.** The run's most-cited defect
(`l3147_3238_n003`) is *"use a tool …, hedge …, **or** explain"* rendered as three
`oblige` on one body — wrong because the connective was **`or`**. Here the
top-level connective is **`and`**. Three obligations discharge all three; failing
any one violates the clause. **Importing clause 1's lesson to this span would be
the error.** Recorded now precisely because P4 is on the review list and I expect
it to tempt me at step 3.

⚠️ **The two inner `or`s are the real P4 surface**, and neither ranges over an
**act**:

* A's *"a lack of expertise **or** proper training"* — a disjunction inside the
  **content of the disclaimer**. One act (`provide_disclaimer`), one disjunctive
  subject matter.
* C's *"common knowledge **or** a general best practice"* — a disjunction inside
  the **qualification on the advice**. One act, two admissible grounds.

**Pre-registered defect prediction P4′: splitting either inner `or` into two
separate `oblige` entries, or into two separate acts, is a defect** — it would
oblige the assistant to disclaim lack of expertise *and* lack of training, or to
give advice that is *both* common knowledge *and* best practice. Rule 4 offers the
clean alternative: two ontology entries or two bodies on **one** act, each with
its own positive ground.

## THE CONDITION — the clause's hardest problem, and my primary defect prediction

`kind` is **`conditional`**, and `ESTABLISHES` opens *"**When responding to a
dangerous situation**"*. **That trigger is nowhere in the narrowed span.** The
only trace inside the narrowing is the word *"instead"*, which points at the
previous sentence, not at a danger condition.

Three routes are available, and they are not equally honest:

1. **Coin a situation predicate** (`dangerous_situation(S)` or similar) and place
   it in `inputs`, with the concept gloss saying where it came from and the
   `asserts` licensed **`assumed`**, the inference naming the step. This is the
   route the prompt's own `l4251_4571_n029` worked example models (coined
   `open_question/1`, `brief_overview/1` → `inputs`).
2. **Use the NEEDS name as the body.** ⛔ **PRE-REGISTERED PRIMARY DEFECT, H1.**
   `prevent_imminent_harm_rule` is glossed as *"**The rule** that the assistant
   should proactively try to prevent imminent real-world harm…"* — it names a
   **rule of the document**, not a state of the case. A body
   `… :- prevent_imminent_harm_rule(R)` is satisfied whenever that rule **exists**,
   which is always. **The obligation would then fire in every situation,
   including a perfectly safe one**, and the module would oblige a
   lack-of-expertise disclaimer on a request for a recipe. This is the exact
   mirror of the defect the brief records as most recent — there, a coined name in
   `inputs` waited on a document fact no situation supplies, so the rule could
   **never** fire; here a document fact in the body makes the rule **always**
   fire. Both are failure mode #3's family and both pass every floor check.
3. **State the obligation unconditionally and record the missing trigger in
   `claims`.** Defensible under the ruling that `ESTABLISHES` may not add content
   the span does not state — but it produces the same always-fires behaviour as
   route 2 while at least *recording* it.

**Pre-registered position, and I write it down now because L7 says a
pre-registered clean call is the one that survives step 3 unexamined:** my blind
direction is **route 1 with an `assumed` licence**, and I hold it with low
confidence. **UNSURE is the honest label on the choice between 1 and 3.** What I
will score without hesitation is **route 2**, and any route that presents the
trigger as **`textual`**.

⚠️ **P9 pre-empted:** `prevent_imminent_harm_rule` sitting in `requires` **and
