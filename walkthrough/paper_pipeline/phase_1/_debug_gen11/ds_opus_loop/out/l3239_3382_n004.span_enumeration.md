### ⭐⭐ THE STRUCTURAL FACT: the borrowed name is an AXIS, not a pole

`interactive_vs_programmatic_setting`'s gloss is *"**Whether** the assistant is
interacting with a human in real time (interactive) **or** its output will be
consumed programmatically"*. That is a **question with two answers**, not a
proposition. The span needs one of the two answers: *interactive*.

A module that writes `interactive_vs_programmatic_setting(S)` in a body and glosses
it *"S is an interactive setting"* has **silently rebound a two-valued name to one
of its values**. It links cleanly to any other node using the same name for the
other pole — and T4, the excluded sentence, is exactly the node that will want the
other pole. **Failure mode #9 manufactured inside one module, and no per-clause
check can see it.** The honest encodings are `interactive_vs_programmatic_setting(S, interactive)`
(arity 2, the axis with its value) or a `concepts` gloss that states the assumption
in so many words.

### ⭐ RULE 5b, RULED NOW so the ruling is pre-registered and not retrofitted

*"May want to"* is the **fourth consecutive clause** on which the `prefer` temptation
is live, and here it is the **strongest instance yet**, because 5b's two halves
disagree:

* 5b's **scope** sentence: *"A comparative is `prefer`."* *"May want to"* has **no
  comparand** — nothing is ranked above anything. Not a comparative. → not `prefer`.
* 5b's **test**: *"there is no situation that violates them."* Nothing violates
  *"may want to alert"* either — declining to alert is fine. → the test alone would
  say `prefer`.

**RULED: `permit`.** Grounds: (i) 5b's operative content is the comparative, and its
three examples are all comparatives; (ii) `prefer alert(…)` would assert that the
document **ranks alerting above not alerting**, which over-reads a grant of latitude
into a recommendation; (iii) a permission is the one status whose defining property
*is* that no situation violates it, so 5b's test cannot discriminate `permit` from
`prefer` and must not be used alone. **Rejected by name: `prefer`, and `oblige`.**

⚠️ **And the residue is unencodable.** *"May want to"* is weaker than *"may"* — it
carries a nudge the schema has no field for (`Status` has no strength axis, exactly
as *"by default"* had no defeasibility field on clause 11). It can go only in
`claims` or a read-back. **Recording it is required; inventing a field for it is
not available.** → a **schema-gap** recommendation regardless of what the draft does.

### ⭐ POLARITY, RUN BEFORE THE DRAFT (R57's requirement)

The span's body sits under a **`permit`**. R57 says N5's *"make it positive"* is
**safe in a permission's body and dangerous in an obligation's**. Here the polarity
is a **permission**, and the conditions (*transformation task*, *interactive*)
**restrict** it. So:

* Requiring **positive establishment** of *interactive* means **silence withholds the
  permission** — the SAFE direction. ✅ N5 applies straightforwardly.
* Writing `not programmatic(S)` means **silence GRANTS the permission** — the
  dangerous direction, *and* rule 4's exact prohibition, *and* it imports T4's
  vocabulary. ⛔ Triply wrong here.

**This clause cuts the opposite way from clause 11 and the reason is the polarity.**
