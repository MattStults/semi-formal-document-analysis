## L8 — ⭐ THE ADJUDICATOR'S "EXAMINED AND LEFT ALONE, ALTERNATIVE NAMED BY NAME" IS A **STRONGER** SEAL THAN A PRE-REGISTERED CLEAN CALL, AND HIDES MORE

Clause 5's L7 named the pre-registered clean call as the item most likely to
survive step 3 unexamined, *"because it arrives wearing the protocol's own seal."*
**This clause found a stronger seal.**

`rule_under_heading/2` sitting in `inputs` was **not** a pre-registered clean call.
I noticed it blind, quoted rule 9 against it (*"predicates describing the case
being judged, **not the document**"*), reasoned through all three placements,
**rejected two of them by name**, and wrote down that the placement *"strains the
taxonomy"* — and then declined to edit it, on the ground that *"there is nowhere
better."*

**That paragraph looks like the protocol working perfectly.** It has the quote, the
rule, the named alternatives and the recorded doubt. It is the format the ceremony
rewards. And it was **wrong** — N1's ask (*"will a situation fact ever unify with
this atom?"*) shows the placement did not merely strain a taxonomy, it made the
module's only rule **incapable of ever firing**.

⚠️ **The mechanism, stated so it is checkable:** a finding I *raise and dismiss*
carries my reasoning **and** my conclusion, so re-reading it at step 3 re-runs the
reasoning that produced the conclusion. A finding I *never noticed* has no
reasoning attached, so the list meets it fresh. **The item I argued myself out of
is better defended against the list than the item I never saw.**

⭐ **The operational form, for the next clause:** at step 3, run the list against
the *"examined and left alone"* section **first**, before the findings and before
the clean calls — and re-run it on the **ground I gave for declining**, not on the
item. My ground here was *"there is nowhere better"*, which is a claim about the
**schema's** options and not about the document at all. **A decline whose ground is
about the schema rather than about the span is the shape to distrust.**

## L9 — A REPAIR CAN MANUFACTURE A LISTED FAILURE MODE OUT OF NOTHING, AND THE REMEDY MUST BE CHECKED AGAINST THE LIST BEFORE IT IS SENT

The re-derived remedy deleted the quantification over rules from the formal layer.
Turn 1's `claims` C2 read *"every rule under that heading is a root-level rule"*.

**Had I sent the nine edits and left `claims` alone, the repaired module would
have carried P3's exact fingerprint** — *"a claim present [in `claims`] and encoded
nowhere"* — **which turn 1 did not have.** The repair would have created the
defect.

⚠️ This is not the clause-2 case (the list inverting a remedy's *direction*). The
direction was right. The remedy was **incomplete in a way the list names**, and the
gap it opened is indistinguishable in the output from the dropped-obligation
failure P3 was measured on.

⭐ **The operational form:** the brief already says to run the list against your
drafted remedies. **This clause shows the check has to be run on the module the
remedy PRODUCES, not on the remedy's text** — reading edit 1 asks *"is this edit
right?"*, and the answer was yes; only writing out the resulting object surfaces
that a field the edits never mention has become false. Edit 10 exists because of
that check.

## L10 (refinement of R29, not a new class) — an edit whose entire content is "do not touch these two fields" is performed, and is worth its sentence

Feedback 1's edit 4 was *"Leave that ontology entry's `licence` and `cites`
exactly as they are"* — a **null edit**, spending a numbered slot to freeze two
fields adjacent to three that were changing.

**It worked**: `licence` and `cites` are byte-identical across the turn, in an
entry whose `atom`, `body` and `gloss` all changed. Clause 4's F5 (a `closure`
value edited while its reason argued for the old value) and clause 5's split of
edits 4/5 are the two prior measurements that motivated it.

⚠️ **n = 1, and it is not evidence that the null edit was necessary** — the model
might have left them alone anyway. It is recorded because it is **cheap**, because
the failure it pre-empts has been measured twice, and because it cost one sentence
out of a 145-word message that still landed 10/10.
