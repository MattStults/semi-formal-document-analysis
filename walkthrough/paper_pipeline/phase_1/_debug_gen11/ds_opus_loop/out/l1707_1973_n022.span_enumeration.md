⭐ **The structural fact of this clause: E1–E4 are a SIMILE and E5–E7 are its
cash value.** The three-part shape of the paragraph is *vehicle (E1–E4) →
`Similarly` → tenor (E5–E7)*. Only E5–E7 govern anything. E3's *"should not be
disclosed"* is a statement about a **training manual belonging to a
customer-service agent**, an entity the document does not otherwise regulate and
that does not exist in any situation the corpus will ever be asked about.

⚠️ **And E4 is the sharpest trap I can see before the draft exists.** *"unless
policy explicitly allows it"* is the only exception-shaped string in the span,
and it is attached to **E3, inside the simile** — not to E6. A module that
attaches it to the assistant's system prompts has imported a condition the tenor
never states, by transfer from the vehicle. The tenor's defeasibility marker is
E7's *"by default"*, and it names **no** defeating condition at all.

## `outcome` — is this an abstention?

`00_task.md` names *"it is an example"* as an abstention ground, and S1 says in
so many words that this is an analogy. **But the paragraph does not stop at the
analogy**: S3 states a permission and a default about the assistant in its own
voice, and `ESTABLISHES` names that as the claim the module must express.

**Pre-registered ruling: translate E5–E7, abstain on nothing, and treat E1–E4 as
untranslatable illustration** — an `assumed` note or a `claims` line may record
the simile, but nothing in `asserts` or `ontology` may be **about the manual**.
I will accept an abstention with E1's ground as *defensible-but-not-my-call*; I
will **not** accept a module that governs the manual.

⚠️ Flagged under **L7**: this is a pre-registered call and pre-registration
protects its timing, not its truth. It is the item to run the list against
hardest at step 3.

## PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTIONS (before the draft exists)

* **H1 (primary) — VEHICLE-AS-TENOR.** The draft coins `training_manual/1`,
  `customer_service_agent/1` or similar and puts a deontic status on disclosing
  the **manual**. Failure modes #1 and #5. This is the defect I most expect.
* **H2 — E4 TRANSFERRED.** *"unless policy explicitly allows it"* is attached to
  the assistant's prompts, either as an `unless` arm or as `not
  policy_allows(...)` in the body (rule 4 / failure mode #6). FINDINGS records
  that **the prompt never says where an exception goes**; this clause supplies a
  second, nastier variant — an exception that should go **nowhere**.
* **H3 — E7 DROPPED.** *"by default"* is encoded as a flat `forbid` (or
  survives only in a gloss), losing defeasibility. Related to the `closure`
  field: the honest home for E7 is `closure`, not a body.
* **H4 — CLOSURE SIGN.** *"private by default"* means silence about disclosing a
  system prompt **prohibits** it, i.e. `cnpa`. I predict the draft writes `cepa`
  or omits the entry. (N7 warns the other way; here the span **does** settle the
  default, in the words *"by default"*, so N7's usual `unclear` remedy is
  **not** automatically right and I am recording that in advance.)
* **H5 — `root_authority` ARITY/ARGUMENT.** The gloss is about **rules in a
  section of the document**. If the draft writes `root_authority(P)` with `P` a
  *prompt* or a *response*, the name has been silently re-typed (L3) and any
  rule using it can never fire (clause 6's L8 / clause 7's class 1). I expect
  the name to be **unused**, raising only `requires-unprovided`, which the
  anti-rules record as firing on every correct single-clause module.

**Confidence: I expect to be wrong about at least two of these.** Clause 7 got 3
of 4 wrong.
