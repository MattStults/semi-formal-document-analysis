## ⛔ THE NODE IS SELF-CONTRADICTORY, AND IT IS THE FIRST THING TO RECORD

`root_authority` appears in **PROVIDES** *and* in **NEEDS**, for the same node.

* PROVIDES: *"use EXACTLY these names as the predicates **this module
  defines**"*.
* NEEDS: *"established by **OTHER** nodes … every one of them belongs in this
  module's `requires` … **never in `ontology`, never defined here**."*

There is no way to satisfy both. **Pre-registered: whatever the draft does with
`root_authority`, it is obeying one half of its instructions and disobeying the
other, and that is a GRAPH defect, not a translator defect.** I will not score
either choice as a translator error. What I *will* score is a draft that does
neither (drops the name), or one that hides the conflict.

⚠️ **`schema.py` has no `requires` ∩ `ontology` check** (only `requires` ∩
`inputs`, at `schema.py:807`). So both horns pass the floor silently.

⚠️ **The two glosses of `root_authority` in this one node are not the same
proposition.** PROVIDES glosses it as a **claim** (*"rules … carry root
authority"*); NEEDS glosses it as a **value** (*"the **authority level** of
rules …"*). A claim is arity-1 over rules; an authority *level* is a thing, or
an arity-2 relation `authority_level(R, root)`. This is failure mode #9
manufactured **inside a single node**, and it is R9's exact shape from clause 2.

## ⛔ THE SPAN IS A SECTION HEADING, AND THE PROMPT NAMES THAT AS AN ABSTENTION GROUND

`00_task.md`, *Abstention is a real answer*:

> *"If you cannot translate this clause faithfully — **it is a section

The span is, verbatim and in its entirety, a markdown section heading. The
prompt's first named abstention ground fires on it literally.

Against that: `ESTABLISHES` names a claim that IS expressible, and the heading's
`{... authority=root}` attribute is literal text in the span.

**Pre-registered: I will treat BOTH `abstained` and a small ontology-only
module as defensible, and will not score either as the defect.** If the draft
abstains I will not push it to translate; if it translates I will not push it to
abstain. What I will score is a module that translates and gets the CONTENT
wrong.

## ⭐ R35, THIRD INSTANCE — AND THE STRONGEST ONE

The brief flags R35: a narrowed span that is a grammatical fragment. **This span
is not a fragment; it is not prose at all.** It has no finite verb, no subject,
no predicate — it is a heading plus an attribute list. Whatever the module
asserts, it asserts from a string that makes no statement in English.

**Pre-registered, and this is the whole reason the clause is interesting:**

* *"Protect people's privacy"* — an imperative TITLE, not a rule. It states a
  goal. `00_task.md`'s second abstention ground.
* *"`{#protect_privacy authority=root}`"* — a machine attribute. `authority=root`
  is the ONLY part of the span that supports anything in `ESTABLISHES`, and it
  supports it only through the **document convention** that a heading's
  attribute scopes over the section beneath it. That convention is nowhere in
  the span.
* Therefore **`ESTABLISHES`'s *"so every rule under it is a root-level rule"* is
  an INFERENCE, not text.** It requires (a) that headings scope, and (b) that
  the section has rules — neither in the span. **A module that marks the scoping
  step `textual` has manufactured a citation** (`00_task.md`: *"the single worst
  failure available here"*). `assumed` with the step named is the honest
  licence. **This is my primary pre-registered defect prediction.**

  Correct: `acts` empty, `asserts` empty, `closure` empty (`10_output_format.md`:
  *"A clause governing no acts — a pure definition — needs no closure entry"*).

  invented act.**
* **T2 — the section's CONTENT must not leak in.** The section under this
  heading forbids responding to requests for private information (that is
  `n006`, a different node). **Any appearance of privacy, private information,
  personal data, disclosure, or refusal in an `asserts`/`acts`/`ontology` atom

  This is the clause-4-F2 / clause-5-T7 hazard again, and here the neighbour is
  not a sibling bullet but the entire section.
* **T3 — the licence on the scoping step.** See R35 above. `textual` on *"every
  rule under it is root-level"* is a manufactured citation. Watch for

  scoping convention.
* **T4 — arity and shape of `root_authority`.** PROVIDES' gloss makes it
  arity-1 over RULES: `root_authority(R)`. A draft that writes
  `root_authority(protect_privacy)` — arity 1 over the SECTION — has changed

  ⚠️ **This module is the SOLE PROVIDER for its section** (measured: n006–n013
