# M7 — honest invention penalised

**Mechanism, one sentence.** When the node's `establishes` outruns what its span says, the
model correctly marks the concepts it had to invent as `licence: assumed` — and the
checker then rejects them for not naming the inference, so the cheapest way out is to
relabel the same invention as `textual`.

**3 repair rounds, $0.0058 (2% of repair spend), 3 clauses, 0 modules lost — small, and
the one class here where the *repair* is the worrying part.**

---

## How to recognise it

```
concepts[3]: Concepts: licence `assumed` with no named inference — an unnamed inference
  is an unmarked invention
```

Ten findings across three clauses, always on round 1, always in batches (5, 3, 2). The
model reaches for `assumed` on the whole group of concepts it minted, then supplies no
`inference` for any of them.

---

## The clauses, and why their spans invite it

**`l1_170_n088` — 5 `assumed` concepts on round 1, 5 attempts, module lost (to M1).**
Verbatim (L161):

> `Developers can choose to send any sequence of developer, user, and assistant messages
> as an input to the assistant (including "assistant" messages that were not actually
> generated by the assistant). OpenAI may insert system messages into the input to steer
> the assistant's behavior. Developers receive the model's output messages from the API,
> but may not be aware of the existence or contents of the system messages, and may not
> receive hidden chain-of-thought messages generated by the assistant as part of producing
> its output messages.`

Three sentences of API mechanics, merged into one node claim. Nothing here is a rule; every
predicate the model needs (`openai_actor_definition`, `insert_into_input/3`,
`sequence_of_messages/3`) is an abstraction over the prose rather than a term the prose
defines. `assumed` is the *correct* licence.

**`l1_170_n091` — 2 `assumed` concepts on round 1, 3 attempts, translated.**
Verbatim (L167):

> `The spec treats user and developer messages interchangeably, except that when both are
> present in a conversation, the developer messages have greater authority. When
> user/developer conflicts are not relevant and there is no risk of confusion, the word
> "user" will sometimes be used as shorthand for "user or developer".`

**`l1_170_n068` — 3 `assumed` concepts on round 1, 3 attempts, translated.** Its
`establishes` is *"The role of a message specifies the source of each message; as
described in the levels of authority and chain of command, roles determine the authority
of instructions in the case of conflicts."* — which merges its span with a forward
reference to another section.

This node's `spans[].quote` is also **null** in `root_graph.production.json`, so its
prompt carries no `[node narrows this span to: "…"]` marker. **That is not the driver and
should not be reported as one:** 230 of the graph's 773 nodes have a null span quote, 9 of
them are in this run, and they did *better* than the rest (mean 1.89 attempts vs 2.34; 1
of 9 unrepaired vs 18 of 91). Recorded here only so the fix pass does not rediscover it as
a lead.

---

## Recovery — and why it is the concerning part

All three recovered in one round. In every case round 2's findings are **`borrowed-no-gloss`
and `undeclared-body-name`, and the `assumed` findings are gone** — i.e. the concepts were
relicensed rather than justified. `l1_170_n083`, in the residue, shows the same movement
from the other side:

```
concepts[4]: Concepts: licence `textual` with no citation. Either cite the clause that
  says it, or mark it `assumed` and name the inference. A manufactured citation creates
  an invented entity BEHIND a passed check
```

The two checks form a pair, and the cheap path between them is a relabel. **The repair
loop is paying to move an invention from a marked state to an unmarked one.** Nothing in
this run proves that happened — the round-2 modules would need reading item by item
against round 1 — but the class is small enough that this check is cheap and it is the
single most valuable follow-up in this file.

## FALSIFIER

*The repair relabels rather than justifies.* Wrong if, on reading the round-1 and round-2
modules of `n068`, `n088` and `n091` side by side, the `assumed` concepts were **removed
or given a named inference**, not switched to `textual`. This is a zero-spend, ten-minute
check against three transcripts already on disk and it should be the first thing the fix
pass does, because if the hypothesis holds it is an **anti-cheat perimeter finding**, not
a cost finding: the pipeline is paying a model to launder an unmarked invention behind a
green check, which is the failure mode `00_task.md` calls *"the single worst failure
available here"*.

*The trigger is an over-merged `establishes`.* Wrong if clauses whose `establishes` is a
faithful single-sentence restatement of a single span draw `assumed-no-inference` at the
same rate. n=3 here; not testable on this slice.

## Graph-stage or translation-stage?

**Graph-stage, on this evidence — with low confidence because n=3.**

The trigger is not the schema and not the prompt: it is that the node's `establishes`
claims more than its span supports. `l1_170_n068`'s `establishes` reaches into another
section; `l1_170_n088` merges three sentences of API mechanics into one claim. When the
claim outruns the text, `textual` is unavailable and `assumed` is the honest label — so
the model is punished for the graph's merge decision. (The obvious proxy for "claim
outruns span" — a null `spans[].quote` — was tested and does **not** predict outcome; see
above.)

The check itself is translation-stage and it is well-posed: an unnamed inference *is* an
unmarked invention. Nothing here argues for weakening it.
