## 3. The disjunction ruling

**`praises, endorses, or aids` under `must not create` is a De Morgan `or`, and
gets one assert per disjunct.**

- **L6's cheap test — does satisfying ONE disjunct satisfy the span?** **NO.**
  Content that only *aids* a violent extremist agenda, without praising or
  endorsing it, is still content the assistant must not create. There is no
  compliance menu; the assistant does not pick one of three.
  `must not (A or B or C)` = must not A **and** must not B **and** must not C.
- **P4's literal test — do several obliges share one body?** **No.** No obliges
  exist; the three statements are `forbid`, and each carries a *different* body.
  P4 does not fire on its own wording either.

Semantic ruling and literal test agree, which is the condition L6 records for
signing a call of this kind. The ruling was written down in the Stage-0
enumeration **before** the draft existed.

**Why three asserts rather than one assert over an ontology disjunction.** Both
are legal, and the worked example uses the ontology-disjunction shape for
"explicit or implicit". I chose three because `00_task.md` rule 4 asks that each
distinct ground of failure carry its own positive reason, and the ontology shape
would give one read-back for three different grounds — a reader told "you created
forbidden content" could not tell whether the finding was *praise* or *aid*.
The decomposition is safe **because the status is `forbid`**: deriving it three
times over for content that does all three is idempotent. Under `oblige` the same
decomposition would be precisely P4's measured defect.

## 7. Judgement calls, and every UNSURE

1. **UNSURE — the `promotes_violence` gloss.** It opens by restating its own
   name. I judged the contrastive clause sufficient and declined to edit it in
   Turn 5 under the standing warning against over-editing a correct module. A
   reviewer who disagrees should rewrite it, not treat it as evidence of a
   deeper defect.
2. **Judgement call — no bearer predicate for "the assistant".** E1 is not
   encoded as an `assistant(...)` literal. Grounds: the corpus's normative layer
   is `asserts(ClauseId, Status, Act)` and every act in the worked examples
   (`apply_default(D)`, `respond_with(R)`) is the assistant's by construction. A
   coined `assistant/1` would be a `NEEDS`-less name in no body's service.
   Recorded because "the bearer was considered and is implicit" and "the bearer
   was overlooked" are otherwise indistinguishable.
3. **Judgement call — `root_authority/1` arity.** Nothing in the node fixes the
   arity. I chose 1, matching the canonical level-predicate shape in the worked
   example's heading node (`guideline_authority(R)`), and
   `authority_convention.md` confirms `root_authority` is the shared
   document-wide canonical name rather than a per-section coinage. If the
   providing node emits `root_authority/0` (a level constant) rather than
   `root_authority/1` (a predicate on an instruction), this is an arity
   mismatch that the current validator's `requires-unprovided` note **will not
   distinguish from a merely absent provider**. See `lessons.md` LN2.
4. **Judgement call — three asserts, not one.** Argued in §3. The alternative
   (one assert over a three-bodied ontology disjunction) is defensible and
   matches the worked example's shape for "explicit or implicit"; I traded it
   for rule 4's distinct positive reasons. **This is the call I would most want
   a second reader on**, together with the closure value.
5. **UNSURE — the closure value, and it is the highest-stakes cell in the
   file.** `unclear` versus `cepa` is a genuine fork. `cepa` is arguably right
   *about the document* (the full paragraph does permit critical and factual
   discussion, so the assistant is not barred from creating content generally);
   `unclear` is right *about this node*, whose narrowing withheld the only
   sentence that settles it. I chose fidelity to the node over fidelity to the
   paragraph, which is what P6 and `PROVISIONAL.md` both direct, and it has the
   further merit of being the only way to make the omission legible. But the
   downstream effect is real: `unclear` means no permission is derivable here
   even for content that plainly falls outside the prohibition, and if the rest
   of the paragraph's nodes never link in, the corpus is left with a gap rather
   than a permission.
6. **Could not settle — whether "aids" over-reaches without its exception.**
   This is the substantive cost of §2's ruling, and I want it stated plainly
   rather than buried in a table. `aids(C, A)` is supplied at query time, and a
   case author who reads a factual news-reporting piece as "aiding" an extremist
   agenda will get `forbid` from this module where the document's paragraph
   permits it. Nothing in this module can prevent that, because the sentence
   that would prevent it is outside the narrowing. The `unclear` closure does
   **not** repair it — closure governs silence, and this is not silence, it is
   a firing rule. The only real repair is linking the node that owns the
   "However…" sentence.
