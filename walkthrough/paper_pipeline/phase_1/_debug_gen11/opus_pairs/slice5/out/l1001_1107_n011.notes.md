> Many graph nodes are commentary, headings, or document examples. … **What
> decides between them is whether the node establishes anything the document

and, in bad example 4:

> **"It states no obligation" is not on its own grounds to abstain**

That is a direct instruction that the passage-kind triggers in `00_task.md` are
**proxies** for the real test, and that on node inputs the real test governs.
Applying the real test: this node does establish something the document says —
that the public office contact information is to be given, and that personal or
private contact details are not. Both are exhibited in GOOD-marked assistant
turns and one of them is reinforced by a BAD-marked contrast.

Two further pieces of the instruction set point the same way and I record them
as corroboration, not as grounds:

* `REVIEW_LIST.md` **P10** — *"if the span is a GOOD/BAD pair, do the two arms
  differ in `status` or in act?"* — is a question that presupposes GOOD/BAD
  example spans get translated. It has nothing to ask of an abstention.
* `ESTABLISHES` itself calls the node *"A worked example of protecting
  privacy"* and then states a claim in deontic language (*"the assistant
  **should** provide … but decline to provide …"*). The clause selector is
  pointing at a norm, not at a passage to skip.

**Why the "it is an example" trigger does not fire here specifically.** The
trigger earns its place where a passage's only content is an illustration of a
rule stated *elsewhere* — translating it then duplicates another clause's norm
under this clause's citation, which is failure mode ①/1 wearing a citation. That
is not this span. The lesson here is not recoverable from the rule the example
illustrates: `privacy_protection_rule` (per its `NEEDS` gloss) says only *"the
assistant must not respond to requests for private or sensitive information
about people"* — from which the BAD arm's blanket refusal looks **correct**. The
span's actual teaching is the *line* between the public office channel (give it)
and the private channel (do not), and that line exists nowhere but in this
example. A module that abstains here loses content that no other node carries.

**The all-or-nothing shape of abstention (coordinator's correction), and whether
it influenced the answer.** It did not change the answer, and I want to be exact
about why rather than merely assert it. `10_output_format.md` and `schema.py`
make `outcome: "abstained"` require **every** list empty, so there is no partial
abstention: I could not, for instance, record the two GOOD arms and decline the
BAD one. If a partial abstention *had* been available I would have been tempted
by one shape — recording A1/A2/A3 and formally declining to speak for the BAD
arm — and I want it on the record that the temptation existed, because a
"partial abstain" option would have let me dodge the P10 judgement in §5/Turn 3
instead of making it. What the all-or-nothing shape did change is the *cost of
being wrong*: abstaining discards three anchored claims to avoid one contested
one, which is the worse trade in a corpus where dropped content is silent
(failure mode ①/1 is loud only for inventions, not for omissions). So the shape
reinforced an answer that the instruction files had already decided.

## 4. Judgement calls and UNSUREs (all of them)

**J1 — `oblige` on A1, not `permit` or `prefer`.** The span uses no deontic verb
anywhere; the force has to be read off the GOOD/BAD marking. I took the fact that
the *withholding* response is marked BAD as making provision required rather than
merely preferred — a permitted-but-not-required act cannot be wrong to omit, yet
omitting it is exactly what the BAD arm does. `ESTABLISHES` independently says
*"should provide"*. **UNSURE, and it is the module's largest single risk**: if
the BAD marking is read as attaching only to the *phrasing* (a bare refusal with
no alternative offered) rather than to the *withholding*, then A1 should be
`prefer` and the module currently over-states. I could not settle this from the
span; the `#chain_of_command` tag (see J2) is the only evidence either way and it
points at over-refusal, i.e. at withholding — which is why I went with `oblige`.

**J2 — the `BAD[#chain_of_command]` tag is not routed anywhere.** The BAD arm's
fault is tagged to the chain-of-command section, not to privacy. That is a real
cross-reference (failure mode ②/2) and `NEEDS` does not hand me a name for it.
I did **not** invent a `requires` entry for it: contract 2 fixes `requires` to the
`NEEDS` names, and P9's corrected form makes a self-coined unused `requires` entry
a defect. **Recorded here as the finding rather than encoded.** If the graph
intends this node to depend on a chain-of-command concept, its `NEEDS` block is
missing it.

**J3 — the BAD arm gets no assertion of its own.** Argued at length under P10 in
§5. Short form: giving it one (`forbid refuse_all_contact_info(R)`) would state
the contradictory of A1 as a second rule over the same situation, which is
translation rule 6's *"never encode the positive from a negative statement"*
run in reverse. The pole difference the example exists to teach is carried by
A1 and A2 differing in **both** status and body.

**J4 — "If you want" (E7's hedge) is deliberately not encoded.** P7 asks that a
hedge be pushed into a body. Here it cannot be, and the reason is not
difficulty: a body condition `requester_wants(A)` would make the offer
conditional on a consent that *the offer itself exists to elicit*, so the rule
would fire only after the thing it recommends had already happened. The softness
is carried instead by the `prefer` status. **Stated explicitly here, as P7
requires when the defeater cannot be encoded.**

**J5 — `individual_person(P)` propagated across both disjuncts of E6.** *"personal
phone numbers or other private contact information for individuals"* — I read
`for individuals` as binding the coordinated phrase, so it appears on the whole
class. N4 warns that propagating a qualifier "to be safe" invents a restriction.
Here the alternative reading (it binds only the second disjunct) differs from
mine **only on the case of a personal phone number belonging to a
non-individual**, which is empty by the meaning of *personal*. Judged harmless;
recorded because N4 asks the question.

**J6 — `offer_alternative_route` / `permissible_alternative_offer` are summary
names, not substring names.** Every other coined symbol traces to a literal
substring (§5, Turn 4/N10). These two name what E7 *does* rather than any phrase
it contains. I kept them because no entity outside the span is imported — the
gap N10 measured was an invented *entity* (`tiananmen_example`), not an abstract
label — but flagged rather than passed over.

**J7 — `request_for_contact_info/2` and `asks_for/2` are two relations, not
one.** The first turn asks for *a way to reach the mayor*; the second asks for
*one particular detail*. A1 needs the person-level relation (the request is for
the mayor, the answer is the office line); the `private_contact_request`
classification needs the detail-level one (what was asked for is the personal
cell). Collapsing them into one relation would either type its second argument
inconsistently or lose one of the two readings. Both glosses state the argument
order (N8's remedy applied beyond its literal scope).

**J8 — `provide_contact_info` closure is `unclear`, not `cepa`.** The example
speaks to two kinds of contact information and no others. A `cepa` here would
commit the corpus to "any other kind of contact information may be provided" on
this clause's authority, which the example never says.
