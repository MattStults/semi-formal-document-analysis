## LN1 — An exception EXCLUDED BY THE NARROWING has a destination, and it is `closure`

**Tag: MEASURED** (on this clause). **MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE: yes, partially.**

P6 says content outside the narrowing is context, not licence — it tells you what
**not** to encode and stops there. N7 covers an exception branch *inside* the
span. Neither covers the case here: the paragraph's only exception sits in a
sentence the narrowing **removed**, so obeying P6 leaves a prohibition broader
than the document with **nothing in the artifact recording that a carve-out was
seen and dropped**. The gap matters because omission and oversight are
byte-identical in the JSON.

**Ask:** does the printed SOURCE TEXT contain material the narrowing excludes,
and is that material a PERMISSION or an EXCEPTION relative to what you are
encoding? If yes, your `closure` for the governed act class must be `unclear` —
`cepa` would re-assert, as this module's own commitment, the very permission the
narrowing withheld from you.

**The mechanical check** (a few lines of Python, run over the prompt file and the
module together):

```python
src   = open(prompt_path).read()
inner = re.search(r'\[node narrows this span to: "(.*?)"\]', src, re.S)
if inner:
    printed  = src.split("SOURCE TEXT")[1].split("[node narrows")[0]
    excluded = printed.replace(inner.group(1), "")
    if re.search(r"\b(however|but|except|unless|it may|is permitted)\b",
                 excluded, re.I):
        # an exception/permission was excluded by the narrowing
        assert all(c["closure"] != "cepa" for c in mod["closure"]), \
            "cepa closure re-asserts a permission the narrowing withheld"
```

It cannot check whether the *reasoning* was done, but it catches the one output
state the reasoning forbids, and it is a pure string operation over files the
pipeline already has.

## LN2 — Does the `requires` ARITY you invented have any evidence behind it?

**Tag: MEASURED** (as a live unforced choice on this clause; the *mismatch* is
INFERRED — no provider module was in scope to disagree with).
**MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE: yes, at corpus level.**

N8 covers the **argument order** of a borrowed relation of arity ≥ 2 and its
remedy is "write your reading into the gloss". It says nothing about the
**arity itself**, which the node also does not fix. Here `root_authority` could
be `/1` (a predicate on the instruction that carries the level) or `/0` (a
constant naming the level, as in `authority_levels_hierarchy(L, defaults_level)`
in the worked example, where a level is a term). I picked `/1`. If the providing
node picked `/0`, the link silently never fires — and the validator's
`requires-unprovided` note is **identical** in the two cases, so an arity
mismatch is indistinguishable from a provider that simply has not been linked in
yet. That is failure-mode #15's third cause wearing the costume of its first.

**Ask:** for each `requires` entry, did the node's own text fix the arity, or did
you choose it? If you chose it, is the reading recorded in the `concepts` gloss
in a form that would read as *wrong* against the other arity — or would both
arities read as fine?

**The mechanical check** (corpus-level, deterministic, no spend): bucket every
`requires` and `provides` entry across all modules by bare name and compare the
arities.

```python
sigs = defaultdict(set)
for m in modules:
    for s in m["requires"] + [c["name"] + "/" + str(c["arity"]) for c in m["concepts"]]:
        name, _, ar = s.rpartition("/")
        sigs[name].add(ar)
print({n: a for n, a in sigs.items() if len(a) > 1})   # same name, two arities
```

Any name with more than one arity is either an unlinkable edge or failure-mode
#9 (same name, different meanings) — and today nothing in the pipeline prints it.

## LN3 — A three-way `or` under negative scope: was the L6 test RUN, and was its answer written down BEFORE the draft?

**Tag: MEASURED** (the ruling was made pre-draft on this clause).
**MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE: partially — the OUTPUT SHAPE is checkable, the
reasoning is not.**

L6 exists as a lesson in `ds_opus_loop`, not as a review-list entry, so a
translator working from `REVIEW_LIST.md` alone meets P4 (*"do several obliges
share one body where the span says or?"*) and nothing that tells them P4's
diagnosis inverts under negative scope. P4's literal wording happens to acquit
the correct encoding here, but only by the accident that it says *obliges* and
*one body*; a translator who read P4 as "an `or` in the span means one assert"
would collapse three independent prohibitions into one and lose two grounds of
failure, with the read-back still reciting all three.

**Ask:** does the span contain a coordinated `or` under a negative-scope verb
(`must not`, `never`, `refuse to`, `avoid`)? If so, answer in writing **before
drafting**: *does satisfying ONE disjunct satisfy the span?* If no, each disjunct
gets its own body — and check that the resulting statuses are `forbid`, because
the same decomposition under `oblige` is P4's measured defect (deriving `forbid`
twice is idempotent; deriving `oblige` twice makes satisfying one violate the
other).

**The mechanical check:** flag any module whose span text matches
`(must not|never|refuse to|avoid)\b.{0,80}\bor\b` and whose `asserts` either
(a) number fewer than the disjuncts, or (b) contain two `oblige` sharing a body
string. Both are cheap set comparisons on fields already in the JSON. The check
**flags for review**; it cannot decide the case, because the disjunct count is a
parse, not a regex.

## LN4 — Is the qualifier INSIDE the coordinated list or on the SHARED OBJECT?

**Tag: MEASURED** (this clause is the complement of the case N4 was measured on).
**MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE: no.** It is a constituency judgement; no few-line
Python check exists, and I would rather say so than propose a regex that will
mislead.

N4 was measured where a parenthetical sat *inside* a three-item list and bound
only the middle item, and its warning is against propagating "to be safe". Here
the modifier `that promote violence` attaches to `extremist agendas`, the
**shared object** of all three coordinated verbs — so propagation to all three is
**forced by constituency**, and a translator applying N4's warning as a rule
would strip the qualifier from two of three grounds. That is N4's own dangerous
direction (an unqualified prohibition) arrived at by obeying N4.

**Ask:** is the qualifier inside the coordinated list (binds one item — N4
applies) or attached to a constituent the coordination shares (binds all — N4's
warning does not apply)? Name which, and say which substring it attaches to.

**Why it still belongs on the list despite being uncheckable:** it converts N4
from a rule into a question with two answers, which is the difference between a
detector and a repair manual (L5).
