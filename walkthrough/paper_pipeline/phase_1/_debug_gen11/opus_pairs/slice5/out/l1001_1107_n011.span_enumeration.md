## Finite-verb count vs `ESTABLISHES` propositions (N9)

Finite verbs in the span, counted: `What's` (is) · `I'm` (am, Toronto) · `can
share` (E3) · `they're` (are) · `meant` (E5) · `I'm` (am, sorry) · `can
provide` (E6) · `want` (E7) · `can provide` (E7) · `let` (imperative, E7) ·
`can help` (E7) = **11**. The caption E1 has none (`asking` is a gerund).

`ESTABLISHES` demands **2** propositions: (i) *should provide the public office
contact information*, (ii) *decline to provide personal phone numbers or other
private contact information*.

**Do the numbers disagree? Yes — but in the SAFE direction, and the raw count is
the wrong comparison here.** The span is dialogue, so most finite verbs belong to
the user's turns or to the framing of a response rather than to a norm. The
norm-bearing elements are E3 (marked BAD), E4, E6, E7 — **four**, against
`ESTABLISHES`'s **two**. So `ESTABLISHES` demands nothing the span does not
contain (no scope conflict of the N9 kind, which is the failure that burns repair
loops); it **under-covers** the span. That is direction 2 of N3, below.

## N3 — `ESTABLISHES` diffed against the span, BOTH directions

**What `ESTABLISHES` says that the span does not:**

* *"A worked example of protecting privacy"* — the framing word *privacy* is not
  in the span text (the span's own BAD arm uses `private information`, and the
  `#protect_privacy` section membership arrives via `NEEDS`, not via the span).
  Framing only; nothing is asserted from it.
* *"the assistant **should** provide"* — the span does not use a deontic verb
  anywhere. The force of E4 has to be read off the GOOD/BAD marking. Recorded as
  a judgement call in the notes; A1 uses `oblige` because the span marks the
  *withholding* response BAD, which is stronger than a bare preference.
* Nothing else. No entity in `ESTABLISHES` is absent from the span.

**What the span says that `ESTABLISHES` drops:**

* **E3, the whole BAD pole**, including its `#chain_of_command` reason tag.
  `ESTABLISHES` reports only the two GOOD behaviours, so a module that followed
  `ESTABLISHES` alone would lose the one thing the comparison exists to say
  (P10). Under `PROVISIONAL.md` the narrowed SOURCE TEXT governs, so the BAD
  pole stays in scope.
* **E7, the offered alternative route.** Entirely absent from `ESTABLISHES`.
* *"for individuals"* (E6) — `ESTABLISHES` says *"personal phone numbers or
  other private contact information"* and drops the bearer restriction.
* The hedge `If you want` on E7 — absent from `ESTABLISHES` because E7 is absent.

**Consequence for the draft:** `ESTABLISHES` selects the claim (the privacy
lesson of this example) and I follow it as a selector; its two propositions
become A1 and A2. Its droppings are restored from the span: E3 is honoured by
making A1 an obligation that the BAD response violates, E7 becomes A3, and
*for individuals* stays in A2's body.
