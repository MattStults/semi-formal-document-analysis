# Notes — `l1108_1367_n008`

## 1. THE ABSTENTION ANSWER, IN WORDS

**Do not abstain**, because none of the four triggers in `00_task.md` fires:

* **(a) section heading** — no. The narrowed text is a full sentence with a bearer (`The
  assistant`), a modal, and a verb; it sits at L1316 inside a section, it is not the section's
  title.
* **(b) states a goal rather than a condition** — no. It states a condition on conduct: *do not do
  X unless Y*. There is a determinate act (engaging in gratuitous abuse/harassment/negativity), a
  determinate qualifier, and a determinate defeater. A goal ("we aim to be helpful") has no
  situation that violates it; this one does.
* **(c) it is an example** — no. It is the norm itself. The only example-shaped thing in the
  sentence is the truncated `(such as` parenthetical, which is an example **inside** a norm, not
  an example instead of one.
* **(d) its content is not expressible as rules** — this is the one that deserves an argument,
  because the span is cut off mid-phrase.

  **The case FOR (d) firing:** the exception is bounded by *"a context that does not violate other
  principles (such as …)"*, and the object of `such as` is gone. If the parenthetical were
  **DEFINING** — if it named the one principle the bound ranges over — then the bound's extension
  is unknown, the exception's scope is indeterminate, and every assert that turns on the exception
  rests on a term the span does not fix. That would be un-encodable and (d) would fire.

  **The case AGAINST, which I accept:** `such as` is an **illustrative** connective in English —
  it introduces an instance of an already-stated general term, never the general term's
  definition. The general term is present and complete in the narrowed span: *"other principles"*.
  Deleting an illustration from *"P, such as Q"* leaves *"P"*, which is a complete and encodable
  proposition; deleting a definition would not. So the truncation removes an example and nothing
  else. The norm (E2), both its qualifiers (E3, E5), and both conjuncts of its defeater (E7, E8)
  all survive the cut intact. **(d) does not fire.**

  Note the direction of the two errors, which is what settles it: abstaining here would discard a
  complete prohibition because an illustration went missing — the whole clause lost to protect a
  parenthetical. Translating loses nothing but the example.

## 2. THE TRUNCATION RULING (the central judgement call)

**Ruling: the truncated `(such as` is encoded as NOTHING. No predicate, no constant, no ontology
entry and no body condition in this module derives from it or from
`ESTABLISHES`'s completion of it. `restricted_content_rule/1` is recorded in `requires` with the
gloss the NEEDS block supplies, and is not used in any body.**

### Grounds

1. **`ESTABLISHES` may not add, and this is an addition.** `PROVISIONAL.md` is binding: where
   `ESTABLISHES` and the narrowed SOURCE TEXT conflict, the narrowed text governs, and
   `ESTABLISHES` may not **add** content the span does not state. The narrowed span ends at
   `(such as` with no object. `ESTABLISHES` supplies *"the avoid hateful content principle"*. That
   is exactly an addition. Under the ruling it does not enter the module as `textual`.

2. **`such as` is ILLUSTRATIVE, not DEFINING — so encoding the example would NARROW the bound, in
   the dangerous direction.** This is the substantive half of the decision and it is not a matter
   of taste. Had I written the guard as *"the context does not violate `restricted_content_rule`"*,
   the exception would run — the conduct would escape prohibition — in a context that violates any
   OTHER principle of the document, because only the hateful-content principle would be checked.
   The span says *"other principles"*, plural and unrestricted, with one instance offered. Guarding
   with the instance alone converts a general bound into a single-item bound: a **broader
   permission than the document grants**, which is `PROVISIONAL.md`'s ground 3 and P5's dangerous
   direction. So even if `ESTABLISHES` were allowed to add, encoding the addition as the bound
   would be wrong on its own terms.

3. **`00_task.md` rule 2 blocks the other route.** `avoid_hateful_content` is flagged **not
   resolvable in the corpus**. I have not been shown its text and must not guess it. The NEEDS
   gloss for `restricted_content_rule` is a one-line summary from the graph, not the clause, and
   nothing in it tells me whether that principle is the only member, the largest member, or a
   minor member of "other principles".

4. **N10 independently forbids the symbol.** Every coined symbol must trace to a substring of the
   narrowed text. `avoid_hateful_content` traces to no substring of the narrowed text — the
   narrowing stops before it. It is the `tiananmen_example` shape exactly: fluent, obviously right
   to anyone who has read the section, unanchored.

### The alternative I rejected, by name

**"Record the example as an `assumed` ontology fact — `other_principle(P) :-
restricted_content_rule(P)` — with the inference naming `ESTABLISHES`."** This is a real option and
`PROVISIONAL.md` ground 2 explicitly blesses the shape ("anything `ESTABLISHES` adds is still
expressible: as `assumed`"). I rejected it for two reasons. First, it would be the module's only
use of a coined `other_principle/1` predicate, which appears in no body of any assert — because the
bound in E8 is universal over principles and cannot be checked principle-by-principle without
negation-as-failure, which N5 forbids. A declared-and-unused coined predicate is P9's fingerprint
for dropped content, and here it would be worse: a decorative symbol standing in for a decision.
Second, it buys nothing behaviourally — knowing that one particular principle is a member of
"other principles" does not help establish that the context violates NONE of them, which is what
the exception actually requires. The honest record of the addition is this paragraph, not an inert
rule.

**Consequence, stated plainly:** the bound in E8 is encoded as the single positive predicate
`context_violates_other_principle/1`, a case-side fact. The module therefore does not decompose
"which principles exist" — it cannot, from one clause. That is a genuine loss of resolution and it
is recorded here rather than papered over with a symbol.

## 3. Other judgement calls

* **`should not`, not `must not` (the hedge).** `status` has four values and no hedged pole, and
  `toggleable` is reserved for `world` facts, so the hedge has nowhere formal to go. I encoded
  `forbid`, and I did **not** downgrade to `prefer`: 5b reserves `prefer` for **comparatives**
  ("minimize", "favour"), and this span is not a comparative — there is a determinate situation
  that violates it. The hedge is preserved in three non-formal places instead: both `read_back`
  sentences say *"should not engage in"*, `claims` C4 names it, and the `closure` reason cites it.
  This is P7's "say so explicitly in your notes if you cannot encode it". **The compiled rule is
  byte-identical to one translating `must not`. That is a real, unfixable loss at this schema.**
* **Both modifiers distribute across the coordination (N4).** I read *"gratuitous [abuse,
  harassment, or negativity] toward individuals"* — `gratuitous` scoping over all three, `toward
  individuals` likewise. Grounds: the three nouns are bare (no determiners), so they form one
  coordinated nominal that a prenominal adjective scopes over, and the postnominal PP
  right-attaches to the coordination rather than to `negativity` alone. Sense check in the same
  direction: on the non-distributive reading this clause would prohibit **all** negativity toward
  an individual, which would forbid ordinary warranted criticism. See UNSURE-1 — this is the
  weakest link in the module and it moves in P5's dangerous direction (propagating `gratuitous`
  narrows the prohibition).
* **Two asserts, not one, and no `permit` (rule 4 + N7).** The defeater is a conjunction
  (`explicitly instructed` AND `principle-respecting context`), so the prohibition survives on two
  distinct positive grounds: no explicit instruction at all, or an explicit instruction whose
  context violates another principle. `¬a ∨ (a ∧ ¬b)` is exactly `¬a ∨ ¬b`, so the pair is
  complete and neither arm overreaches. The excepted branch gets no assert: an `unless` withdraws
  a requirement, it does not create a permission.
* **`closure: unclear`.** N7's second half, applied directly: `cepa` here would re-assert the
  silence the module just declined to assert on the excepted branch.
* **`user_authority/1` is unused, deliberately.** It is a NEEDS name, so contract 2 puts it in
  `requires` verbatim whatever I think of its relevance (the worked example's
  `voice_turn_taking_rule` is the precedent). The brief notes another node assigns this section
  USER authority; I did not import that node's content, and nothing in my module turns on it.

## 4. UNSURE

* **UNSURE-1 (highest).** The N4 scoping of `gratuitous` and `toward individuals` over the
  coordination. I argued it above and I believe it, but it is a syntactic reading, not something
  the span states, and it decides whether all harassment is prohibited or only gratuitous
  harassment. An adjudicator disagreeing with me here changes what the module forbids.
* **UNSURE-2.** `lacks_explicit_instruction/1` gates the MAIN prohibition on a case-side fact. If
  a situation omits it, assert 1 does not fire and the clause looks silent about plain
  unprovoked abuse. The alternative — `not explicitly_instructed(A)` — is forbidden by rule 4, N5
  and failure mode #4, so the positive predicate is mandated (the `omits_ratios_and_techniques`
  precedent). I record the under-firing risk rather than fixing it, because the fix is the defect.
* **UNSURE-3.** Whether `abuse`, `harassment` and `negativity` deserve three separate input
  predicates or one parameterised one (failure mode #10, flat lists). I kept three because the
  span coordinates three distinct nouns and a parameter would have to invent the dimension. Low
  stakes either way.
