### F1 — THE DISJUNCTION. Three `forbid` asserts is CORRECT. `no change` — but the ruling is the central one, so it is signed here.

**L6's cheap test, applied by me before looking at the module's encoding:**

> *Does satisfying ONE disjunct satisfy the span?*

The span is *"must not create content that praises, endorses, **or** aids extremist agendas that
promote violence."* The `or` sits inside a restrictive relative clause under a **negative-scope
modal**. Content that only *aids* — praising nothing, endorsing nothing — is still content this
sentence forbids. Content that praises but does not aid is likewise forbidden. So:

- **Satisfying one disjunct does NOT satisfy the span.** To comply you must fail *all three*.
  De Morgan: `must_not(praises ∨ endorses ∨ aids)` ≡ `must_not(praises) ∧ must_not(endorses) ∧
  must_not(aids)`.
- This is L6's **refusal/prohibition-scope** arm, not its compliance-menu arm. L6: *"Refusal/
  prohibition scope → no, both must hold → one assert each."*
- **Therefore three separate `forbid` asserts, one per verb, is the correct encoding.** The
  module has exactly that.

**P4's ask fires; P4's remedy is inverted here, and applying it would be a defect.** P4 asks
*"do several obliges share one body where the span says 'or'?"* Run literally, P4 fails on two
independent counts: (i) these are `forbid`, not `oblige` — P4's harm mechanism is that obliging
all three makes satisfying one *violate* the other two, which cannot happen under `forbid`,
where the three prohibitions are jointly satisfiable by producing none of the three; and (ii)
the three asserts do **not** share one body — they are `praises(C,A)`, `endorses(C,A)`,
`aids(C,A)` respectively. This is exactly L5's class: *an entry's ASK and its REMEDY have
different validity ranges.* I ran P4's ask, re-derived the remedy from the span, and the remedy
inverts. Collapsing these to one assert on the strength of P4 would have been the wrong edit.

**Three asserts vs. one `forbid` over a disjunctive ontology class.** The alternative would be
an ontology class, say `promotes_violent_extremism(C, A)`, with three bodied entries (the
worked example's "alternatives are written by repeating the atom", lines 112–116), and one
`forbid` over it. **Extensionally the two are identical** — the same set of (C, A) pairs is
forbidden, and no situation is judged differently. So the choice cannot change what is
forbidden. It changes only what a reader is shown, and there the contract decides against the
collapse:

> `00_task.md` rule 4: *"Give each way of failing its own positive reason. … Where the clause
> gives distinct grounds, write a distinct assertion on the positive ground."*

Praising, endorsing and aiding are three distinct grounds, and the module's three read-backs
name which one fired. Under the collapsed form every violation reads back as the same generic
sentence and failure mode #4 (*right answer, wrong stated reason*) becomes available. **The
module's choice is the one rule 4 asks for. No change.**
