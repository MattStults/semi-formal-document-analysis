## Finite verbs vs. propositions demanded (N9)

**Finite verbs in the narrowed text: ONE.** `engage`, the imperative under `Don't`
(do-support; the imperative is the finite form). `authority=user` has no verb at all —
`=` is a metadata assignment, not a predication. `#avoid_abuse` has no verb.

**Propositions `ESTABLISHES` demands: TWO.**
1. the `#avoid_abuse` heading carries user authority;
2. that authority applies to **all rules in that section**.

**They disagree, and worse than by count: the two sets are DISJOINT.**
The one finite verb (E2) supports neither proposition, and both propositions rest
entirely on the verbless metadata E3, which contains no verb to count. So the N9 arithmetic
(1 vs 2) is real but it is not the interesting failure here — the interesting fact is that
a naive "translate the sentence" reflex would encode E2 (`forbid engage_in_abuse(A)`) and
thereby translate the ONE element `ESTABLISHES` does not select, while leaving the two it
does select unencoded. I record that now, before drafting, so the draft cannot drift into it.

The remedy N9's measured case wants (drop unanchored content until propositions == verbs)
does **not** transfer: here the correct module has ZERO finite-verb content and TWO
metadata propositions, one of which is downgraded (below). Per the standing warning
(`ds_opus_loop/out/l171_426_n022.lessons.md` L5) I ran N9's ask and re-derived the remedy.

## `ESTABLISHES` diffed against the narrowed span, BOTH directions (N3)

**What `ESTABLISHES` says that the span does not — "applying to all rules in that section".**
The span attaches `authority=user` to **the heading**, inside the heading's own attribute
block. It says nothing whatever about the rules underneath it: no quantifier, no "all", no
mention of rules, no scope statement. That the label distributes over every rule in the
section is (a) the document's heading-label convention and (b) the node summary's addition —
it is **not in these eight tokens**.

Under `PROVISIONAL.md` the narrowed source text governs, and what `ESTABLISHES` adds is still
expressible: as `assumed`, with the inference naming `ESTABLISHES` as its source. So:

* the label itself → an `ontology` ground fact, `textual`, citing `l1108_1367_n013`;
* the distribution over the section's rules → the bodied `user_authority/1` rule, `assumed`,
  with the inference naming both the convention and `ESTABLISHES`.

This is a genuine, deliberate divergence from the worked example `l3995_4164_n001`, which
marks the equivalent distribution rule `textual`. I diverge because that example's own node
text says *"the rules under a heading carry guideline authority"* (rules quantified in the
node), while my licence surface is a bare attribute block. Recorded as a judgement call in
the notes.

**What the span says that `ESTABLISHES` drops — the heading's imperative E2, and the anchor
as a title.** `ESTABLISHES` keeps `#avoid_abuse` (as a reference) but drops the words
*"Don't engage in abuse"* entirely. Per `PROVISIONAL.md`, `ESTABLISHES` legitimately acts as
the **selector of which claim** the module expresses; the power it is denied is changing a
claim's content. Dropping E2 is selection, not content change — and the sibling nodes of
this span (e.g. `l1108_1367_n014`) are where the section's actual norms live. So this
direction of the diff produces no correction. I note it and encode nothing from E2.

⚠️ The asymmetry-of-errors argument in `PROVISIONAL.md` ground 3 points the same way here:
following `ESTABLISHES` literally would let a single `textual` citation carry the claim that
the document assigns user authority to every rule of the section, from text that assigns it
to a heading. That is the direction in which a slip is undetectable.
