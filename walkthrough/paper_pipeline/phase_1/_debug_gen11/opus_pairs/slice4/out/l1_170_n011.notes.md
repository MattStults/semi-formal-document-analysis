# Notes — `l1_170_n011` (DRAFTER, pass A, slice 4)

## Stage 1 — the frame question, answered in words

**Should this clause be translated at all, or does it meet an abstention trigger
in `00_task.md`?**

**It meets one, and the module abstains.** `00_task.md` lists "it states a goal
rather than a condition" among the abstention triggers, and this span's main
verb *is* the goal verb: **"we aim to increase transparency … and invite public
discussion"**. The matrix verb is "aim to"; its subject is "we" = OpenAI; its
complements ("increase transparency", "invite public discussion") are the
content of an aspiration OpenAI holds about its own publishing conduct. There is
no act of the assistant here to attach a status to, and no situation that could
satisfy or violate the sentence — which is exactly the shape `00_task.md` names.

Two review-list entries independently reach the same place and were checked
before I concluded, not after:

* **P2** — the subject of the main verb is OpenAI, not the assistant/model, so
  any `oblige`/`permit` rendering would assert a rule the document never states.
  P2's remedy is "route to `ontology`" — see the next paragraph for why that
  route is also unavailable here.
* **N2** — strip the matrix verb: "aim to" is the matrix verb, and *its object
  is not even a rule*. In N2's measured case (*"we're exploring how to let
  developers…"*) the object was an embedded rule, which is what made that span
  seductive; here the object is a pair of aspirations ("increase transparency",
  "invite public discussion"), so there is not even an embedded norm to be
  tempted by. N2's promote-the-hedge remedy (`under_exploration(...)`) has
  nothing to take as an argument.

**Why not the structural-fact route instead of abstaining.** The graph-node
worked example is emphatic that "it states no obligation" is *not on its own*
grounds to abstain: a node stating a **structural fact about the document**
(which authority a section carries, what class a thing falls in) goes to
`ontology` with `asserts` empty, and is a translation. I tested this span
against that route explicitly and it fails on three counts:

1. **`PROVIDES` is `(none)`.** In the heading-node example the third route was
   forced *because* `PROVIDES` named `guideline_authority` and other nodes were
   waiting on it; abstaining would have stranded them. Nothing is waiting on
   this node, so nothing is stranded.
2. **There is no classification to record.** A structural fact classifies
   something — a rule, a section, a term. This span classifies nothing; it
   reports a motive. "OpenAI aims at transparency" is not a predicate over any
   entity the corpus quantifies over.
3. **Anything I could coin would be a hollow stub or an inert constant.**
   `aims_to_increase_transparency(model_spec)` or `purpose(model_spec,
   transparency)` is failure mode 5 verbatim — a single opaque symbol echoing
   the document's own words, which reads correctly in every explanation while
   containing nothing — and it is also N1's inert ground constant: no fact in
   any real situation can ever unify with it.

The abstention is therefore the *content* answer, not a refusal of difficulty.
The span is short, unambiguous, and completely understood; it simply states an
aim.

## ⛔ The known trap — a P3-family entry fired, and here is the branch I took

**Did a P3 / "is every claim encoded" entry fire? Yes — P3 itself, and I am
saying so explicitly as the brief requires.**

**Which branch did I take?** *Neither of the two harmful ones.* The trap's two
measured failures are (a) adding a body condition the situation can never
supply, so the rule stops firing while looking enforced, and (b) deleting the
claim. I took a third branch: **the module asserts nothing at all, and declares
that it asserts nothing, with a stated reason.**

**Can the rule still fire in a situation that does not affirmatively supply an
extra fact?** *There is no rule.* An abstention has no rule that can fire or
fail to fire, and — this is the part that matters for the trap — **it cannot
masquerade as one.** The two measured failures are dangerous precisely because
the module keeps its shape: the read-back still recites the obligation, the
`claims` list still carries it, and the enforcement is silently gone. An
abstention with every list empty has no shape to hide behind. `outcome:
"abstained"` is a declared, countable, reviewable answer, and the abstention rate
is a signal the design says it wants.

**The asymmetry I relied on.** If the abstention is wrong, the cost is one
under-translated goal statement, visible in the abstention count and trivially
recoverable. If a translation of a goal statement is wrong, the cost is an
invented obligation on the assistant, attributed to the document, cited to a
node — and failure mode 16 records that a clean reviewer given a fabricated
policy and a fluent read-back answered "faithful, nothing unsupported" and then
reasoned from the fiction. Those errors are not symmetric, which is the same
reasoning `PROVISIONAL.md` uses in its ground 3.

## Where the PROMPT, not the translator, may be producing a questionable signal

Two observations, offered as findings on the instrument rather than as
complaints:

1. **The node's `kind:` field says `conditional`.** This span contains no
   condition of any sort — no "if", "unless", "when", "where", or "provided
   that" — and its matrix verb is an aim. A translator who treats `kind:` as
   authoritative is being pushed toward manufacturing a conditional that is not
   there, which is a direct path to the invented-obligation failure. I ignored
   `kind:` in favour of the narrowed text, consistent with `PROVISIONAL.md`'s
   principle that the verbatim source governs its derived annotations. **If
   `kind:` is auto-assigned by the segmenter, this looks like a mislabel worth
   surfacing to the coordinator**, and it may well be systematic across
   purpose/preamble nodes rather than unique to this one.

2. **`ESTABLISHES` is phrased as "the one claim this module must express",** and
   the imperative "must express" sits in tension with `00_task.md`'s abstention
   triggers whenever the established claim turns out to be a goal, a heading, or
   an example. Here the tension is resolvable — `ESTABLISHES` faithfully preserves
   "aims to", so honouring it *is* recognising a goal — but the wording is
   pressure toward translating whatever `ESTABLISHES` names, and this clause is a
   clean instance of a node whose `ESTABLISHES` is accurate and whose correct
   answer is still abstention. The framing, not the translator, is what would
   produce a non-abstention here.
