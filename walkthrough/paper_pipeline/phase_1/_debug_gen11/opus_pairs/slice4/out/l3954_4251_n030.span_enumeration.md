## Verb count vs proposition count (the N9 question)

**Finite verbs in the narrowed text: 2.**

1. `use` — imperative, so finite; subject elided (the assistant).
2. `should be` — inside the parenthetical E6.

**Propositions `ESTABLISHES` demands: 3.**

1. inline LaTeX math is delimited with the inline delimiters (E3);
2. display math is delimited with the display delimiters (E5);
3. the display delimiters each sit on their own line (E6).

**2 ≠ 3 — is this a scope conflict?**

**No, and here is the resolution I took and why.** The mismatch is entirely explained
by E4: one finite verb (`use`) governs **two coordinated object+purpose pairs**, and
each pair carries its *own trigger* (`for inline LaTeX math` / `for display math`) and
its *own required act* (a different delimiter). Coordination under one verb is the
ordinary way English states two directives with one verb; it is not summary inflation.
Each of the three propositions traces to a distinct substring of the narrowed text
(E3, E5, E6), which is the test N10 sets, so nothing here is unanchored.

The alternative resolution — collapse E3 and E5 into one proposition "use the
appropriate LaTeX delimiters for math" — was **rejected**: it would name no delimiter,
which is the only content the sentence has, and would produce exactly the hollow stub
failure-mode 5 describes (one opaque symbol echoing the document's words).

So: **3 asserts**, and the count is deliberate, not drift.
