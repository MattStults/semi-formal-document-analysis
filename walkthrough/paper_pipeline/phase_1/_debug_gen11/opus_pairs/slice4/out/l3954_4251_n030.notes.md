## Stage 1 — the frame question, in words

**Translate.** No abstention trigger in `00_task.md` governs this span.

* **Not a section heading.** It is a full sentence with a finite imperative verb.
* **Not a goal.** Its main verb is `use` — a directive with a concrete trigger
  (*"for inline LaTeX math"* / *"for display math"*) and a concrete required act
  (a specific pair of delimiters). Compare a goal such as *"we aim for readable
  output"*, which names no act and no trigger; this names both.
* **Not an example.** There is no `**Example**:` heading, no good/bad response pair,
  and no dialog. It is stated as the rule itself, not as an illustration of one.
* **Expressible as rules.** A formatting convention is as rule-shaped as content
  policy is: *if the expression is display math, then the delimiters are these, and
  they go on their own lines.* Nothing here needs a normative force to be invented —
  the imperative supplies it.

Route on the three-way test in the node worked example: this node states a **norm**
addressed to the assistant, so it goes to `asserts` (not the ontology-only structural
route, and not abstention).

## ⛔ The known trap — stated explicitly, as required

**Did a P3-family entry fire?** Not as a defect: `claims` and `asserts` are already
one-to-one and nothing is unencoded. But the trap's *mechanism* — "the fix chosen was
to add a body condition that the situation can never supply, so the rule stops firing"
— applies to a choice this module makes, so I state it rather than let it pass silently.

**Which branch I took.** All three asserts carry `markdown_latex_formatting_rule(E)`
in the body. I neither deleted a claim nor added an unsuppliable guard, but I did add
a **borrowed** guard, and that deserves the same scrutiny.

**Can the rule still fire in a situation that does not affirmatively supply the extra
fact?** **No — and that is the intended reading, not an accident.** Stated plainly:
in a link scope containing only this module, all three obligations are inert, because
nothing derives `markdown_latex_formatting_rule/1`. Validation reports exactly that as
the `requires-unprovided` note. My grounds for accepting it:

* The condition is a **`NEEDS` name**, i.e. a predicate the graph says another node
  **provides**. Failure mode 15 is explicit that "never fired" has three causes and
  that an honest `requires` is what distinguishes *waiting on an unlinked clause* from
  *dead*. This is the second cause, declared.
* It is the correct content, not a defensive guard. The parent rule is defeasible —
  *"unless otherwise specified"* — and P7 instructs me to push a defeater into a body
  **when I can**. Here I can. Without it, this module asserts that a plain-text-only
  response must still carry LaTeX delimiters, which the document does not say.
* The alternative — leaving the `NEEDS` name in `requires` unused, as the birthday
  worked example legitimately does — is available and safe, and I rejected it **by
  name** because that example's own reasoning is that *nothing in it turned on* the
  borrowed concept. Here everything turns on it: the span's first two words, *"For
  math"*, presuppose the Markdown-with-LaTeX regime the borrowed rule establishes.

If the reviewing pass disagrees, the change is a one-literal deletion from three
bodies and it does not touch the assert count — I flag that so the disagreement can be
resolved cheaply rather than by a redraft.

## UNSURE — what I could not settle

1. **The index of `markdown_latex_formatting_rule/1`: expression or output?** The
   `NEEDS` gloss is written about **outputs** (*"Assistant outputs should be formatted
   in Markdown…"*), and a provider node will most plausibly define it over an output,
   `markdown_latex_formatting_rule(O)`. I index it over the **math expression** `E`.
   *Why:* the alternative needs a linking predicate (`occurs_in(E, O)`) that N10 would
   reject — no substring of the narrowed text names an output — and the node worked
   example does exactly what I have done, indexing `voice_turn_taking_rule(R)` by the
   response with the gloss carrying the "is subject to" reading. Per N8 I have written
   my reading into the gloss (*"E occurs in an assistant output that falls under…"*),
   so a provider that indexes by output surfaces as a **description disagreement**
   rather than joining silently and wrongly. **Unsettled at the module level; it is a
   linking question and I cannot see the provider.**

2. **The register of *"should be"* in the parenthetical.** Encoded as `oblige`. There
   is no pole between `oblige` and `prefer`, and `prefer` would be a category error
   (no comparative is stated). If the corpus has a convention distinguishing
   Model-Spec *should* from Model-Spec *must*, this module does not express it. See
   turn 3, P7(a).

## Where I think the PROMPT, not the translator, produced a questionable choice

**The delimiters themselves are unwritable in the fields that most need them.**
`_BAD_IN_TEXT` rejects backslashes and braces in every `read_back` and every `gloss`
— correctly, since those strings are interpolated into quoted ASP annotations. But
this clause's *entire content* is a set of backslash delimiters, so the read-backs
have to spell them in English (*"backslash-open-parenthesis"*). A reviewer comparing
the read-back to the span is comparing a transliteration to the real thing, and a
module that named the **wrong** delimiter would still read fluently. Nothing is wrong
in this module as a result, and I am not asking for the guard to be relaxed — I am
recording that on typographic clauses the read-back check is weaker than it looks, and
the weakness comes from the format, not from the translation.
