## Finite-verb count vs `ESTABLISHES` propositions

* **Finite verbs in the narrowed text: 0.** It is a verbless list item — a noun phrase
  (`outdated information`) joined by a colon to a `due to`-adjunct. The predication is
  elided.
* **Propositions `ESTABLISHES` demands: 1** — *"Outdated information: uncertainty due to
  the model's knowledge cutoff or rapidly changing circumstances."* `ESTABLISHES` is the
  same verbless apposition; it demands one classificatory proposition, internally
  disjunctive over two causes.

**Is this a scope conflict?** No — but it is the N9-adjacent shape, so I state the
resolution explicitly rather than leaving it implicit.

0 finite verbs against 1 demanded proposition is a **deficit**, not the surplus N9 warns
about (`ESTABLISHES` demanding more propositions than the text can anchor). The deficit is
purely grammatical: an elided copula in a bulleted list, restored identically by
`ESTABLISHES` and by any reader. Nothing in `ESTABLISHES` is unanchored — every content
word of it (`outdated information`, `knowledge cutoff`, `rapidly changing circumstances`,
the `or`) is a substring of the narrowed span. The only word `ESTABLISHES` adds is
*"uncertainty"*, which is the genus supplied by the enclosing list, not by this item.

**Resolution: restore the elided copula as ONE classification, and do not coin a predicate
for the added word "uncertainty".** The classification is written as two `ontology` entries
(one per disjunct of E5) whose shared head is the node's own `PROVIDES` name. The genus
"uncertainty" is carried in the `concepts` gloss of that name — where it is a written
definition that a reader can check against the document — rather than as a separate coined
`uncertainty/1` predicate, which would be a symbol tracing to no substring of the narrowed
text (N10).
