## The N9 count

**Finite verbs in the narrowed text: 2.**

1. `aim` — matrix verb; subject "we" (OpenAI). Aspirational.
2. `shape` — finite verb inside the embedded interrogative "how we shape model
   behavior"; subject "we" (OpenAI). It is *mentioned as the topic of the
   transparency*, not asserted as a norm.

("publishing" is a gerund; "to increase", "invite" (elided "to"), and "to
improve" are infinitives. None is finite.)

**Propositions `ESTABLISHES` demands: 1.**

> "By publishing the Model Spec, OpenAI aims to increase transparency around how
> it shapes model behavior and invite public discussion on ways to improve it."

That is one proposition — a single aim with two coordinated complements —
restating the span almost verbatim with "we" resolved to "OpenAI".

**Mismatch? No scope conflict.** 1 matrix finite verb ↔ 1 demanded proposition.
The second finite verb (`shape`) is subordinate and is part of the same single
proposition, not a second one. `ESTABLISHES` neither **adds** content the span
lacks nor **drops** a qualifier the span attaches (N3, both directions checked;
PROVISIONAL.md's ruling therefore has nothing to arbitrate here).

The counting exercise makes the real finding sharper rather than resolving a
conflict: **the one proposition the module is asked to express is an aim, and
its bearer is OpenAI.** That is not a rule about any act, so the mismatch is not
between the two counts — it is between the demanded proposition and the
requirement that a module express a norm or a classification.
