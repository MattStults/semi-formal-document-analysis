## L1 — A redundant TYPE literal in a body is a silent narrowing of an unconditional obligation
**MEASURED** (this module, caught only in the Stage 4 re-run of turn 2, not in turn 1 or 2).
**⭐ MECH? YES — high confidence.** Rule: for each assert body, if a literal `p(V)` appears whose
single variable `V` also appears as an argument of another literal `q(_, V)` or `q(V, _)` in the
same body, flag it. The flag is advisory, not automatic deletion — the reviewer decides whether the
relation's gloss already fixes the type.

Here `work_of(X, C), creator(C)` looked like a harmless type annotation and was one to a human
reader, because `work_of`'s gloss already says its second argument is the creator. To the solver it
is an **extra query-side fact the obligation waits on**: a situation supplying `work_of(w, c)`
without also supplying `creator(c)` never triggers an obligation the span states flatly. This is
P5's dangerous direction (narrowing) arriving in the disguise of tidiness rather than of a
qualifier, and it is invisible to a read-back, which recites the obligation in full either way.

**Ask:** does any body contain a literal whose only job is to re-state a type the other literals'
glosses already fix? If so, is it adding a fact the query side must separately supply?

*Duplicates?* Partially overlaps **P5** (scope drift, narrowing direction) and **failure mode #3**
(rules that under-fire), but neither names this trigger: P5's examples are all about qualifiers from
the text, and this literal comes from nowhere in the text at all. Worth its own entry because the
mechanical test is cheap and the human eye reliably passes it.

## L2 — "while / as / alongside" is CONCURRENT, not concessive — and N6 mis-fires on it
**MEASURED** (N6 fired on this module and was declined; recorded per the known-trap instruction).
**⭐ MECH? PARTLY.** A grep for `\b(while|as|alongside|whilst)\b` in the narrowed span, cross-checked
against a non-empty `forbid_body` or a non-empty `beats`, would surface every instance for human
judgment. The concurrent/concessive distinction itself is not decidable by a few lines of Python.

*"The assistant must respect creators … **while** striving to be helpful to users"* invites the
reading "helpfulness may never excuse disrespect", which wants a `forbid_body` ban or a `beats`
entry. The span says no such thing: it says both are pursued together. Encoding the concessive
reading manufactures a priority claim, and priority claims are the one thing the schema insists must
record who says so. **This is the third distinct clause on which N6 has produced a
plausible-but-unstated meta-claim**, and on this one the fix would have been additive rather than
weakening — which is a *new* failure shape for that entry, and arguably a worse one, since an
invented priority rule reads as authoritative.

**Ask:** if you are about to write `forbid_body` or `beats` from a connective rather than from an
explicit "regardless of" / "no exception" / "overrides", can you point at the words that exclude the
exception? If the connective is *while/as/alongside*, assume concurrent until the text says
otherwise.

*Duplicates?* Directly qualifies **N6**, and should be filed as an addendum to it rather than a free
-standing entry. It also touches the E6/N6 suspicion note already in the brief.

## L3 — An abstract act term is legitimate; an abstract ONTOLOGY predicate is the hollow stub
**MEASURED** (this module: `respect/1` survived rule 5's hollow-stub test, and the reasoning had to
be written out to see why).
**⭐ MECH? NO** — it needs a judgment about where the abstract name sits, though a mechanical
*pre-filter* is easy: flag any act functor that is a verb appearing verbatim in the span AND whose
module contains no ontology entry and fewer than two distinct body predicates.

Rule 5 ("do not write an opaque symbol that echoes the document's words") reads, on first
application, like a reason to abstain from any clause whose verb is abstract — *respect*, *honor*,
*consider*. That is the wrong cut, and taking it would abstain from a large class of real
obligations. The hollow stub is an **ontology/condition** predicate standing in for content that is
absent (`deception_policy(X)` doing all the work in a body). An **act** is supposed to be the
document's own verb — `refuse`, `produce`, `apply_default` — and its abstractness is the document's,
faithfully preserved. The discriminator is whether the module's *classification* vocabulary is
decomposed: here `creator` / `work_of` / `intellectual_property_right_of` are three anchored
predicates, not one echo.

**Ask:** is the abstract name an `act`, or is it doing work inside a `body`? If the latter, which
substring of the span supplies its content?

*Duplicates?* Sharpens **rule 5 / failure mode #5**; no existing REVIEW_LIST entry covers it (P8's
gloss half is the closest and tests something different).

## L4 — Verb count and proposition count may legitimately differ when one verb distributes over a coordinated object
**INFERRED** from applying N9 here (N9 was applied as written and produced a clean result; this is a
clarification, not a correction).
**⭐ MECH? YES, as a counter-check:** if `len(claims) > finite_verb_count`, require that each surplus
claim map to a distinct comma- or "and"-separated item of an object list in the span. String-level
and cheap.

N9's check is "how many finite verbs, how many propositions does `ESTABLISHES` demand" — and a naive
reading of 1 verb vs 4 propositions on this span looks like the exact scope conflict N9 says burns
repair loops. It is not one: *"must respect A, B, and C"* is one verb distributing over three
objects, each anchored to its own substring, plus one adjunct. Recording this so a later reader does
not abstain from, or over-collapse, a coordinated obligation on N9 grounds. The distinction that
matters is **anchoring**, not arithmetic: N9's real target is a proposition no substring supports.

*Duplicates?* An addendum to **N9**; would weaken the list if filed separately.

## L5 — Prefer the `assumed` licence for a `NEEDS` gloss the narrowed span does not mention
**MEASURED** (this module: `root_authority`'s meaning is handed over by the graph's `NEEDS` block,
and the narrowed span contains no word about authority).
**⭐ MECH? YES.** Rule: for each `concepts` entry with `licence == "textual"`, check whether the
concept name's word stems appear anywhere in the narrowed span text. A `NEEDS` name marked
`textual` whose stems are wholly absent from the span is the flag.

The good worked example marks its `NEEDS` concepts `textual` with the node id, and copying that
pattern blindly produces a `textual` citation to text that says nothing on the subject — the
"dressed-up textual" the task file calls the single worst failure available. PROVISIONAL.md's
ground 2 already implies the fix (anything the graph adds beyond the span is expressible as
`assumed`, with the inference naming its source), but nothing states it for `NEEDS` glosses
specifically, and the worked example points the other way.

*Duplicates?* None in the REVIEW_LIST. Overlaps PROVISIONAL.md ground 2, which is a ruling file
rather than a translator check — see `l1001_1107_n002.prompt_finding.md`, which files the worked
example's pattern as a prompt-level defect.
