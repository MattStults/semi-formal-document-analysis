## L1 — ⭐ MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE · MEASURED · the one I think is most real

### A coordinated infinitive series under one modal must produce one assert PER CONJUNCT

**Ask:** does the narrowed span contain a coordinated series of bare infinitives under a single
modal (`should X, Y, and Z`)? If so, count the conjuncts, and count the asserts whose act
functor is distinct. **If the module has fewer distinct act functors than the span has
conjuncts, content was collapsed.**

*Why it is a real entry and not a restatement of P3:* P3 catches a claim listed in `claims`
and encoded nowhere — it is a **self-consistency** check, and it passes trivially on a module
whose `claims` list already collapsed the three aims into one line. This entry is anchored on
the **span's grammar**, so it fires even when the module is internally consistent. That is the
exact failure this clause is historically famous for: a module asserting only "de-escalate"
reads perfectly, cites correctly, and has lost two thirds of the spec's guidance on
de-escalating radicalization.

*Mechanically:* split the narrowed span on `, ` and ` and ` inside the span of the modal; count
infinitive heads; compare against `len({functor(a["act"]) for a in module["asserts"]})`.

*Duplicates:* partially overlaps **P3** (which it strengthens) and **N9** (which counts finite
verbs — and would have counted **2** here while the span carries **6** propositions, so N9's
count alone does not detect this collapse). **N9's counting question should be widened from
finite verbs to conjuncts under a shared modal**; that is the concrete amendment I would make.

## L2 — ⭐ MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE · MEASURED

### Two qualifiers in one span can attach at different depths — resolve each SEPARATELY and say so

**Ask:** list every qualifier phrase in the narrowed span (`if relevant`, `without X`, `while
doing so`, `where possible`). For **each one independently**, name the conjunct(s) it binds and
point at the punctuation that decides it. **If the module treats two qualifiers of one span as
binding the same scope, one of them is wrong.**

*Measured here:* this span has exactly two, and they bind **oppositely** — *"if relevant"* is
inside the third conjunct and binds it alone; *"while doing so"* is set off by an em-dash after
the series and binds all three. A translator who resolves one and then applies the same
instinct to the other loses the manner constraint from two thirds of the guidance, or invents a
relevance precondition on de-escalation.

*Mechanically:* checkable in the weak form — count qualifier phrases in the span, count the
distinct body-conjunct sets among the asserts; a span with N>1 qualifiers whose asserts all
share one identical body is a flag for human review. Not fully decidable by Python, but the
flag is cheap and this class is expensive.

*Duplicates:* **N4** ("a qualifier in a list bounds ONE item") covers the single-qualifier case.
This extends it to the multi-qualifier case, where N4's own answer is *not* uniform — and N4 as
written invites exactly the wrong generalisation, because its one worked example (the meth
parenthetical) binds narrowly and so trains "bind narrowly" as the default. Proposed as an
**amendment to N4** rather than a new entry.

## L3 — MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE (in part) · MEASURED · the borrowed-gloss licence

### A `requires` gloss whose content appears nowhere in the narrowed span must not be `textual`

**Ask:** for each `concepts` entry whose `name` is a `NEEDS` name, does the narrowed span
contain the subject matter of its gloss? If not, is the entry marked `assumed` with an
inference naming the `NEEDS` block — or is it marked `textual` and citing this node?

*Measured here:* `root_authority`'s gloss is about authority level and the
`#avoid_extremist_content` section. **The narrowed span contains neither word.** A `textual`
licence citing this node asserts that this sentence says something about root authority, which
it does not — `00_task.md` lines 26–29 call that the single worst failure available. I marked it
`assumed`.

*Mechanically:* the weak form is decidable — flag any `textual` `concepts` entry that is a
`NEEDS` name and shares no content word with the narrowed span. Human judgment then rules on
the flag.

*Duplicates:* none in the current list. **This is the gap I would most want filled**, because
the production prompt actively pushes the other way (see `l1108_1367_n005.prompt_finding.md`):
`node_worked_example.md`'s good example marks every NEEDS gloss `textual` + `cites`, so a
translator following the example produces the citation `00_task.md` forbids, and no existing
review entry catches it.

## L4 — ⭐ MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE · INFERRED (identified, did not fire)

### `forbid_body` must not be populated unless the span contains an exception-excluding phrase

**Ask:** is `forbid_body` non-empty? If so, quote the substring of the **narrowed** span that
excludes an exception (`regardless of`, `never`, `no exception for`, `in all cases`, `under no
circumstances`). **If you cannot quote one, delete the entry.**

*Why:* N6 tells a reviewer where a "regardless of X" belongs. It does **not** tell them when
**not** to reach for it, and the brief records N6 producing the identical harmful weakening
under two independent critics on another clause. A `forbid_body` entry is a claim about the
**rule set** — that no rule of a shape may exist anywhere in the corpus — which is a much
stronger commitment than any assert, and it is checked by inspection rather than by running, so
a spurious one is defended by a passing check forever.

*Mechanically:* trivially decidable — `module["forbid_body"] and not any(kw in span for kw in
KEYWORDS)` is a one-line flag.

*Duplicates:* **N6**, as its missing negative half. I would attach it to N6 as a **guard**
rather than file it separately, so the two are never read apart.

## L5 — INFERRED

### On a manner constraint, `forbid` vs `prefer` turns on whether a situation can VIOLATE it — not on the enclosing modal

**Ask:** for a constraint on *how* an act is performed (`in a non-X manner`, `without being
Y`), can you describe a concrete response that violates it? If yes, it is not a comparative and
`prefer` understates it. If no — if it only ranks two acceptable responses — it is a
comparative and `forbid` invents a violation condition.

*Why:* rule 5b gives the comparative test but its examples are all *verb*-shaped ("minimize",
"avoid excessive", "favour"). A manner adjunct under a soft `should` looks comparative and is
not, and the two review entries in play pull opposite ways (5b toward `prefer`, P1 warning that
`prefer` reliably compiles to the opposite of the document). **This is the one contestable
choice in my module** and I flagged it as UNSURE rather than settling it, so this entry is
INFERRED and I would want it adjudicated before it is added.

*Mechanically:* no. Genuinely a judgment.

*Duplicates:* sits between **5b** (`00_task.md`) and **P1**; it is the missing bridge, not a
new claim.
