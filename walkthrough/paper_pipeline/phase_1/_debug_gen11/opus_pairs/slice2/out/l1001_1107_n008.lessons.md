## L1 — *"should be able to"* is a PERMISSION, and its shape invites `oblige`
**MEASURED · ⭐ MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE (high confidence)**

The narrowed span is *"the assistant **should be able to** provide information…"*. The
word `should` is the document's obligation marker everywhere else in this corpus, and the
sentence has the assistant as subject — so P2's subject test **passes** and points at a
deontic assert, which is right, while saying nothing about *which* status. The phrase is
an entitlement (*is permitted to*), not a requirement (*must*): reading it as `oblige`
would force the assistant to disclose public-figure information it merely may disclose,
turning a privacy carve-out into a disclosure mandate. That is the dangerous direction —
an over-assertion inside a privacy section.

**Ask:** does the span attach `should`/`must` to a **capability or entitlement** phrase —
*be able to*, *be free to*, *be allowed to*, *can* — rather than directly to the act? If
so the status is `permit`, not `oblige`, however imperative the modal looks.

**Mechanical check:** regex the narrowed span for
`(should|must|may)\s+(be\s+(able|free|allowed)\s+to|can\b)`; if it matches and any
`asserts` entry has `status == "oblige"`, flag.

**Duplicates:** none. Nearest is **P2**, which decides *deontic vs fact* by the subject of
the main verb; this decides *which deontic status* once P2 has passed. It is the gap P1
occupies for `prefer`, applied to the `oblige`/`permit` pair. Suggest filing adjacent
to P1 as its `oblige`/`permit` twin.

## L2 — a "for X" restriction needs a BINDER, and the binder is never in the text
**MEASURED · ⭐ MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE (high confidence)**

*"**For public figures**, the assistant should be able to provide information…"* — the
restriction quantifies over a person `P`, but the act is over the information `I`, and the
span never states the relation between them (it never says *about*). To write the body at
all you must coin `information_about(I, P)`. This puts two of the existing entries in
direct tension on the same term: **N10** demands every coined symbol trace to a substring
and this one cannot, while dropping it leaves `P` unbound and the solver refuses the whole
file (bad worked example 1). The resolution is neither — it is `assumed` with the
inference named — but nothing currently *says* that, so a translator obeying N10 literally
deletes the binder and breaks the module, and one obeying the solver marks it `textual`.

**Ask:** does the span restrict its norm with a prepositional phrase (*for X*, *in the
case of X*, *with X*) naming an entity the act term does not mention? If so, name the
relation joining them, and licence it `assumed` with the inference — never `textual` (no
substring supports it) and never omitted (the variable would be unbound).

**Mechanical check:** two checks, both cheap. (a) For each `asserts` entry, collect
capitalised variables in `act` + `body`; every variable in `act` must appear in `body`
and every `body` variable must appear in at least one body literal — an unbound variable
is a hard error. (b) For each coined `concepts` name with `licence == "textual"`, check
its name-parts appear as a substring of the narrowed span (case-folded, underscores →
spaces); flag any that do not. Check (b) is a generalised, automatable **N10** and would
have caught the `tiananmen_example` near-miss N10 was written from.

**Duplicates:** sharpens **N10** — it supplies the *remedy* N10 lacks (N10 says "you are
importing knowledge the citation cannot support", which reads as "delete it"; the right
move is often "relicence it"). Overlaps nothing else.

## L3 — an affirmatively-stated exception arm is NOT the hole N7 describes
**MEASURED · ⭐ PARTLY MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE**

This span *is* an exception to the privacy prohibition, which is the shape **N7** warns
about — and N7, read quickly, says the excepted branch carries no assert of its own and
that `cepa` there is wrong. Applied literally here it would delete the module's only
`permit`, because the exception arm would be treated as a hole. But the span states the
permission **in the document's own words** (*"the assistant should be able to provide"*),
so it is transcribed, not invented. N7's real target is an assert **manufactured** on a
branch the span merely withdrew a requirement from (*"should honor … unless it
conflicts"* — the *unless* arm says nothing about what to do there). The discriminator is
whether the excepted branch has its own deontic verb, and N7 does not currently state one.

**Ask:** on an exception arm, does the span contain an affirmative deontic verb phrase
**for that arm** (*should*, *may*, *is able to*, *must*)? If yes, encode the status it
states. If the arm is only the residue of an *unless*/*except* with no verb of its own,
encode nothing there and set `closure: unclear`.

**Mechanical check:** partial. Detecting the exception arm is cheap (the narrowed span
contains *unless*, *except*, *for X,* at its head); detecting whether that arm carries its
own deontic verb is a regex over the arm's text, which is right most of the time and not
always. Worth pairing with a human read.

**Duplicates:** amends **N7** — same clause family, opposite failure. N7 as written
protects against over-asserting on the excepted branch; this protects against
**under**-asserting on it, which on a permission carve-out inside a prohibition section
means silently re-imposing the prohibition.

## L4 — an EMPTY ESTABLISHES/span diff is a distinct result and must be recorded as one
**MEASURED · ⭐ TRIVIALLY MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE · weaker: a process lesson, not a defect**

`ESTABLISHES` here is character-identical to the narrowing but for the final period. **N3**
tells you to diff in both directions but says nothing about the empty case, and
`PROVISIONAL.md` reads as though a conflict always exists. A module whose notes say
"PROVISIONAL followed" when the ruling was never engaged is indistinguishable from one
that actually resolved a conflict — and if the owner overturns the ruling, the re-derive
list cannot be computed, because it cannot tell which modules the ruling actually touched.

**Ask:** is the diff between `ESTABLISHES` and the narrowed span empty? If so, say
`PROVISIONAL was inapplicable`, not `PROVISIONAL was followed`, and name that in the notes.

**Mechanical check:** yes, trivially — normalise whitespace and trailing punctuation, then
string-compare. Could be computed for the whole corpus in one pass, giving the exact set
of clauses PROVISIONAL.md actually decides. That set is currently only knowable by reading
every notes file.

**Duplicates:** addendum to **N3**.

## L5 — a permission carve-out with no `forbid` in it should not close `cepa`
**INFERRED (I reasoned to it here; I did not see it fail) · ⭐ MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE as a
flag, not as a verdict · weakest of the five**

A clause whose only assert is a `permit` on a bounded class exists *because* something
outside that class is not permitted — otherwise the grant is redundant. But that
prohibition usually lives in the un-narrowed neighbouring sentence, so **P6** forbids
importing it and `cnpa` cannot be asserted either. `unclear` is the only reading that
neither imports the prohibition nor declares the redundancy, and it is what I chose. I am
flagging this as INFERRED rather than MEASURED because I did not observe the wrong answer
being produced — I reasoned my way to it, and the default `cepa` is defensible.

**Ask:** if this module's asserts are all `permit` and its span is a carve-out from a
neighbouring prohibition, does a `cepa` closure make the carve-out redundant? If so,
`unclear` unless the narrowed span itself states the prohibition.

**Mechanical check:** the flag is checkable — *all asserts are `permit` AND some closure
entry is `cepa`* is a two-line query. Whether that is actually wrong needs the span, so
this should raise a review flag, never fail a gate.

**Duplicates:** extends **N7**'s closure sentence (*"a `cepa` closure re-asserts the
silence you just declined to assert"*) from the *unless*-arm case to the permission
carve-out case.

## L6 — ⭐ N10 must be applied INSIDE GLOSSES, not just to coined names
**MEASURED (on this module, by the independent critic — I missed it; it is the critic's
one pressed finding and survives in its revised version too) · ⭐ MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE**

My turn 4 ran N10 over every coined **name** and passed cleanly. The critic ran the same
question over gloss **prose** and found that `root_authority`'s gloss said *"…which no
lower level of the chain of command may relax"* — a clause present neither in the span,
nor in any sentence of the source text, nor even in the `NEEDS` line that the gloss was
supposed to be transcribing. It is exactly the `tiananmen_example` shape N10 was written
from — fluent, obviously right to anyone who has read the Model Spec, unanchored — and it
was sitting under a `textual` citation with 0 validator breaches.

**Why the existing entry misses it:** N10 says *"for each **name** you coin, which
substring of the NARROWED span does it come from?"* A gloss is not a name, so a
conscientious reader can pass N10 while writing a paragraph of unsourced document
knowledge underneath it. Glosses are the largest free-text surface in the module and the
least checked — and for a **borrowed** name the ceiling is tighter than the span, because
the gloss is meant to transcribe the `NEEDS` line, not to explain the concept.

**Ask:** for each `concepts` gloss, is every substantive clause in it traceable to the
narrowed span, or — for a `NEEDS` name — to the `NEEDS` line? Delete any clause that
comes only from your own knowledge of the document, however obviously true.

**Mechanical check:** yes, as a flag. For each `concepts` entry naming a `NEEDS`
predicate, compare gloss content words against the union of the `NEEDS` line's content
words; flag glosses introducing content words found in neither the `NEEDS` line nor the
narrowed span. On this module that flags "chain", "command", "relax", "lower" — the exact
four words of the invented clause. Cheap, high-precision on borrowed glosses; noisier on
coined ones, where added prose is legitimate (see L7).

**Duplicates:** extends **N10** from names to glosses. Should probably be folded into N10
as a second sentence rather than filed separately.

## L7 — does `textual` license the NAME or the CONTENT?
**MEASURED (⚠️ by ME ALONE — an earlier draft of this entry claimed both passes hit it,
which was false; the critic's licence audit never reaches these two rows) · NOT
mechanically checkable**

`public_figure` and `generally_public` are terms the span **uses** but does not
**define**. The prompt simultaneously forbids a gloss that restates its own name and
demands one saying what makes the predicate true — so a compliant gloss *must* contain
prose the span does not contain, which then sits under `textual`. Two independent readers
marked these `textual` and neither called it a defect, but neither could point to a rule.

**Ask:** when you mark a `concepts` gloss `textual`, are you claiming the span *uses this
term*, or that the span *states this definition*? If the second is false, say so.

**Mechanical check: no** — this needs a ruling, not a checker. It is filed in
`l1001_1107_n008.prompt_finding.md` as a prompt gap; a list entry cannot fix it, and an
entry that fires on it would fire on every correct module until the ruling exists.

**Duplicates:** none. Adjacent to L2 (which handles the *name-with-no-substring* case) and
to L6 (the *gloss-exceeds-its-source* case); this is the third corner of the same missing
ruling.
