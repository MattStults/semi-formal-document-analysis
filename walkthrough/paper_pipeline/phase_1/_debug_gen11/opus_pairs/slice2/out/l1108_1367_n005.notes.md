## Stage 0 — SPAN ENUMERATION (written before any drafting)

### (a) Finite verbs — **2**

1. `shows` — subject *a user*, in the antecedent.
2. `should aim` — subject *the assistant*, the matrix verb of the consequent.

Everything else is non-finite and hangs off `aim to`: *de-escalate*, *discourage*,
*contextualize* (three bare infinitives coordinated under one `to`), and *doing* (a
participle in the manner adjunct).

### (b) Distinct propositions the span states — **6**

| # | proposition | textual anchor |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | there is a triggering condition: a user shows signs of vulnerability to radicalization | "If a user shows signs of vulnerability to radicalization" |
| S2 | on that trigger, the assistant should aim to de-escalate | "should aim to de-escalate" |
| S3 | on that trigger, the assistant should aim to discourage extremist involvement | "discourage extremist involvement" |
| S4 | on that trigger, **and where it is relevant**, the assistant should aim to contextualize such ideologies as harmful and widely discredited | "and, if relevant, contextualize such ideologies as harmful and widely discredited" |
| S5 | the response carrying out those aims must not be moralizing | "while doing so in a non-moralizing … manner" |
| S6 | the response carrying out those aims must not be lecturing | "…, non-lecturing manner" |

**Deliberate decisions demanded by the brief:**

* **The three-part aim is THREE propositions, not one.** They are coordinated by *and*, so
  they are conjunctive (this is *not* the P4 disjunction defect — see turns.md, turn 2).
  Collapsing them into one act would be exactly the "hollow stub" the failure list calls
  #5, and — given this clause's history in this project — the deletion the apparatus exists
  to catch.
* **"if relevant" is a qualifier on S4 ALONE.** It sits inside the third conjunct, after the
  coordinator (*"and, if relevant, contextualize…"*). Propagating it to S2/S3 would invent a
  restriction on de-escalation; that is N4's exact failure shape, in the dangerous direction
  (it would let a moralising refusal to de-escalate pass whenever "relevance" was absent).
* **The manner constraint is ONE clause but TWO propositions.** *"non-moralizing,
  non-lecturing"* are two adjectives, and rule 4 of `00_task.md` ("give each way of failing
  its own positive reason") requires each its own assertion on its own positive ground —
  a single `not moralizing_or_lecturing(R)` would give a right verdict with an unsupported
  reason.
* **The manner constraint binds ALL THREE aims, not just the last.** The em-dash sets it off
  from the whole coordinated list, and *"doing so"* is anaphoric to that whole list. This is
  the *opposite* reading from "if relevant" and both are N4 questions with different answers.

* **`ESTABLISHES` adds over the span:** *nothing*. No content, no qualifier, no norm.
* **The span states beyond `ESTABLISHES`:** *nothing*. Both the "if relevant" qualifier and

Proposition count: span 6, `ESTABLISHES` 6. **Diff = 0 in both directions.** PROVISIONAL.md's
ruling ("the narrowed SOURCE TEXT governs") therefore has nothing to decide here — recorded
because a silent "no conflict" is indistinguishable from an unasked question.

## Stage 0b — ⭐ THE ABSTENTION QUESTION, in words

**Answer: this clause SHOULD have been translated, and I translate it.**

`00_task.md` lists four abstention triggers; I take each:

1. **Section heading?** No — it is a full sentence of body text.
2. **⭐ States a goal rather than a condition?** *This is the live one, and the brief is right
   that "should aim to" makes it live.* The trigger's wording is *goal*-shaped. But the
   abstention trigger as written is disjunctive-with-a-contrast: a *goal* **rather than a
   condition**. This span states a **condition explicitly and first** — *"If a user shows
   signs of vulnerability to radicalization"* — and the node's own `kind` is `conditional`.
   The thing that makes an aspirational span untranslatable is that there is no situation in
   which one can say the guidance applies; here there is, and it is named in the text. What
   "aim to" hedges is the *achievement* of the goal, not the *applicability* of the guidance,
   and that hedge is encodable (see below) rather than a reason to decline. So the trigger
   inspects but does not fire.
3. **An example?** No — no dialogue, no good/bad pair, no *"Example:"* header.
4. **Content not expressible as rules?** No — it is a conditional attaching directives to a
   named situation, which is the most translatable shape in the corpus.

**And the asymmetry decides the close call.** Abstaining here deletes the document's guidance
on de-escalating a user's radicalization from the corpus entirely — the precise loss that
`cycles/patient-pricing-2026-08-04` was reverted for. A translation that under-states the
force stays visible and citable; an abstention leaves nothing to inspect.

**How the "aim to" hedge is carried rather than dropped** (this is what makes the answer
honest rather than convenient): the hedge is promoted **into the act term** — the module
obliges `aim_to_de_escalate(U)`, not `de_escalate(U)`. That is N2's prescribed remedy for a
hedge that *is* the main verb and so has no body to be pushed into. A reader of the compiled
program sees an obligation to *aim*, which is what the document says; it is not silently
upgraded to an obligation to *succeed*.

## UNSURE — things I could not settle

1. **`forbid` vs `prefer` on the manner constraint (S5/S6).** The whole sentence sits under
   *"should aim to"*, which is soft; but *"in a non-moralizing, non-lecturing manner"* is a
   stated manner requirement, not a comparative (rule 5b's examples are "minimize", "avoid
   excessive", "favour"), and a moralizing de-escalation is a situation that plainly violates
   it — which is the test rule 5b gives. I chose `forbid`. **The residual risk is that the
   enclosing "should" makes this over-forceful by one notch.** I did not use `prefer` because
   P1 records that `prefer` has no negative pole and reliably compiles to the opposite of the
   document unless the act is renamed to the avoidance; renaming
   `respond_with(R)` to an avoidance act to obtain a weaker status seemed a larger distortion
   than the force question itself. Flagged for the reviewer as the one genuinely contestable
   choice in the module.
2. **`cepa` on `respond_with`.** This clause rules out two manners of response and says
   nothing else about responses; I read its silence as permitting (the shape of the worked
   example's reason). `unclear` is defensible on the ground that a narrow manner clause is
   thin evidence for a global commitment about responses. N7's *"a cepa closure re-asserts the
   silence you just declined to assert"* does **not** apply — that rule is about an `unless`
   arm, and this span has no excepted branch; its negative branch is simply the antecedent
   failing. Recorded rather than silently resolved.
3. **Whether `extremist_ideology_at_issue/2` should be arity 2 at all.** *"such ideologies"*
   is anaphoric to the user's vulnerability, so I tied ideology to user. A unary
   `extremist_ideology/1` would be simpler but would let the third aim fire on an ideology
   unrelated to this user — scope drift outward (P5). Kept at 2.
