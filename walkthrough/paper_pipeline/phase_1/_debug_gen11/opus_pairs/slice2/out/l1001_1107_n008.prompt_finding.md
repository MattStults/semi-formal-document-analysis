# l1001_1107_n008 — PROMPT FINDING (not a clean module; a declined fix)

> Filed under the ⭐ rule: I declined to make a change I would otherwise have made,
> **because the prompt licenses and demonstrates the thing I would have fixed.** This is a
> different result from "the module is clean" and is deliberately not filed as one.

## The finding

**A `NEEDS` concept's `gloss` is required to exist, and the worked example demonstrates
giving it `licence: "textual"` with `cites` set to the node's own id — even when the
node's `SOURCE TEXT` does not contain the glossed content anywhere.**

### Files and lines

* `prompt/10_output_format.md`, **line 66**:
  > ⭐ **And every `requires` entry must also have a `concepts` entry saying what you need
  > it to MEAN.**

  This forces the entry to exist. On its own it is not the defect — the entry is
  genuinely load-bearing (it is how a provider node's definition is ever matched to a
  consumer's need).

* `resolve_runs/graph_v2/node_worked_example.md`, **lines 47–49** — the good conditional
  node:
  ```json
  { "name": "authority_levels_hierarchy", "arity": 2,
    "gloss": "which of two levels of the chain of command is the higher one",
    "licence": "textual", "cites": "l527_796_n012", "inference": null, "toggleable": false }
  ```

* `resolve_runs/graph_v2/node_worked_example.md`, **lines 230–232** — ⭐ **the sharper
  case**, the voice/birthday node:
  ```json
  { "name": "voice_turn_taking_rule", "arity": 1,
    "gloss": "R is subject to the requirement that a voice response fit an iterative, turn-taking exchange and adapt as the conversation shifts",
    "licence": "textual", "cites": "l4251_4571_n029", "inference": null, "toggleable": false }
  ```
  The prose at **line 268** of the same file says of this entry: *"Nothing in this
  birthday example turns on voice turn-taking."* So the file states in prose that the
  span does not support the content, and in the same breath marks the fact `textual`
  and cites the node for it.

### Why this is a defect

`prompt/00_task.md` states the opposing rule in its strongest available terms:

> **Do not manufacture a citation to make a fact look textual.** Citing a plausible-looking
> clause for something the clause does not actually say is **the single worst failure
> available here** … An honest `assumed` is always better than a dressed-up `textual`.

and failure mode **16** says the licence is *"the only thing that makes an invented entity
visible"* — a fact marked `assumed` with its inference named can be checked; the same fact
marked `textual` cannot.

The two instructions collide exactly on `NEEDS` glosses. A `NEEDS` gloss is, by
construction, **the translator's assumption about what a predicate another node owns must
mean** — `10_output_format.md` line 71 says so itself: *"You are not defining the term. You
are recording what this clause has to assume about it."* A fact the prompt itself calls an
assumption is being demonstrated with the licence reserved for facts the cited text states.

**Direction of harm.** It disables the one signal designed to catch the arity/order/meaning
mismatches that N8 and failure modes 8–9 are about. If every `NEEDS` gloss is `textual`,
then a translator's *guess* at a borrowed predicate's meaning and a translator's *reading*
of the node's own words are byte-identical in the record, and the linking stage cannot
prefer the second.

## What this clause did, and why

**I followed the worked example: both `NEEDS` glosses are `licence: "textual"`, cites
`l1001_1107_n008`.**

* `root_authority/1` — content (*"rules in the #protect_privacy section carry root
  authority"*) appears **nowhere** in this node's `SOURCE TEXT`, narrowed or unnarrowed.
  This is the `voice_turn_taking_rule` shape exactly.
* `privacy_context_dependence/2` — content *does* appear in the unnarrowed source text
  (*"Whether information is private or sensitive depends in part on context"*), though not
  in the narrowing. Weaker case, same treatment for consistency.

**Absent the worked example I would have written both as `assumed`**, with the inference
*"the meaning is taken from the node's `NEEDS` block, not from its source text"*. That is
the form the licence table and 00_task.md's citation rule point at, and it loses nothing —
the name is still recorded, the link is still inspectable, and the assumption becomes
checkable rather than asserted.

I did not, for two reasons: (1) matching production behaviour is the point of this pass —
a drafter silently diverging from the prompt makes the arm measure the drafter rather than
the prompt; and (2) a one-clause unilateral departure would show up as a licence
disagreement against every other module in the cohort and be read as *this* module's
defect.

## The fix this suggests (for the owner, not applied here)

One sentence in `10_output_format.md` near line 66 settling which licence a `NEEDS` gloss
carries, and the worked examples changed to match. Either answer is defensible — a
defensible case exists that the node input *is* the cited artifact, so `textual` is
literally true of the node even when false of its source text. **What is not defensible is
leaving it unstated while the two prompt files demonstrate opposite principles**, because
the translator then resolves it silently, differently on different clauses, and nothing
records that a choice was made.

# ADDENDUM — TURN 2: independently confirmed, and the artifact no longer carries the defect

## Status change

The finding above was filed as a *declined fix*: I had deliberately kept the manufactured
`textual` citations in the module to match production, so that the arm measured the prompt
rather than me.

**That is no longer the case. Both citations were relicensed to `assumed` in turn 2**
(notes §TURN 2). The reason for shipping the defect was spent once the finding was
MEASURED from two independent directions — it is on the record either way, and the module
should not carry a citation `00_task.md` calls "the single worst failure available here".

**This section therefore stands as a PROMPT finding, not as a description of the shipped
artifact.** The prompt still teaches the defect; this module no longer contains it.

## Independent confirmation

An independent critic (`l1001_1107_n008.critic_1.md`), which did not read this file or any
of my notes, reached the same finding from scratch and filed it under its own
`## PROMPT FINDINGS`, landing on the **same two worked-example entries** I did:
`node_worked_example.md` **lines 47-49** (`authority_levels_hierarchy`) and **lines
230-232** (`voice_turn_taking_rule`), with the contradicting commentary at **line 268**.
It also supplies two citations mine lacked:

* **`node_worked_example.md` line 19 — contract 2 itself**: *"Give it a `concepts` entry
  carrying the meaning the node text hands you."* This states the requirement and **says
  nothing about which licence it takes** — so the gap is in contract 2, one level above
  where I had located it.
* **`00_task.md` line 26** (the prohibition) with its remedy at **line 29**: *"An honest
  `assumed` is always better than a dressed-up `textual`."*

Its recommended fix is addressed to contract 2 rather than to `10_output_format.md`, which
is the better target for the same reason: add one sentence to `node_worked_example.md`
contract 2 fixing the licence for `NEEDS` glosses — either explicitly blessing `textual`
(on the theory that the node's `NEEDS` block is part of the node, so citing the node id is
accurate) or requiring `assumed` with the inference naming the `NEEDS` block — **and make
the two worked examples at lines 47-49 and 230-232 match whichever is chosen.** Its
summary of the present state is the sharpest statement of the defect either pass produced:
*"the prompt's prose and its examples point opposite ways, and the examples win."*

It also records the scope explicitly: this is **not confined to this clause** — it fires
on every borrowed `NEEDS` gloss in the corpus, and the critic confirms it fires on both
borrowed names here.

## ⭐ A SECOND, SEPARATE PROMPT GAP — MINE ALONE, not corroborated

⚠️ **Attribution correction.** An earlier draft of this addendum credited this gap and the
next one to the critic. **That was wrong**, and it is corrected here rather than quietly
deleted, because inventing corroboration is the exact failure this apparatus exists to
catch — a finding that looks independently confirmed and is not. The critic file at
`l1001_1107_n008.critic_1.md` files **one** prompt finding (PF-1, the one above) and does
not raise either of the two items below. **They are mine alone and carry a single pass's
weight.** See notes §TURN 2 for the provenance problem that produced the error.

**The `textual` licence is undefined for a name the span USES but does not DEFINE.**

`10_output_format.md` forbids a gloss that restates its own name (⛔, with `pasted_text/1`
glossed *"pasted text"* as the negative example) and demands one saying **what makes the
predicate true**. For `public_figure` and `generally_public` — terms this span uses without
defining — obeying that requirement *necessarily* produces prose the span does not contain,
which then sits under a `textual` licence.

So `textual` is doing two different jobs with no ruling on which is meant:

* **"the span uses this term"** — true of both, and the reading I applied; or
* **"the span states this content"** — false of both glosses' definitional prose.

I did not call these two rows defects: under the first reading they are correct, and under
the second the prompt's own anti-restatement rule makes a compliant gloss impossible. The
critic's licence audit does not reach these two rows at all — it examines only the two
borrowed names — so this gap is untested by a second pass. **The ambiguity is real, it
is a prompt gap rather than a translator choice, and it is unrecorded.** It is also
adjacent to the finding above: both are the same missing ruling about what a `concepts`
gloss's licence ranges over — the *name*, or the *definition*.

Two clauses of one ruling would close both:

1. a gloss for a **borrowed** (`NEEDS`) name is `assumed`, inference naming the `NEEDS`
   block (the finding above);
2. a gloss for a name the span **uses but does not define** is `textual` on the strength of
   the term appearing in the span, and the definitional prose it is required to add does
   not need its own licence.

## A third item, considered and declined — also MINE ALONE

**Weakest-licence propagation into `asserts`.** `00_task.md` says *"a conclusion inherits
the weakest licence in its derivation"*, and this module's `textual` assert has an
`assumed` input (`information_about/2`) in its body. Read literally, the assert should be
`assumed`. I declined to apply it, on the ground that `00_task.md` also says *"a rule is
not a fact — licences are for the facts your module asserts"*, and because applying it
would demote nearly every module in the corpus, including the prompt's own worked
examples.

⚠️ **This too was mis-attributed as jointly held in an earlier draft.** The critic touches
the propagation rule only in passing, and only to observe that it has nothing to propagate
here because neither borrowed predicate appears in any body — a narrower point that does
not reach the `information_about/2` case at all. **The resolution above is one pass's
judgment, not two.**

Filed rather than dropped, because two sentences of `00_task.md` appear to contradict each
other on it and the resolution is currently transcript-only — the shape of thing that
ought to be a ruling in the prompt rather than re-decided per clause. But it wants a
second reader before it is treated as established.
