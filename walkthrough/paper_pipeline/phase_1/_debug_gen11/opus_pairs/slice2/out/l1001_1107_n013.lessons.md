**MEASURED** (seen on this real module) vs **INFERRED**; and ⭐ **MECHANICAL** = could a few

## L1 — Whose VOICE is the finite verb in?  ⭐ MECHANICAL (partly) · MEASURED

**Ask, before N9's verb count:** of the finite verbs in the narrowed span, how many are in
the **document's own voice**, and how many belong to a message the document is *displaying*
(anything inside `<user>`, `<assistant>`, `<system>`, `<developer>` tags or a fenced block)?
If the answer to the first is zero, what is the module citing?

Measured here: the span has ~20 finite verbs and **exactly 2** in the document's voice, both
inside an HTML comment (`<!-- GOOD: does not include … even if they've leaked online -->`).
Every `textual` fact in the module ultimately rests on those two verbs and on three bare
verdict labels. A raw verb count of 20 makes an example span look content-rich and licenses
almost anything; the voice-split count of 2 is the number that governs.

**Mechanically checkable:** yes for the split (tag- and fence-aware tokenisation of the span
plus a finite-verb heuristic); the judgement of what the residue licenses is not.

**Duplicates:** refines **N9**, does not replace it. N9 counts verbs; this asks which of them
the citation contract can reach. Recommend folding in as a second sentence of N9.

## L2 — In a GOOD/BAD example, does the module encode the DELTA between the arms, or one arm's content?  ⭐ MECHANICAL · MEASURED

**Ask:** for a GOOD/BAD span, list the properties on which the arms actually differ, and
check that at least one body literal corresponds to each. If the module's bodies mention
only properties the GOOD arm has *in common* with a BAD arm, the compiled program cannot
tell the poles apart even though its read-backs describe the good arm correctly.

Measured here: the bare-refusal BAD arm and the GOOD arm **both** decline to share the
private details. The only difference is the privacy-safe alternative. A module bodied on
`declines_to_share_private_details(R)` alone would read perfectly and be blind to BAD arm 1;
adding `privacy_safe_alternative(R)` as a second conjunct is what makes the pair
discriminable.

**Mechanically checkable:** partly — a checker can verify that a module with an n-arm span
has at least n−1 distinguishing literals or statuses; deciding *which* property is the delta
needs reading.

**Duplicates:** strengthens **P10**. P10 asks only whether the two arms differ in `status` or
in `act` — this module would pass P10 with a single-conjunct body and still be blind, so P10
as written is necessary but not sufficient. Recommend amending P10 to "differ in status, act,
**or a body literal the other arm fails**".

## L3 — N6 and P5 target the SAME phrase and pull in opposite directions — which field did it land in?  ⭐ MECHANICAL · MEASURED

**Ask:** for each "regardless of / even if / no exception for X" in the span, does `X` appear
(a) in an assert `body`, (b) in `forbid_body`, or (c) nowhere? **(a) is the harmful weakening**
— it converts an unconditional prohibition into one that fires only where X holds. (b) is the
correct destination. (c) is a half-encoding.

Measured here: *"even if they've leaked online"*. `publicly_leaked` appears in `forbid_body`
and in **no** body — the assert stays `forbid share(D) :- private_personal_information(D)`.
Following N6 as written made this module strictly better, and the recorded two-time failure
of N6 elsewhere is consistent with a critic pushing the term into (a) while believing it was
applying N6. **The trap is the ambiguity of "encode it", not the entry's destination.**

**Mechanically checkable:** yes, and cheaply — regex the span for the trigger phrases, take
the noun phrase after them, and check which of the two fields the corresponding predicate
appears in. This is the highest-value mechanical check in this set, because it distinguishes
the harmful application of a review-list entry from the correct one.

**Duplicates:** merges the live tension between **N6** and **P5**'s ⚠️ paragraph. They should
be cross-referenced in both directions; today each is readable on its own as licensing the
other's failure.

## L4 — Which items of an enumerated request did the GOOD response NOT refuse?  ⭐ MECHANICAL · MEASURED

**Ask:** when the span contains a request listing several items and a response refusing
some of them, take the set difference. Does the module classify any item the response
declined to refuse?

Measured here: the user asks for *"emails, home addresses, phone numbers, SSNs, credit card
information"* (five) and the GOOD reply refuses *"home addresses, phone numbers, SSNs, or
credit card details"* (four). **Emails are the difference, and the omission is deliberate** —
the reply goes on to offer a route to contacting the company. A module coining
`email_address/1` into the private class would be over-restricting from a span that
positively withholds it, and would read as thorough.

**Mechanically checkable:** yes — the two comma lists are extractable, and the check is
whether a predicate named after a set-difference item appears in the module.

**Duplicates:** a new *direction* of **N4**. N4 covers a qualifier binding one item of a
list; this covers an item silently absent from the second of two lists. Recommend as an
addendum to N4 rather than a separate entry.

## L5 — Does a `concepts` entry exist for every `forbid_body` name, and is that name in no body?  ⭐ MECHANICAL · INFERRED

**Ask:** for each `forbid_body.banned`, is there a `concepts` entry saying what the banned
predicate means; and does that name correctly appear in **no** assert/ontology body?

Rationale: `schema.py` checks the *shape* of `forbid_body` slots (bare name) and nothing
else — no declaration site is required and no gloss. A `forbid_body` entry is therefore the
one place in the module where an entirely unexplained symbol can sit behind a passing
validation, which is the failure-mode-#5 shape ("hollow stub") in the field least likely to
be read. And the second half of the question is L3's mechanical test stated from the other
end.

Measured only weakly here: this module *does* carry a `concepts` entry for `publicly_leaked`,
and the entry is deliberately in neither `inputs` nor `ontology` (notes, UNSURE #1). I did
not observe the failure; I observed that nothing would have caught it.

**Duplicates:** none. Nearest is **P9**, which as corrected covers only names in
`ontology`/`inputs`/non-NEEDS `requires`.
