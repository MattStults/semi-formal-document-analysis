# SPAN ENUMERATION — l1001_1107_n001

## The narrowed span, verbatim

```
#### Respect creators and their rights {#respect_creators authority=root}
```

The printed block and the narrowed span are IDENTICAL here — the node narrows to the
whole of its one source line. So P6 ("content sourced outside the narrowing") has no
gap to open on this clause: there is no surrounding context that is not also the
narrowing.

## Elements

| id | exact substring | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | `####` | a level-4 heading marker — this line OPENS a section of the document |
| **E2** | `Respect creators and their rights` | the heading's title text |
| **E3** | `{#respect_creators` | the section's anchor / identifier |
| **E4** | `authority=root` | an attribute on the heading assigning the authority level `root` |

E2 is imperative in form ("Respect …") but it is a section title, not a rule: it names
the topic of the rules below it. Nothing in the span says who must respect what, or what
respecting consists of. E4 is the only element with propositional content of its own.

## N9 — finite verbs vs propositions demanded

**Finite verbs in the narrowed text: ZERO.**
`Respect` is a bare imperative used as a title (no subject, no object beyond the topic
noun phrase). `authority=root` is an attribute assignment in the document's markup, not
a clause with a verb. There is no finite verb anywhere in the span.

**Propositions `ESTABLISHES` demands: TWO.**
1. the heading 'Respect creators and their rights' carries root authority;
2. *therefore* every rule under it is a root-level rule.

**These numbers DISAGREE (0 vs 2), and I am saying so now, per N9.**

But this is not the scope conflict N9 was written for, and I am recording why rather
than treating it as one. N9's measured failure is *text containing two propositions,
`ESTABLISHES` demanding four* — surplus content with nothing to anchor it, so each
redraft moves the unanchored part instead of removing it. Here the count is 0, because
the span's propositional content is carried by an **attribute assignment** rather than
by a verb, and a verb count cannot see it. E4 (`authority=root`) states proposition 1
directly: it assigns the level `root`, and `=` is doing the predication a verb would
otherwise do.

So of the two propositions:
* **proposition 1 is anchored** — in E4, verbatim.
* **proposition 2 is NOT in the span.** See N3 below. It is the markup's scoping
  convention, not text. It is licensable, but as `assumed`, not `textual`.

Verdict: **one unanchored proposition, and it is anchorable as an inference.** Not a
scope conflict. Not a reason to burn repair turns.

## N3 — diff `ESTABLISHES` against the narrowed span, BOTH directions

**What `ESTABLISHES` ADDS that the span does not state:**

* *"**so every rule under it** is a root-level rule"* — the step from *the section is
  marked `authority=root`* to *each rule inside the section carries root authority*.
  The span attaches the attribute to the **heading**; that the attachment reaches the
  **rules the heading governs** is the document markup's scoping convention. The
  document licenses it (this is what an authority attribute on a section header is
  for) but the span does not state it. Under the licence table that is exactly
  `assumed`, not `textual`.

**What the span states that `ESTABLISHES` DROPS:**

* **E3, the anchor `#respect_creators`.** `ESTABLISHES` identifies the section by its
  title text only. The anchor is the stable identifier and both graph glosses use it
  ("rules in the **#respect_creators** section"), so nothing is lost by keeping it —
  I keep the anchor as the coined constant's provenance.
* **E1, that this is a level-4 heading.** Not normatively load-bearing; the fact that
  it is *a* heading is retained.
* Nothing normative is dropped. `ESTABLISHES` does not, on this node, weaken or widen
  a norm — because the span states no norm at all.

Per `PROVISIONAL.md`: the narrowed span governs. Applied here, that means the module
encodes proposition 1 as `textual` and carries proposition 2's scoping step as a named
`assumed` inference, rather than asserting the whole of `ESTABLISHES` as `textual`.

## ⭐ THE FRAME QUESTION — should this clause be translated at all?

**ANSWER: YES, TRANSLATE — as a structural fact, with `asserts` empty.**

Stated in words, because a silent answer counts as unasked:

This clause is, literally and unambiguously, **a section heading** — which
`00_task.md` names as an abstention trigger. I considered abstaining on exactly that
ground and rejected it, for three reasons:

1. **The worked example decides this case by name.** `node_worked_example.md` §"A
   heading-authority node — a structural fact is translatable" is the same shape as
   this node: a heading whose content is the authority it carries. Its ruling is
   explicit — *"This node obliges nobody, and it is still a translation, not an
   abstention."* The three-way route it lays out puts this node on route 2: a norm goes
   to `asserts`; **a structural fact about the document — which authority a section
   carries — goes to `ontology` with `asserts` empty**; only a node establishing
   neither is an abstention. `authority=root` is a structural fact about the document.
2. **The abstention trigger is about heading-ness as emptiness, not heading-ness as
   form.** The reason a heading is normally an abstention is that a bare title
   establishes nothing. This heading is not bare: it carries E4, an attribute with
   propositional content. Route selection is decided by *whether the node establishes
   anything the document says*, "not what KIND of passage it is" — the worked example
   says so in those words.
3. **`PROVIDES` names `root_authority`, so other nodes are waiting on this module to
   make that predicate derivable.** Abstaining strands every one of them, and an
   abstention here is not a neutral act.

So: **translate, because the span states the structural fact that the #respect_creators
section is assigned the authority level `root`** — and because other nodes depend on
this module to make that derivable. The module is tiny, and per the worked example that
is right; I will not inflate it.

## What the module must therefore contain

* one `ontology` entry making `root_authority/1` derivable for rules under the heading;
* the concept dictionary entries that give it, and its body's predicates, written
  meanings;
* `acts` empty → `closure` empty (a clause governing no acts needs no closure entry);
* `asserts`, `beats`, `defines`, `forbid_body` empty.

## ⚠️ The `PROVIDES` / `NEEDS` collision — flagged at Step 0, before drafting

`root_authority` appears in **both** `PROVIDES` and `NEEDS` on this node. The two
contracts give opposite instructions for the same name:

* `PROVIDES`: *"use EXACTLY these names as the predicates **this module defines**"*
* `NEEDS` / contract 2: *"every one of them belongs in this module's `requires` …
  **never in `ontology`, never defined here**"*

They cannot both be honoured: a name cannot be both defined here and owned elsewhere,
and listing it in `requires` while also writing its `ontology` rule would assert both at
once. This is a defect in the node's own contract — a self-loop in the graph — not a
choice the span leaves open. It is carried into the notes as an UNSURE, resolved in
favour of `PROVIDES`, with the grounds recorded there.
