## ⛔ THE KNOWN TRAP — encountered, recorded, NOT followed

The brief warns that a critic entry shaped *"is every entry in `claims` actually encoded — and
can the rule that encodes it ever FIRE?"* has twice produced an identical **harmful
weakening**, with P3 and N1 as its nearest analogues here.

**It presented itself, in the expected shape.** Each assert body carries six literals, one of
them (`extremist_agenda_promoting_violence(T)`) resting on a predicate no node in scope
defines. The firing question invites the fix: *drop the topic literal — or drop the two
context literals — so the rule can fire.*

**That is the trap and I did not follow it.** Deleting the topic literal converts a narrow
carve-out ("critical discussions **of such topics**") into a general permission to provide any
critical discussion whatsoever; deleting the context literals extends the permission to every
setting. Both make the rule fire by **weakening what it says**, and both push the module in
the over-permission direction that `PROVISIONAL.md` ground 3 identifies as the one that
"is indistinguishable in the output from a real rule". The same reasoning blocks the N1-shaped
variant — promoting `extremist_agenda_promoting_violence` from `inputs` into a bodied
`ontology` rule so it becomes derivable. I have **no definition** for that class: it lives in
the prohibition half of the sentence, outside the narrowing, and inventing a body for it is
failure mode 1 plus the hollow-stub rule. It stays in `inputs`, where a case supplies it.

A rule that does not fire because the case does not satisfy it is correct. Failure mode 15 is
explicit that "never fired" has three causes, and declaring `requires` honestly is what lets
the third be told from the first.

would be worse than losing it.** Rule 8b says a superiority claim goes in `beats` with this
clause as the sayer. The relation is real: this permission carves out of *"must not create
content that praises, endorses, or aids extremist agendas that promote violence"*. But **I was
not given that node's id** — it is a different node of the same sentence, and no id for it
appears anywhere in my input. A `beats` entry needs a winner and a loser, and writing a guessed
id would be a fabricated cross-reference behind a passed check. I left `beats` empty. The
consequence is real and I am not softening it: **a downstream query joining this permission
against that prohibition will find no ordering between them**, and the `unclear` closure is the
only thing recording that this module knows it is not the whole story. If the coordinator can
supply the sibling node's id, a `beats` entry with this clause as sayer is the right addition.

**UNSURE-2. The arity of `objective_point_of_view` is my choice and could be wrong.** Contract
2 assigns the arity to me. I read it as **arity 1** — a property of the discussion, "D is
presented from an objective point of view". It could equally be **arity 0**, a constant naming
the point of view itself, referred to rather than predicated (the NEEDS gloss, *"An objective
point of view, referenced by … as a condition for allowed discussion"*, reads more like a thing
than a predicate). Per N8 I wrote my reading into the `concepts` gloss so a mismatch with the
providing node surfaces as a **description disagreement** rather than a silent link failure.
The same uncertainty attaches to `root_authority/1`, which I read as taking a section.

**UNSURE-3. I licensed `root_authority` as `assumed`, deviating from the worked example.** The
worked example marks NEEDS-derived concepts `textual` citing the node id (it does this even for
`voice_turn_taking_rule`, a name irrelevant to its span). I followed that for

graph contract as the source. **I flag this as a deliberate divergence from the worked

**UNSURE-4. The openness of the context class is under-encoded, and no field can hold it.**
