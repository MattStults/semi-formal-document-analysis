restrictive modal over an act (`generate`) whose implied agent is the assistant: it does not merely
describe a class, it bounds when the class may be produced. "May only … under X" is a genuine

No N2/matrix-verb problem: the main verb is *"may … be generated"*, its subject is *sensitive
content*, and its object is content, not a rule. (Contrast the measured N2 case, which is the

## TRAP CHECK

The brief's known trap is an entry of the shape *"is every entry in `claims` actually encoded — and
can the rule that encodes it ever FIRE?"*, whose analogues are P3 and N1. **It fires on this module,
and I am declining it.** Recording the firing and the judgement, as instructed.

**What fires.** `specific_circumstance/1` is derived by four `ontology` rules
(educational / medical / historical / transformation) and then **used by nothing** — it appears in no
`asserts` body, in no other ontology body, and in no `forbid_body` slot. Under P9's corrected form it
is a coined name in `ontology` with no use, which that entry calls *"the fingerprint of dropped
content."* Under N1 the four rules are bodied and would classify real situations, so they are not
inert constants — but nothing consumes what they classify. Claims C3 and C4 are therefore
represented in the module only as ontology that hangs free.

**The two available "fixes", and why each is the trap.**

1. **Make the existing rule fire off it** — replace `outside_specific_circumstances(C)` in the
   assert body with `not specific_circumstance(C)`. This is *exactly* the trap: it makes the rule
   fire by weakening what it asserts, converting a positive finding about the case into
   negation-as-failure. It is banned three times over — `00_task.md` rule 4 (*"`not p` carries no
   account of why, and the read-back then states a wrong reason for a right verdict"*), failure mode
   4, and `REVIEW_LIST.md` N5 (*"Under NAF, `not X` makes SILENCE license the act"*). It would also
   invert the safety direction: a case with no circumstance facts supplied at all would silently
   become forbidden, or — read the other way at query time — the absence of the fact becomes the
   ground for a verdict. **Declined.**

2. **Add a consumer** — `permit generate(C) :- sensitive_content(C), specific_circumstance(C)`. This
   is the harmful weakening in its most attractive dress: it makes all four rules live, it makes the
   module look complete, and it is **false to the span**. *"May only be generated under specific
   circumstances"* is a necessary condition; it does not permit generation inside those
   circumstances, it declines to forbid it there. N7 names this precisely (*"the EXCEPTED branch is a
   hole, not a rule"*), and the module already recorded the reasoning as C6 before I got here.
   Adding this assert would license generating erotica on any educational pretext. **Declined —
   this is the one that would do real damage.**

**Judgement.** The four `specific_circumstance` ontology entries are **correctly present and
correctly unconsumed.** They are the traceable pieces `00_task.md` rule 3 requires for C3 and C4;
they are the only record in the corpus that the span named those four circumstances; and they are
available to any later module that needs to reason about them. A rule that classifies truthfully and
is not yet joined is failure mode 15's third cause (*"it is waiting on a clause not yet linked in"*),
not the first (*genuinely dead*) — and the prompt's remedy for that is to declare the situation
honestly, which the module does.

**Grade: NOT A DEFECT.** No repair proposed. Nothing here reduces `asserts`.

I also confirm the one finding I did raise (F1) fails the trap test in the safe direction: broadening
`sensitive_content` would make the prohibition fire on *more* cases, not fewer, and would not weaken
what any rule asserts. I declined it anyway, on invention grounds, not on trap grounds.
