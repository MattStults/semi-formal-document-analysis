# UNSURE — everything I could not settle

**UNSURE 1 — the `#chain_of_command` tag is dropped, and I am not certain it should be.**
The span does not merely mark the refusal BAD; it names the rule the refusal offends. I
encoded the *label* and dropped the *tag*, on two grounds: `chain_of_command` is **not** in the
NEEDS block, so putting it in `requires` would invent a cross-reference the graph did not
make; and using it in a body would mean guessing what the chain-of-command rule says, which
rule 2 forbids. The cost is real — a reader of this module cannot see which rule the document
says the BAD response broke. A coordinator with the graph in front of them may well judge that
`chain_of_command/1` belongs in `requires` after all.

**UNSURE 2 — is `forbid` the right force for a BAD-labelled example, in *this* module?**
The prompt's worked-example node used `prefer`, on the stated ground that *its* lesson is a
preference between two permitted responses. I used `forbid`, because this BAD arm carries a
rule-violation tag rather than a stylistic contrast. But the tag attributes the violation to
`#chain_of_command`, so a reasonable reading is that the *prohibition* belongs to that clause
and this example only *illustrates* it — in which case this module should carry the `permit`
alone and the `forbid` is folding in another clause's content. I chose `forbid` because
dropping the BAD arm entirely leaves the module unable to distinguish the two poles (P10) and
loses content the span carries and `ESTABLISHES` had already dropped once (N3). Recorded so the
choice is visible rather than assumed.

**UNSURE 3 — is `cites_privacy_of_individuals(R)` load-bearing in the `forbid` body?**
Keeping it means a bare refusal with no stated reason escapes the prohibition; dropping it
would make the rule "refusing such a request is forbidden", which is arguably what the GOOD/BAD
contrast actually shows (the GOOD response answers; the BAD one refuses). I kept it, because
the span exhibits only the privacy-citing refusal and rule 4 wants each ground stated
positively — an honest under-assertion that stays visible, per `PROVISIONAL.md` ground 3.
I do not regard this as settled.

**UNSURE 4 — `seem to` / `the most` in E8.** I read them as selection guidance inside the
answer rather than as conditions on the permission, and encoded the flat `has_online_presence`.
If they are read as conditions, the permission is narrower than I have written it.

**UNSURE 5 — `unclear` on both closures.** After turn 3 both act classes carry `unclear`.
I believe each is separately justified (an example that condemns one response settles nothing
about the document's silence elsewhere, and the surrounding `#protect_privacy` section is what
governs the other cases). But two `unclear`s in one module can also read as an evasion of a
declaration that is forced precisely to prevent evasion, and I cannot rule that reading out
from inside this clause.
