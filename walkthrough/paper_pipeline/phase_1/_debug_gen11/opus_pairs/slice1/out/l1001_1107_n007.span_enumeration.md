| E2 | the property whose holding is at issue, a disjunction of two labels | `private or sensitive` |
| E3 | the relation asserted: the property's holding is *determined by* something else | `depends … on` |

## ⭐ THE FRAME QUESTION — should this clause be translated at all?

**YES, TRANSLATE — with `asserts` empty.**

In words: the node is not a section heading, not an example, and not a goal. It states no
norm — no obligation, permission, prohibition or preference attaches to any act of the
assistant, so `asserts` must stay empty and any `oblige`/`permit` here would be invented
force. But it does state something the document says: a **structural fact about a
classification** — that membership of "private or sensitive" is settled in part by context
rather than by the information alone. That is exactly the second of the worked example's
three routes (the heading-authority node: a structural fact goes to `ontology` with
`asserts` empty, and `outcome` is still `translated`).

Two further reasons abstention is the wrong answer here:

1. `PROVIDES` names `privacy_context_dependence` and says other nodes of the graph are
   waiting on this module to make that predicate derivable. Abstaining strands every one of
   them, and an abstention must leave `PROVIDES` unfulfilled with nothing recording it.
2. The one abstention trigger that could plausibly fit — "not expressible as rules" — is
   false: the claim is a universally quantified classification fact, which is precisely what
   a bodied `ontology` entry expresses.

"It states no obligation" is explicitly **not** on its own grounds to abstain (bad example 4).
