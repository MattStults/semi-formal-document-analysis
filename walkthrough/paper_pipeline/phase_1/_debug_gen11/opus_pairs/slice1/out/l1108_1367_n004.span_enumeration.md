## N9 — finite-verb count vs proposition count

* **Finite verbs in the narrowed text: 1.** `may provide` (modal + main verb). There is no
  second finite verb — "critical/discouraging/factual" are adjectives, "of such topics" and
  "in contexts like …" are prepositional phrases. (`However` is a connector, not a verb.)
* **Propositions `ESTABLISHES` demands: 1.** A single permission, with a disjunctive
  subject-matter qualifier, a POV qualifier, and a disjunctive context qualifier.

**1 vs 1 — NO SCOPE CONFLICT.** The disjunctions multiply the number of *rules* (three
`asserts` bodies for E2), but they do not add propositions: they are alternative grounds for
the one permission. Nothing here needs content the span does not carry, so this clause is not
a repair-loop-exhaustion candidate.

## N3 — diff `ESTABLISHES` against the narrowed span, BOTH directions

`ESTABLISHES`: *"The assistant may provide critical, discouraging, or factual discussions
(from an objective point of view) of extremist agendas that promote violence in contexts like
news reporting, historical analysis, or education."*

**What ESTABLISHES ADDS:**
* It resolves E4's anaphor `such topics` → **"extremist agendas that promote violence"**.
  That phrase is a substring of the *printed* sentence but **not of the narrowed span**.
  Under `PROVISIONAL.md` (the narrowed span governs; `ESTABLISHES` may not add content), this
  resolution cannot be taken as `textual`. It is not droppable either — an anaphor with no
  referent leaves the permission unbounded, which is the dangerous direction (P5). ⇒ **encode
  it, licensed `assumed`, with the inference naming the anaphor and its antecedent.** This is
  exactly the escape `PROVISIONAL.md` ground 2 provides: "anything ESTABLISHES adds is still
  expressible: as `assumed`. Nothing is lost, only marked."

**What ESTABLISHES DROPS:**
* `However` (E6) — the carve-out relationship to the prohibition in the same sentence. Not
  recoverable as a `beats` entry: the span states a permission, not a superiority, and the
  other node's id was not supplied. Recorded as UNSURE in the notes; **not invented**.
* Nothing else. Unlike the measured N3 case (`l831_1000_n005`), `ESTABLISHES` here **keeps**
  the parenthetical (E3) and **keeps** "contexts like …" (E5). There is no qualifier deletion
  to defend against, so the span and the summary agree on the norm's content.

## ⭐ THE FRAME QUESTION — answered in words, before drafting

**YES, TRANSLATE.** Reason: the span **states a norm**. Its main verb is `may provide`; its
subject is the assistant; the deontic force is a permission the document grants outright.

Checked against each abstention trigger in `00_task.md`:
* **section heading** — no; it is a full sentence inside a rule's body text.
* **states a goal rather than a condition** — no; it is not aspirational ("we aim to…"), it
  grants a permission whose conditions the span itself supplies (E2, E3, E5).
* **is an example** — no. E5 contains examples (*"contexts like …"*), but the span is not
  itself a document example: there is no good/bad response pair, no dialog, no illustration
  of another rule. A norm that lists exemplary contexts is still a norm (so P10 does not
  apply — there are no two poles here).
* **not expressible as rules** — no; it is a conditional permission, the most directly
  expressible shape there is.
* **N2 matrix-verb check** — the matrix verb is `may provide`, its subject is the assistant,
  and its object is a **discussion**, not a rule. This is not the `l1108_1367_n014` shape
  ("we're exploring how to let…"). Nothing is hedged into a matrix verb.
* **N7 check** — is this merely an excepted arm, a hole rather than a rule? **No.** N7's case
  ("should honor … *unless* it conflicts") *withdraws* a requirement without saying anything
  about the excepted branch. Here the document affirmatively writes **"it may"** on the
  excepted branch. An explicit `permit` is what the text says, and encoding it is not
  inventing force. (N7 still governs the `closure` choice — see notes.)
