## ⭐ THE FRAME QUESTION — should this clause be translated at all?

**YES, TRANSLATE.** Grounds, against each of `00_task.md`'s four abstention triggers:

* **Section heading?** No. `ESTABLISHES` calls it one, but the narrowed SOURCE TEXT is a
  complete declarative sentence with a finite verb and a deontic operator. The narrowing
  governs (PROVISIONAL.md). A heading has no `may only`.
* **States a goal rather than a condition?** No. `"may only be generated under specific
  circumstances"` is a condition on an act, not an aspiration.
* **Is an example?** No. It *contains* two illustrative lists (E2, E7–E10); it is not itself a
  worked example, and the lists are inside a norm rather than instead of one.
* **Not expressible as rules?** No. It is expressible: a prohibition on generating sensitive
  content outside the bounding condition, plus the classification the section provides.

Two further reasons that are not triggers but settle the direction:

* `PROVIDES: sensitive_content` — other nodes of the graph are waiting on this module to make
  that predicate derivable. Abstaining strands every one of them (the heading-node precedent
  in the worked examples: "do deliver what PROVIDES promises").
* The deontic bearer is the model. The passive `"be generated"` elides an agent, and the
  agent of generation in this document is the assistant — not OpenAI, not the document, not a
  message. So P2 does not route this to `ontology`-only.

**The N2 near-miss, recorded because it is the one that could flip this answer.** N2 was
measured on `l1108_1367_n014` — a sibling node of this very span-range — where a matrix verb
(*"We're exploring how to ..."*) made an embedded rule read as translatable when the passage

heading **establishes that** ..."), not in the source text. Scoring N2 on the narrowed span,
the matrix verb is `may ... be generated` and its subject is `Sensitive content` — the span
states the rule, it is not about one. N2 does not fire.
