## 1. QUESTION ZERO — SHOULD THIS CLAUSE HAVE BEEN TRANSLATED AT ALL?

**Answer: YES, it should have been translated — but the reason is NOT that the narrowing
removed the words "For example,". It should have been translated in spite of that.**

The printed source line is `For example, the assistant should be able to provide the office
phone number of a public official but should decline to respond to requests for the
official's personal phone number (given the high expectation of privacy).` The node narrows
to the fragment beginning *after* the discourse marker. So the marker is gone from the text
the drafter was told to translate.

**Does the narrowing change the answer? No. A narrowing that deletes "For example," cannot
convert an illustration into a norm; it can only HIDE that the passage was offered as an
illustration.** Whatever this span is, it was that before anyone cut two words off the front.
If the abstention trigger "it is an example" applied to the full line, it applied to the
narrowed fragment too, and the narrowing would then be a defect in the graph, not a licence.
I am treating the narrowing as evidentially worthless in either direction and scoring the
content itself.

Scoring the content itself, the abstention trigger does not fire, for four reasons that are
independent of the narrowing:

1. **The controlling test in the contract is not "what KIND of passage is this".**
   `node_worked_example.md` states it outright: *"What decides between them is whether the
   node establishes anything the document says — not what KIND of passage it is."* And it
   supplies a node that is expressly *"a document example (a good/bad response pair)"* and is
   nonetheless **translated**, not abstained (`l4251_4571_n029`). So "it is an example" in
   `00_task.md` is not a syntactic trigger on the word *example*; it is a trigger for a
   passage whose only content is a dialog or an illustration carrying no extractable norm.

2. **This fragment carries deontic force in its own finite verbs.** Two of them: *should be
   able to provide* and *should decline to respond*. It does not narrate a good/bad exchange
   and leave the reader to infer the lesson; it states the lesson directly, in the document's
   own deontic vocabulary, with a subject that is the assistant.

3. **It quantifies over a class, not an incident.** "a public official", "the office phone
   number", "the official's personal phone number" are classes. There is no named person, no
   named conversation, no transcript. Contrast N10's `tiananmen_example` — a real incident
   pulled in from outside the text. Nothing here is an incident. A situation fact can unify
   with every predicate in this module (N1's test), which is precisely what an inert
   illustration cannot offer.

4. **It supplies its own ground.** "(given the high expectation of privacy)" is a *reason*,
   attached to the prohibition arm. Illustrations do not usually argue; rules do. And the
   node's own `NEEDS` block hands the module `root_authority` — *rules in the #protect_privacy
   section carry root authority* — which is the graph asserting that this material is
   rule-bearing and inherits root authority. An abstention here would strand that reading.

**What I am NOT saying.** I am not saying the span is a free-standing corpus norm. The
document offered it as an instance of a more general privacy principle stated upstream, and
after the narrowing nothing in the module records that. The module's read-backs now speak as
though the Model Spec had a stand-alone rule about public officials' telephone numbers. That
is a fair description of what the sentence says, but it loses the sentence's *rank* — an
illustration of a principle is weaker evidence about unlisted cases than a principle is. The
right place for that worry is the module's `closure`, and — see Finding 6 — the drafter
happened to handle it correctly by choosing `unclear` on `provide_number` rather than a
confident `cepa`. Had the module put `cepa` there, I would be arguing that the narrowing had
been used to manufacture a document-wide commitment out of an example, and that WOULD be
conclusion-changing.

**Verdict: translate. Not an abstention. The narrowing is not what makes it translatable, and
it should not be cited as if it were.** Class NARROW-1 below makes this mechanical.

### PF-3 — A narrowing may delete a discourse marker, and nothing records that it did.

**Where:** the node input format itself (`[node narrows this span to: "…"]`), and
`REVIEW_LIST.md` P6, which tells the translator *"the printed block around it is context, not
licence"* — with the PROVISIONAL ruling still unratified.

P6 correctly stops content being imported from outside the narrowing. It does not stop
*evidence about the passage's kind* being destroyed by the narrowing. Here the deleted prefix
is "For example," — two words whose entire function is to tell a reader that what follows is an
instance. Under P6 as written, the translator is instructed to disregard the only marker that
bears on the abstention decision, and the module that results is indistinguishable from one
translating a free-standing rule.

I reached the same verdict on the merits (§1), so nothing turns on it here. But the input
format currently makes an example-marker deletion invisible, and that is a general hazard: the
next span it happens to may not have two finite deontic verbs to save it. A one-line remedy is
for the node builder to surface the deleted prefix explicitly (e.g. `[narrowing dropped a
leading discourse marker: "For example,"]`) so the abstention question is forced rather than
optional.

## 6. CLASSES RAISED

Each is a question a later reader can apply mechanically to a DIFFERENT clause.

**NARROW-1.** *Does the narrowed span begin mid-sentence, and do the deleted leading words
include a discourse marker of illustration ("For example", "For instance", "e.g.", "Consider",
"Say that", "Suppose")? If so, was the abstention question answered explicitly somewhere in
the record?* — **MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE (partly):** the marker test is a prefix match of the
printed source line against the narrowed string plus a small marker list — a few lines of
Python. Whether the abstention question was *answered* is not mechanical, but "the module
contains no abstention discussion and the marker was deleted" is a clean flag for human review.

**NARROW-2.** *If the span is an illustration of a principle stated elsewhere, does any
`closure` entry commit the corpus to `cepa` on an act class the illustration only touches in
passing?* — **MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE (partly):** listing `cepa` entries whose act class appears
in exactly one assert, on a node whose narrowing dropped an example marker, is mechanical; the
"in passing" judgment is not. This module passes it (`unclear`, not `cepa`).

**ASSUME-1.** *Does any assert state a deontic status the narrowed span does not state in its
own words, while carrying `licence: "textual"`? Every assert that adds force beyond the span's
verbs must be `assumed` with a named `inference`.* — **MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE (partly):** the
"textual + cites self" half is trivially checkable; deciding whether the span states the force
is not. A high-yield narrowing: flag any module where the count of `textual` asserts exceeds
the count of deontic finite verbs in the narrowed span.

**NAF-1.** *Does any `body` in the module contain `not`? If so, does the rule it guards PERMIT
or ALLOW something? A `not` in the body of a `permit` makes silence license the act.* —
**MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE: yes, fully.** Substring `not ` in any `body`, joined against the
entry's `status`. `permit`/`prefer` + `not` is the high-severity cell; `forbid`/`oblige` + `not`
is the lower one. This module has zero.

**GROUND-1.** *Where the span gives a REASON for a directive ("given…", "because…", "since…",
"as…"), does that reason appear in the BODY of the corresponding assert, or only inside its
`read_back`? A reason present only in the read_back is decorative — deleting it changes no
verdict.* — **MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE (partly):** "the span contains a reason connective" and
"some coined predicate traceable to the reason phrase appears in an assert body" are both
scriptable; the tracing step needs a substring heuristic. This module passes.

**GLOSS-1.** *Is any `ontology` entry's `gloss` byte-identical (or near-identical) to the
`concepts` gloss of the same predicate? An ontology gloss must describe the BODIED CASE, not
the predicate.* — **MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE: yes, fully.** Normalize whitespace, compare
`ontology[i].gloss` against `concepts[name_of(ontology[i].atom)].gloss`. This module has one
hit (Finding 10), left unedited as cosmetic.

**ORDER-1.** *For every predicate of arity ≥ 2 named anywhere in the module, does its
`concepts` gloss distinguish the argument positions — by naming the variables, by "first
argument"/"second argument", or by an unambiguous relational phrase?* — **MECHANICALLY
CHECKABLE: yes, near-fully.** For each concept with `arity >= 2`, require that every variable
appearing in the atom occurs in the gloss text, or that the gloss matches an ordinal pattern.
This module passes on all three of its arity-2 names; N8 currently asks the question only of
BORROWED relations, and this class widens it to coined ones, where the same silent inversion is
available.

**CLOSURE-1.** *Is every distinct functor in `acts` present exactly once in `closure`, and does
each `reason` quote or paraphrase something in the narrowed span rather than general policy?* —
**MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE (partly):** the set-equality of functors is fully mechanical (this
module passes 2/2); the reason's groundedness is a substring heuristic at best.

**NEEDS-1.** *Is every `NEEDS` name present in `requires`, spelled exactly, absent from
`inputs`, absent from `ontology` heads, and given a `concepts` entry — with its unusedness in
bodies NOT treated as a defect?* — **MECHANICALLY CHECKABLE: yes, fully**, given the node input
alongside the module. Worth automating precisely because the naive "declared and never used"
check fires on correct work (the corrected P9), and a machine check that encodes the correction
stops a reviewer re-deriving the wrong version.

## 7. WHAT I COULD NOT SETTLE

1. **Whether the corpus wants the `forbid provide_number` arm at all.** I judged it a sound,
   honestly-licensed inference (Finding 2), and it errs in the safe direction. But it is a
   prohibition the span does not utter, and there is a coherent house style — visible in
   N7 — under which a module asserts only what the span asserts and lets the query side derive
   incompatibility between "obliged to decline" and "provided anyway". Which of those this
   corpus wants is a standing design question I cannot answer from the four contract files, and
   it should be ruled on rather than left to each drafter. **I did not remove the assert**: on
   an unsettled question, deleting content is the irreversible move and the invisible-deletion
   failure is the one this dispatch is measuring.

2. **The right arity and shape for `root_authority`.** The `NEEDS` gloss is propositional —
   *"rules in the #protect_privacy section carry root authority"* — while the module makes it
   `root_authority/1` over a rule. That is the natural reading and matches the heading-authority
   worked example (`guideline_authority/1` over a rule), but the true arity is owned by whatever
   node PROVIDES it, which I am fenced from reading. A mismatch here would be a link-time
   finding, not a defect of this module, and it is exactly the disagreement PF-1 says the licence
   field is currently unable to surface.

3. **Whether `privacy_context_dependence` SHOULD bear on this span's outcome.** The graph linked
   it, and there is a reading on which context-dependence qualifies the office-number
   permission — an office number sought in order to harass is a different case. The span does
   not say that, and building it in would narrow the permission on untraceable grounds (and,
   built into the decline arm instead, would weaken a prohibition — see the TRAP section). I
   left it recorded-and-unused, which is what contract 2 requires. But whether the graph
   intended it to be load-bearing here is not decidable from the node.

4. **PF-1's remedy.** I am confident the current encoding is prompt-taught and confident the
   licence field cannot presently carry the distinction. I am NOT confident which fix the
   project wants — a ruling that self-cite is correct for `NEEDS` glosses, a mandated `assumed`,
   or a new licence value. That is an owner's decision and I have not pre-empted it.
