## (a) Stage 0 — span enumeration

NARROWED text (the printed source line is longer; the narrowing governs):

> "the assistant should be able to provide the office phone number of a public official
> but should decline to respond to requests for the official's personal phone number
> (given the high expectation of privacy)"

**Finite verbs in the narrowed text: 2.**

1. `should be able` (modal + copula; the subject "the assistant" governs it) — heading the
   infinitival `to provide the office phone number of a public official`
2. `should decline` (modal + verb; same elided subject, carried across "but") — heading the
   infinitival `to respond to requests for the official's personal phone number`

Non-finite / nominal and therefore NOT counted: `to provide`, `to respond`, `requests` (noun
here — "requests for X"), `given` (participial preposition heading the parenthetical).

**Propositions `ESTABLISHES` demands: 2, plus one attached ground.**

- P1 the assistant should be able to provide a public official's **office** phone number
- P2 the assistant should decline to respond to requests for that official's **personal**
  phone number
- (ground) the reason for P2 is "the high expectation of privacy" — a parenthetical
  *because*-clause attached to P2, not a third independent proposition. `ESTABLISHES`
  reproduces it with the same subordination ("given the high expectation of privacy").

**2 finite verbs / 2 propositions — no mismatch, no scope conflict to resolve.** Handled
the ground by giving it its own encoded step (the `high_privacy_expectation/1` ontology
rule) so that P2's assert has a *positive stated reason* rather than a bare
number-type test — task rule 4.

Content that lies OUTSIDE the narrowing and is therefore deliberately not encoded:
the leading `For example,` and the whole second sentence
(`When possible, citations should be used to validate any provided personal data[^l132]`).
Nothing in the module mentions citations or validation.

## (b) Stage 0b — the abstention question, answered

**No — this clause is translated, not abstained on.**

The source line does begin "For example, ...", which is one of `00_task.md`'s abstention
triggers, so the question is live rather than rhetorical. Three things decide it the other
way:

1. **The node narrows the span past the example marker.** The narrowing begins at "the
   assistant should be able ..." — `For example,` is outside it. What the narrowing hands me
   is not an illustration of a norm, it *is* a norm, stated with a deontic modal and with the
   assistant as its subject.
2. **The worked-example file rules on exactly this shape.** `l4251_4571_n029` is a document
   example and is translated, and the file states the discriminator explicitly: "What decides
   between them is whether the node establishes anything the document says — not what KIND of
   passage it is." This span establishes two things the document says.
3. **It has translatable normative force in both arms.** "should be able to provide" is a
   permission on an act of the assistant; "should decline to respond" is an obligation on an
   act of the assistant. Neither is a heading, neither is a goal, and both are expressible in
   the fixed four-status vocabulary without inventing force.

So: `outcome = "translated"`. Abstaining here would strand a genuine permission/obligation
pair on the sole ground that a sentence the narrowing excludes began with "For example".

### Things I could not settle

1. **The third assert (`forbid provide_number(N)` on the personal number) is my least certain
   item.** The span states an obligation to *decline to respond*; the prohibition on *providing*
   is an entailment, not a quotation, and I marked it `assumed` with the inference named rather
   than dressing it as `textual`. The argument for keeping it: without it, a query about the act
   of providing the personal number joins nothing at all, and the clause's actual protective
   content would live only in an obligation about a different act. The argument against: it is
   two encodings of one proposition, and a reviewer counting propositions may read it as scope
   inflation. I kept it, and the licence makes the choice visible and reversible.
2. **The `provide_number` closure (`unclear`) is a genuine judgment call.** `cepa` is arguable
   — this is a permissive example inside a privacy section — but the span settles only two
   specific numbers and I did not want its silence about every other number read as a blanket
   permission. `unclear` records that the clause does not settle it. This is the kind of choice
   that measurably flips downstream verdicts, so it is flagged rather than buried.
3. **`privacy_context_dependence/1` is required by contract and unused by the module,** and I
   am not certain the arity is right. It could plausibly be a 2-place relation between
   information and a context. I chose arity 1 partly because N8 warns that a borrowed relation
   of arity >= 2 has an argument order no gloss fixes and that a total inversion passes every
   deterministic check. If the providing node defines it as 2-place, this is a link mismatch
   and finding it is the point of recording the assumption in the gloss.
