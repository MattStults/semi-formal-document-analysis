## 1. QUESTION ZERO — SHOULD THIS CLAUSE HAVE BEEN TRANSLATED AT ALL?

**Answer: YES, it should have been translated, and as an `asserts` module — not an abstention
and not an ontology-only module.** I take each of `00_task.md`'s four abstention triggers in
turn against the narrowed span:

> "The assistant must not generate hateful content targeting protected groups (race, religion,
> gender, sexual orientation, disability, etc.). This includes slurs, insults, and any language
> that demeans or dehumanizes."

- **Is it a section heading?** No. It is two finite sentences with a subject and a modal verb,
  not a title. It sits at L1239 inside a section, not at its head.
- **Does it state a goal rather than a condition?** No. "must not generate X" is a directly
  testable prohibition on a specific act by a specific bearer. It is not "we aim to reduce
  hate" or "we are committed to…" — contrast P2, whose measured failures all have OpenAI or
  the document as the subject of the main verb. Here the subject of the matrix verb is **the
  assistant**, and the matrix verb is **must not generate**. N2's matrix-verb test also
  passes: there is no embedding verb ("we're exploring how to…") whose object is the rule;
  the rule *is* the main clause.
- **Is it an example?** No — and this is the trigger the dispatch warns was missed elsewhere,
  so I checked it hard. The span carries no `**Example**:` heading, no good/bad response pair,
  no dialog. The second sentence begins "This includes…", which is *extension of the
  prohibited class*, not an illustrative case of the assistant behaving well or badly. An
  "includes" list is normative scope; an example is a demonstration. They are not the same
  thing and only the latter triggers abstention.
- **Is the content inexpressible as rules?** No. It is one of the most straightforwardly
  expressible things in the whole document: one act (`generate`), one status (`forbid`), one
  class of content, one targeting condition.

There is no route on which abstaining here would have been right. Abstaining on a `must not`
that names its act would delete a hard prohibition from the corpus, which is the worst
available outcome on a clause of this kind. **Translated is correct.**

## 2. THE CENTRAL QUESTION — is the second sentence reachable through the single assert?

**Answer: YES. Every one of the four "includes" items reaches the single assert. Nothing has
been parked in `ontology` where no deontic rule fires on it.**

### The trace

The one assert is:

```
asserts(l1108_1367_n006, forbid, generate(C)) :-
    hateful_content(C), targets_group(C, G), protected_group(G).
```

`ontology` supplies four rules with the same head:

```
hateful_content(C) :- slur(C).
hateful_content(C) :- insult(C).
hateful_content(C) :- demeaning_language(C).
hateful_content(C) :- dehumanizing_language(C).
```

and two with the head `protected_group/1`:

```
protected_group(G) :- group_defined_by(G, A), protected_attribute(A).
protected_group(G) :- additional_protected_group(G).
```

with `protected_attribute(race). …(religion). …(gender). …(sexual_orientation). …(disability).`
as ground facts.

**Trace for "a slur that targets a protected group":** supply `slur(c)`,
`targets_group(c, g)`, `group_defined_by(g, race)`. Then `hateful_content(c)` derives from
rule 1; `protected_group(g)` derives from `group_defined_by(g,race)` and the ground
`protected_attribute(race)`; the assert body is satisfied and the program concludes
`asserts(l1108_1367_n006, forbid, generate(c))`. **Yes, the compiled program concludes that
generating it is forbidden.** The same trace runs identically from `insult/1`,
`demeaning_language/1` and `dehumanizing_language/1`. The eleven `ontology` entries are not a
parking lot: six of them are bodied rules feeding the assert's own body, and the other five
are the ground constants those bodies range over. Every ontology entry is load-bearing on the
single assert. The one-assert shape is correct here — the span states **one** prohibition and
then widens the *extension of its subject term*, and widening a term is exactly what
`ontology` is for. Splitting it into four asserts would state four prohibitions where the
document states one.

### Does the module require a slur to target a protected group before the prohibition fires — and does the span?

**The module: yes.** `targets_group(C,G), protected_group(G)` is in the assert body, so no
route reaches `forbid` without a targeting fact.

**The span: yes, on the only defensible reading.** The demonstrative "This" in "This includes
slurs…" has exactly one available antecedent: "hateful content targeting protected groups".
The second sentence enumerates *forms the prohibited content takes*, inside the scope the
first sentence set. It does not open a second, unconditioned prohibition. The alternative
reading — that the span forbids generating *any* insult whatsoever, targeted or not — would
have the Model Spec prohibit the assistant from ever writing an insult in a novel, a debate
exercise, or a quoted example, which the span plainly does not say, and encoding it would be
a scope *widening* invented by the translator. **The module's targeting condition is
faithful, and I decline to remove it.** See finding F1 for the residual issue this leaves.

### Is the parenthesised list encoded as EXHAUSTIVE, closing the span's "etc."?

**No — and this is the module's best single decision.** The five characteristics are ground
`protected_attribute/1` facts, but there is a *second, independent* route into
`protected_group/1`:

```
protected_group(G) :- additional_protected_group(G).
```

with `additional_protected_group/1` in `inputs` and glossed *"G is a group protected on some
characteristic beyond the five the clause names outright — the case supplies it, because the
clause's list ends in 'etc.' and is therefore not closed"*, licence `assumed`, with the
inference named. `protected_group/1` is *also* declared in `inputs`, giving a third route.
The "etc." is therefore carried into the compiled program rather than silently dropped. Claim
C2 states the openness explicitly. **The dangerous narrowing the dispatch asked about did not
happen.**

## 3. FINDINGS

### F1 — a slur's targeting must be separately supplied by the case (CONCLUSION-CHANGING in principle; **no edit — every available repair requires invention**)

`slur/1` is glossed *"C is a term of abuse applied to people for belonging to a group"* — i.e.
targeting is analytic in the concept. But the assert still demands an independent
`targets_group(C,G)` fact. A query that supplies `slur(c)` and nothing else derives nothing,
even though the span's second sentence says slurs are covered.

Why I did not edit:
1. Every repair I can construct invents a predicate the span does not supply. Deriving
   `targets_group(C,G)` from `slur(C)` leaves `G` unbound — the solver refuses the whole file
   (bad one #1 in the worked example). Binding it would need a coined `slur_for(C,G)` relation
   that appears nowhere in the narrowed span; that is failure mode #1, made-up things.
2. **The error direction is silence, not permission.** Because `closure` for `generate` is
   `unclear`, an under-supplied case yields *no verdict*, not a permission. Under a `cepa`
   closure this finding would be a live weakening and I would have forced a repair.
3. Removing the targeting condition instead — the "strengthening" move — would forbid every
   insult the assistant ever generates, which the span does not say. That is P5's *other*
   direction and is not an improvement.

Recorded as a class (C1 below) rather than repaired. This is partly a contract gap; see
PROMPT FINDINGS.

### F2 — `hateful_content/1` and `protected_group/1` are both derived in `ontology` and declared in `inputs` (COSMETIC — no edit; and removing them would be harmful)

Both predicates have `ontology` heads *and* appear in `inputs`. This is not a
`requires`/`inputs` collision (the only disjointness the contract states), and `validate.py`
reports 0 breaches with no note on either. It is load-bearing in the safe direction: without
`hateful_content/1` in `inputs`, the *first* sentence's general prohibition would be
unreachable, and the module would forbid only the four enumerated forms — a real narrowing of
the broader prohibition. The redundancy is a widening, not a narrowing. **Left alone.** The

### F3 — no body relies on the absence of a fact (COSMETIC — confirmed clean, no edit)

## 4. PROMPT FINDINGS

Two things I declined to fix because the contract, not the drafter, is responsible.

### PF1 — the contract never rules on a predicate that is both an `ontology` head and an `inputs` entry
`prompt/10_output_format.md`, section **"`requires` and `inputs` must be disjoint"**, states
exactly one disjointness rule, between `requires` and `inputs`. The adjacent starred
paragraph — *"Every predicate you reference must be declared. Anything appearing in a body
must be in your `ontology`, in `requires` (another clause defines it), or in `inputs`"* — is
an inclusive "or" and does not say whether a predicate the module itself derives may *also*
be declared as a situation input. `schema.py` does not check it and `validate.py` emits no
note for `hateful_content/1` or `protected_group/1` (contrast the `situation-input` notes,
which fire only on head-less names — so the tooling knows the difference and deliberately
says nothing). The construction in F2 is therefore **unadjudicated by the contract**, and two
translators will split on it. It is doing the right thing here, but that is the drafter's
judgment filling a hole the prompt left. **The contract should state whether an
`ontology`-derived predicate may be re-declared in `inputs`, and if so that it widens rather
than narrows.**

### PF2 — the contract offers no way to say "X is by its nature Y" without inventing a case-side relation
F1's residue. The span says slurs are covered; "slur" analytically entails targeting a group;
but the module's only expressible options are (a) demand a separate `targets_group/2` fact
(under-fires), (b) leave `G` unbound (`10_output_format.md`: *"An ontology entry with an
unbound variable and NO body… makes the solver reject the whole file"*, and worked-example bad
one #1), or (c) coin a relation absent from the span (failure mode #1). The
`concepts`/`ontology` split gives a place to *state* that targeting is analytic in `slur/1` —
the gloss does state it — but no way to make the solver *use* it. **The gloss carries content
the compiled program cannot reach, and nothing in the contract flags that gap.** This is a
prompt/schema limitation, not a defect in this module.
