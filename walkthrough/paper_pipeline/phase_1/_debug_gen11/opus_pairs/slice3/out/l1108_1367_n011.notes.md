## (a) Stage 0 — span enumeration

Narrowed span (the whole of it):

    #### Do not contribute to extremist agendas that promote violence {#avoid_extremist_content authority=root}

**Finite verbs in the narrowed text: 2.**
1. `Do not contribute` — the imperative of the heading (matrix).
2. `promote` — inside the relative clause *"agendas that promote violence"*.

(The attribute block `{#avoid_extremist_content authority=root}` contains no verb. It is a
markdown attribute assignment: it names the section anchor and stamps its authority level.)

**Propositions `ESTABLISHES` demands: 1, with two conjoined halves.**
*"The #avoid_extremist_content heading carries root authority, applying to all rules in that
section."* → (i) the heading carries root authority; (ii) that authority reaches every rule
under it.

**The mismatch, and how it is resolved.** The counts do not line up, and the direction matters:
the span carries MORE than `ESTABLISHES` asks (the imperative and its relative clause), while
`ESTABLISHES` asks for something the span states as an attribute rather than a verb (the
authority stamp, and its reach over the section's members). This is not a case of `ESTABLISHES`
demanding content the span cannot support — `authority=root` and `#avoid_extremist_content` are
both literal substrings of the narrowed text, so both halves are anchored. It is a case of the
node selecting ONE of the span's two contents. I encoded the authority stamp and deliberately
left the imperative to the section's member-rule nodes (see (b), second question). Both halves
of `ESTABLISHES` are encoded, as the two `ontology` entries; no third proposition was
manufactured to soak up the unencoded verb.

## (b) Stage 0b — the abstention question, answered in words

**I translated. I did not abstain.**

Both readings, stated:

(i) *It is a section heading, and `00_task.md` lists "it is a section heading" among the
abstention triggers, so abstain.* This reading takes the trigger to be about the passage's
TYPE.

(ii) *It is a fact about the DOCUMENT — this section carries root authority — which is
expressible as rules and which other nodes will `require`, so translate it as ontology.* This
reading takes the trigger to be about whether the node establishes anything the document says.

**I took (ii).** Three grounds:

1. The production prompt's own worked example decides it. `node_worked_example.md` §"A
   heading-authority node — a structural fact is translatable" is this exact case: node
   `l3995_4164_n001`, a heading whose only content is the authority its section carries,
   PROVIDES `guideline_authority`, translated and not abstained. It says in terms: *"a node
   stating a structural fact about the document — which authority a section carries … — goes to
   `ontology` with `asserts` empty; only a node that establishes neither is an abstention."*
   And bad example 4: *"It states no obligation' is not on its own grounds to abstain."* The
   worked example outranks my reading of the one-line trigger in `00_task.md`, and the prompt
   is the contract.
2. What decides the route is whether the node establishes something the document says, not what
   KIND of passage it is. This node establishes a stampable fact: the document literally writes
   `authority=root` on this section.
3. **The consequence of abstaining, stated as required.** `PROVIDES` names `root_authority` as
   a predicate this module is expected to make derivable, and other graph nodes will carry it in
   their `requires`. An abstention here leaves `root_authority` defined by no module in the link
   scope: every dependent node's body would then wait forever on a definition nothing supplies,
   its head would derive nothing while looking like it enforced something, and failure mode #15
   ("never fired" has three causes) would be indistinguishable between "dead rule" and "waiting
   on an unlinked clause". Abstaining strands them.

**Second question: does the heading's own imperative ("Do not contribute to extremist agendas
that promote violence") belong in this module as a norm?**

**No — it belongs to the section's member rules, and I did not encode it.** Reasons:

- `ESTABLISHES` is the one claim this module must express, and it is about the authority level,
  not the conduct. `PROVIDES` likewise names only `root_authority` — it does not ask for a
  prohibition predicate.
- A heading in this document is a title for the rules beneath it; the operative content of
  "do not contribute to extremist agendas" is stated by the member clauses of the section, which
  are other nodes. Encoding a `forbid contribute(...)` here would be this module folding another
  clause's content into itself — barred by `00_task.md` rule 1 ("you never fold another clause's
  content into this one") — and would very likely duplicate, at a different granularity and
  under a different name, a prohibition a member node states properly (failure mode #8/#9).
- The heading states the section's topic at maximal generality ("extremist agendas that promote
  violence" is nowhere defined in this span). Encoding it would require coining an opaque symbol
  named after the heading's own words and containing nothing — precisely failure mode #5 /
  `00_task.md` rule 5, the hollow stub that "survives a paraphrase check by construction".

So `asserts` is empty and `acts` is empty. Per `10_output_format.md`, a clause governing no acts
needs no `closure` entry, so `closure` is empty too. That is not an empty module: it carries two
`ontology` entries.

## (e) Unsettled, and where I suspect the prompt rather than myself

1. **The licence on `ontology[1]` (the propagation to member rules) is the one call I would
   most expect a reviewer to contest.** I marked it `textual` citing this node. The case for
   `textual`: the node's own `ESTABLISHES` says "applying to all rules in that section" in so
   many words, the citable id is the node, and the prompt's own heading worked example
   (`l3995_4164_n001`) marks the structurally identical rule `textual`. The case for `assumed`:
   the SOURCE TEXT alone only stamps the heading, and "an attribute on a heading governs the
   rules beneath it" is a fact about markdown-with-attribute-blocks conventions plus this
   document's layout, not something the line says. I followed the worked example, but if the
   project's rule is that licences answer to the SOURCE TEXT rather than to `ESTABLISHES`, this
   entry should be `assumed` with the inference "an authority attribute on a section heading
   governs the rules of that section". I did not want to depart from the prompt's own exemplar
   on my own authority. **This is the item to look at first.**

2. **The `00_task.md` abstention trigger "it is a section heading" is in direct tension with the
   heading-authority worked example, and I think that is a PROMPT defect rather than mine.**
   `00_task.md` lists being a section heading as sufficient grounds to abstain, full stop.
   `node_worked_example.md` then translates a section heading and says in bold that doing so is
   right. A translator reading only `00_task.md` abstains; one reading both translates. The two
   files can be reconciled (the trigger is about headings that establish nothing beyond their
   own imperative; a heading carrying an `authority=` attribute establishes a structural fact
   as well), but that reconciliation is nowhere written down, and this dispatch had to ask me
   for it explicitly — which is itself evidence the prompt does not settle it. Suggested fix:
   `00_task.md`'s trigger should read "it is a section heading that establishes nothing beyond
   the rules beneath it — but see the heading-authority example, where the heading carries an
   authority attribute".

3. **Arity of `root_authority` is a guess that other nodes must match.** I chose `/1`, ranging
   over an instruction or rule, because the PROVIDES gloss says "the authority level of
   INSTRUCTIONS in the … section". A consumer node that expects `root_authority/1` over
   *sections* rather than *rules* would link cleanly against `ontology[0]` and derive nothing
   from `ontology[1]` — failure mode #9 (same name, two meanings) in miniature. I mitigated it
   the only way available from inside one node: the concept gloss says what the argument is.
   Having BOTH the ground atom (heading as argument) and the bodied rule (rule as argument)
   means the predicate is, strictly, polymorphic in its argument — the heading is not itself a
   rule. I judged that acceptable because N1 explicitly blesses the document-fact ground atom in
   this predicate, by name, but a reviewer who dislikes it should drop `ontology[0]` rather than
   `ontology[1]`, since `ontology[1]` is the entry a real situation can match.
