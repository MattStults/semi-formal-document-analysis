**Three things I am least sure of.**

1. **`forbid` vs `prefer` on the BAD arm — the module's biggest call.** `ESTABLISHES` says "should
   decline", which is `prefer`-shaped, and the prompt's own worked-example node
   (`l4251_4571_n029`) translates a GOOD/BAD pair as `prefer`, warning that collapsing a comparative
   into `forbid` is a hollow stub. I went the other way for three reasons: the GOOD arm is an
   *inability* ("I can't"), not a stylistic choice; the `NEEDS` block tells me this section carries
   **root** authority, the strongest level, which is not the register of a preference; and under
   `PROVISIONAL.md` the span (which marks the reproduction BAD) governs over `ESTABLISHES`'s softer
   "should". If this is wrong it is wrong in the over-restrictive direction, which stays visible and
   citable — but I record it as the call most worth a second reader. It is also the one place a
   reviewer could push me to weaken the module, which is the flagged suspect direction, so I have
   stated my grounds rather than left it to be re-litigated in a transcript.
2. **Dropping "when asked" from the forbid body.** `ESTABLISHES` conditions the norm on a request;
   the span's header does not. I followed P5 and the header, leaving the prohibition unconditional
   on being asked. The competing reading — that this example only ever speaks to the asked-for case,
   and an unrequested reproduction is a different situation the span is silent about — is not
   unreasonable, and I could not settle it from the text. I chose the reading whose error direction
   is over-assertion rather than under-restriction.
3. **The `cepa` closure on `provide_full_lyrics`.** Reproducing a *public-domain* song is clearly
   left permitted, which is what I encoded. But `cepa` also commits the corpus to permitting acts of
   this class the span never considered — a *partial* reproduction of a non-public-domain song, say.
   `unclear` was the alternative and I rejected it because the span's qualifier ("not in the public
   domain") is doing real bounding work and does settle something, which is what a closure reason is
   supposed to draw on. I am not fully confident; a reader who thinks the partial-quotation case is
   what matters would pick `unclear`.
