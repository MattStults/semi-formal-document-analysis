# l1108_1367_n001 — translator notes

## (a) Stage-0 span enumeration

NARROWED text (the only licensing text; the rest of L1114 is context — P6):

> The assistant should not generate erotica, depictions of illegal or non-consensual sexual
> activities, or extreme gore, except in scientific, historical, news, artistic or other contexts
> where sensitive content is appropriate.

**THE TWO COUNTS**

* **Finite verbs in the narrowed text: 2.** (1) `should [not] generate` — the matrix verb, subject
  *the assistant*; (2) `is [appropriate]` — inside the relative clause *where sensitive content is
  appropriate*. The exception phrase *except in … contexts* is a prepositional adjunct and carries
  no finite verb of its own.
* **Propositions `ESTABLISHES` demands: 1 top-level claim**, which decomposes into 4 prohibition
  grounds + 1 exception. `ESTABLISHES` is **verbatim identical** to the narrowed span, so there is
  **no scope conflict** (N9): nothing is demanded that the text does not contain, and nothing in
  the text is dropped by `ESTABLISHES`.

**WHAT ATTACHES TO WHAT** (the dispatch asked for this explicitly).

Three prohibited kinds — but the second is itself a disjunction, so **four** grounds:

| # | ground | substring |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | erotica | "erotica" |
| 2 | depiction of **illegal** sexual activity | "depictions of illegal … sexual activities" |
| 3 | depiction of **non-consensual** sexual activity | "depictions of … non-consensual sexual activities" |
| 4 | extreme gore | "extreme gore" |

The exception clause, five context terms:

| # | term | encoded as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | scientific | `exception_context(X) :- scientific_context(X)` |
| 2 | historical | `exception_context(X) :- historical_context(X)` |
| 3 | news | `exception_context(X) :- news_context(X)` |
| 4 | artistic | `exception_context(X) :- artistic_context(X)` |
| 5 | other contexts where sensitive content is appropriate | `exception_context(X) :- sensitive_content_appropriate_in(X)` |

**The exception attaches to the prohibition as a whole**, not to any one of the four grounds: *"…
erotica, X, or extreme gore, **except** in …"* — the adjunct sits after the closed list and scopes
over the verb `generate`. So one exception condition appears in all four assert bodies, rather than
in one of them.

**Attachment of "where sensitive content is appropriate" — the one genuinely ambiguous point.**
Two parses exist:

* **(A) adjacent** — the relative clause modifies *other contexts* only; the four named contexts
  are exempt in their own right. This is the parse N4 prescribes ("a qualifier in a list bounds ONE
  item"), and it matches the document's evident intent that the four named contexts *are* the
  paradigm cases of appropriate contexts.
* **(B) single head noun** — the five adjectives all modify one noun *contexts*, and the relative
  clause modifies that whole NP, so every exempting context must ALSO be one in which sensitive
  content is appropriate.

**I took (A) and encoded it.** Grounds: N4 states the rule directly and warns that propagating a
qualifier "to be safe" invents an untraceable restriction. Recorded as unsettled point 1 in (e),
because (A) is the more permissive of the two and I want that visible rather than buried.

**Deliberately NOT encoded, and why:**

* *"This includes depictions in text, audio …, or visual content"* — the second sentence of L1114,
  **outside the node's narrowing**. Encoding a modality condition here would be P6 content sourced
  outside the narrowing.
* `system_authority/1` — a NEEDS name, recorded in `requires` with the gloss the node handed me
  (contract 2), but used in **no body**. The narrowed span says nothing about authority levels;
  wiring it into a rule would import the section-heading fact the narrowing excludes. P9's
  corrected form makes this explicitly correct, not a defect.
* `sensitive_content/1` — likewise a NEEDS name in `requires`, unused. It could not be *defined*
  here (contract 2 forbids a NEEDS name in `ontology`), and the span's exception is about a
  property of a **context**, not of the content, so my own `sensitive_content_appropriate_in/1`
  carries that. See unsettled point 2.

## (e) What I could not settle, and where I suspect the prompt

**Unsettled 1 — the attachment of "where sensitive content is appropriate" (the biggest one).**
Parses (A) and (B) in section (a) differ in a way that matters: under (A), which I encoded, a
scientific context exempts on its own; under (B), a scientific context exempts only if sensitive
content is also appropriate there. **(A) is the more permissive reading**, and I want that stated
plainly rather than discovered. I chose it because N4 prescribes adjacent attachment and because
the document evidently offers the four named contexts as its own examples of appropriate contexts —
not because any review entry pushed me toward the weaker prohibition. If the coordinator prefers
(B), the change is mechanical: add `sensitive_content_appropriate_in(X)` as a second literal to
each of the first four `exception_context` bodies. Nothing else in the module moves.

**Unsettled 2 — the NEEDS name `sensitive_content/1` is stranded, and I think correctly so.**
The node's gloss makes it a property of **content** ("Content such as erotica or gore that may only
be generated under specific circumstances"), while the span's exception is a property of a
**context** ("contexts where sensitive content is appropriate"). Those are different arities of
idea. I could have forced a use — e.g. gating the residual exception on `sensitive_content(C)` —
but that would narrow the exception on a guess about another node's extension, and contract 2
forbids me from defining the name myself. So it sits in `requires`, unused, with the node's own
gloss, and the link is left inspectable. If the graph intended `sensitive_content` to carry the
context sense, my `sensitive_content_appropriate_in/1` is the duplicate that should be collapsed
into it at link time — that collapse is exactly the disagreement the D4b-level-2 comment says the
description channel exists to surface.

**Unsettled 3 — "should" vs "must" is lost, and no field can hold it.** Recorded under P7 above.
The vocabulary has four statuses and no strength dimension, so this module's `forbid` is
indistinguishable from one translating a "must never". This is a limitation of the fixed
vocabulary, not a choice I made.

**Suspected PROMPT defect (not mine): `_debug_gen11/opus_pairs/slice3/validate.py` is broken and
cannot report a clean run.** Two independent bugs, both in the harness rather than in any module:

1. line 43 — `errs = schema.validate_all(...)` treats the return as a list of errors, but
   `validate_all` returns the **tuple** `(Module | None, list[Breach])`. Every module therefore
   reports exactly *"2 error(s)"*: the first "error" printed is the whole repr of the successfully
   built `Module`, and the second is the literal empty breach list `[]`. **A module with zero
   schema breaches is reported as two errors, and one with real breaches reports two as well** —
   the count carries no information either way, and `rc` is set to 1 unconditionally.
2. line 38 — `for n in notes:` iterates the result of `checks.run_checks`, which returns a
   `CheckResult` dataclass, not a list. It raises `TypeError: 'CheckResult' object is not
   iterable` **before any check note is printed**, so the script never reaches its counts line.

Both are one-line fixes (`mod, errs = schema.validate_all(...)`; `notes = res.findings`). I did not
touch `validate.py` — it is shared by every agent in this slice and a mid-run edit would change
what other dispatches see. I ran the corrected logic out of my scratchpad instead, against the same
`schema.validate_all` and `checks.run_checks` on the same corpus config. **Result: module built,
0 schema breaches, 22 check notes, all of them known non-defects** — 2 × `requires-unprovided`
(both NEEDS names, ANTI-RULE 2) and 20 × head-less-input notes, which are two notes apiece for the
ten query-time `inputs` and are what a correctly declared situation input looks like.
