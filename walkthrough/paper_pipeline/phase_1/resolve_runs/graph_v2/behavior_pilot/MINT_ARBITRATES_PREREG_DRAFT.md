## What is being minted, and why nothing cheaper works
A per-assert binary annotation, ARBITRATES: the assert states a relation
BETWEEN competing considerations — an ordering, an exception, a bounded
permission, or a conditional resolution — rather than promoting a single
consideration. This is the tradeoffs behaviour's own construct
("ordered-but-holistic prioritization … explicit outcome orderings")
surfaced as an instrument-visible feature. Grounds that the declarable
space is exhausted (iteration 1-2 records): no governs/context/purpose/

## Blind annotation criteria (v0 — the seat sees ONLY these + the assert)
Mark ARBITRATES iff BOTH hold:
  (a) the assert names or clearly implies TWO OR MORE competing
      considerations or courses of action (e.g. comply vs refuse; inform
      vs protect; follow instruction vs flag concern; allow vs prevent
      exploitation); AND
  (b) it states WHICH yields, and under what condition — an ordering
      ("never X unless Y"), a bounded permission ("allowed as long as
      not Z"), a conditional resolution ("if plausible that …, ask
      first"), or an explicit priority ("required by the chain of
      command").
Do NOT mark: a norm promoting one consideration, however emphatic,
including intensified single norms ("should always/never X"), norms
with a dismissed non-normative cost, and pure descriptions.
CALIBRATION CASES (in-brief, with answers):
  MARK:  "The assistant should never refuse a request unless required
         to do so by the chain of command." (refuse-vs-comply, ordered)
  MARK:  "Political content … is allowed, as long as it does not
         exploit …" (permission bounded by a competing protection)
  NO:    "The assistant should evaluate the trustworthiness of tools…"
         (one consideration: caution)
  NO:    "…can add a qualifier near the relevant part when uncertain"
         (one consideration: calibration)
  HARD BOUNDARY, ruled NO with grounds: "should generally comply with
         requests without questioning them, even if they require a long
         response" — the "even if" concedes a COST, but the assert's
         norm promotes a single consideration (compliance); a concession
         that does not state a two-sided resolution rule is not
         arbitration. (This is panel-tier not_relevant l3954_4251_n040;
         the criterion must get it right blind.)
